# Maintaining Shared Workflows

Shared Workflows serves every repository that calls the reusable workflows of `CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows` at the tag `v1`. It offers its shared parts as the entries of the [catalogue](../catalogue.json). Each shared part has one or more shared steps, and each shared step has its part folder, a subfolder of `actions/` with a composite action `$/actions/<folder>`. A shared workflow loads each part folder it uses through that action, in the form that [Same-repository references](#same-repository-references) states. The action of the recording step and of the Python language step runs commands; the action of every other part folder is resource-only. A consumer's job calls the recording step and the Python language step at `v1`, and the pipeline runs the Python language step in its `python-verify` job and in shard 1 of `suite-jobs.yml`. The shared parts share the [v1 channel](#the-v1-channel), the [pins file](#pins) and the promotion of `v1`.

An agent in a consumer starts from the catalogue, read at `v1`: the decision rule for CI work, the requests, the local workflow register, and one entry per shared part with its purpose, its configuration, its shared workflows and shared steps, the checks a consumer requires, what it needs from a repository, when it applies and its consumer kit, as [The catalogue](#the-catalogue) says. A shared part's consumer kit holds its caller templates and their explanation for a consumer. This document is for changing the shared parts themselves: what the workflows do and how they are maintained.

## Principles

Every change to Shared Workflows holds to the CI goal and principles in [`docs/agents/ci.md`](agents/ci.md), and to two of its own:

- What is meaningful for repositories in general belongs in the shared workflows, and nothing a caller binds to changes unnoticed.
- Documentation follows what is built; it never decides the design.

## What the workflows do

### Calling the workflows

A consumer calls a shared workflow from a fixed caller under its own `.github/workflows/`, at `CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/.github/workflows/<workflow>.yml@v1`. The caller sets `permissions: {}` at the top and its rights on its job, passes no secrets (except `automation.yml`, which passes `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_PRIVATE_KEY` by name) and runs no step of its own. A required check for a job in a reusable workflow is named `<caller job> / <shared job>` and matches by that exact string. The fixed caller `ci.yml` gives every consumer the one required check `ci / verdict` in its default-branch ruleset.

This repository calls its own shared pipeline, automation and release workflows by local path, with the inputs that a consumer passes through `v1`: `ci.yml` calls `pipeline.yml` as its job `ci` (whose verdict check is `ci / verdict`, the one required check; `ci / plan`, `ci / repository-hygiene`, the fixture parts and the suite jobs `ci / suite-selection-jobs / shard <number>` and `ci / suite-selection-jobs / windows` are not required on their own), `automation-caller.yml` calls `automation.yml` as its job `automation` (whose checks `automation / <job>` are not required), and `release-caller.yml` calls `release.yml`. [Job layout](#job-layout) gives the pipeline's jobs.

The tag `v1` moves only as [The v1 channel](#the-v1-channel) says. Callers keep `@v1` and ignore `CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows` in Dependabot.

### Same-repository references

A `uses:` value that starts with `$/` is GitHub's self-repository form: it resolves to the running workflow's own repository at the commit that is running, without a checkout, wherever `./` works, reusable workflow calls included. It needs runner 2.336.0 or newer ([GitHub changelog, 2026-07-30](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-30-reference-same-repository-actions-with-self-repository-syntax/)). Every same-repository `uses:` of Shared Workflows takes `$/`, and none takes `./`:

| Place | Form |
| --- | --- |
| The part folders that the shared workflows load | `$/actions/<folder>` |
| The shared workflows that this repository's callers, `pipeline.yml` and `automation.yml` call | `$/.github/workflows/<workflow>.yml` |
| The validation workflow that the validation caller template calls | `$/.github/workflows/<validation-workflow>.yml` |

zizmor's `self-repository` audit reports `uses: ./...` and recommends `$/`, so no workflow here carries a `# zizmor: ignore[self-repository]` comment. actionlint 1.7.12, the pinned version, does not know `$/` and reports such a reference as malformed ([rhysd/actionlint#711](https://github.com/rhysd/actionlint/issues/711)). The shared hygiene check therefore ignores exactly two actionlint diagnostics for `$/`, passed as `-ignore` flags so that they apply in every consumer without an actionlint configuration file: `ACTIONLINT_IGNORE` for an action and `ACTIONLINT_WORKFLOW_IGNORE` for a reusable workflow call, both in [hygiene.py](../actions/repository-hygiene/hygiene.py). A consumer's own `.github/actionlint.yml` or `.yaml` stays in use. The exception falls away once the pinned actionlint supports `$/`: a separate change after that pin update removes both patterns, since the pins updater's pull request changes only `pins.json`. The same check ignores one more diagnostic, `ACTIONLINT_LABEL_IGNORE` in [hygiene.py](../actions/repository-hygiene/hygiene.py): exactly `label "ubuntu-26.04" is unknown`, because actionlint 1.7.12 does not know the runner label `ubuntu-26.04` that GitHub runs ([rhysd/actionlint#682](https://github.com/rhysd/actionlint/issues/682)). That exception falls away once the pinned actionlint knows the label: a separate change after that pin update removes the pattern.

### Job layout

The suites run in two places that share one shared workflow, `suite-jobs.yml`: the pipeline's part `suite-selection`, and the full recording of the automation workflow. The pipeline's jobs of the part are:

| Job | Runner | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `plan` | `ubuntu-26.04` | Checks out the repository once, runs the dispatch check, restores the default-branch record, runs the [push lookup](#push-lookup-and-reuse) on pushes and `dependabot-merged` runs, takes the shared CI digests and runs the shared lookup, then runs hygiene change detection, the configuration check and suite selection when the part `suite-selection` is configured, uploads the `tested-state` artifact on non-edited pull requests, and runs the light hygiene checks (branch and reference checks, the unified consumer checks on non-Dependabot, non-edited pull requests, Dependabot coverage and Markdown links; see [Repository hygiene in the pipeline](#repository-hygiene-in-the-pipeline)). Outputs `plan` (with per-part and per-unit decisions and fallback counts), `commit`, `tree`, the push lookup, hygiene change flags, and the suite selection outputs (`shards`, `windows`, `jobs`, `language-steps`, `units`, `reused`, `reused-units`, `selection`, `shared-digests`, `identical`). |
| `suite-selection-jobs` | the suite list's `jobs` | Needs `plan` and calls `suite-jobs.yml` when the selection in `plan` has shards or a Windows job. Its checks are `ci / suite-selection-jobs / shard <number>` and `ci / suite-selection-jobs / windows`. |
| `suite-selection-results` | `ubuntu-slim` | Needs `plan` and `suite-selection-jobs`. On a pull request whose `plan` wrote a plan output with selected or reused units, stores the passed results in the pull request's cache, for the reuse of [Push lookup and reuse](#push-lookup-and-reuse). |
| `verdict` | `ubuntu-slim` | Needs `plan`, the part jobs, `suite-selection-jobs` and `suite-selection-results`, under `!cancelled()`. `ci / verdict` fails when `plan` failed or gave no valid plan output, or when a planned check failed, ended skipped, timed out or never started; an unplanned check counts as not run, and a cancelled run gives no verdict, as the [Principles](agents/ci.md#principles) of the CI say. After a passing default-branch verdict, writes and caches the default-branch record (`default-branch-record-<run id>-<attempt>`) in the default branch's cache scope. |

`suite-jobs.yml` has two jobs. Each runs the same steps as its suite's entry files, and no repository's setup stands in it: a setup is a script that the suite list states, or a pinned tool that the configuration file names ([Pinned tools](#pinned-tools)).

| Job | Runner | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `linux` | the suite list's `jobs.linux.runner` | One job per item of `shards`, named `shard <number>`, with `timeout-minutes` from the plan (or default 20 in full recording). When the shard has entry files, it runs in order: the checkout, the recording step, the tools of its setups by pinned actions, the pinned Gitleaks when its `pinned` names it, outside the recording, `setups --phase before`, `start`, `setups --phase recorded`, then its entry files two at a time, each as its own traced process under `exec --entry` with its per-entry `--timeout`, then `check`, all with `PYTHONPYCACHEPREFIX` outside the checkout. An empty shard (selected only for a language step) skips setups, recording and passed-list steps. Shard 1 (chosen as the least-loaded measured shard when a language step runs) then runs the [Python language step](#python-language-step) outside the recording, when the caller names `python` among its language steps, and the passed list. |
| `windows` | the suite list's `jobs.windows.runner` | One job without shards, only when the `windows` input is not empty, with `timeout-minutes` from the plan (or default 30 in full recording). It runs the same steps for the selected Windows entry files under ETW, one after another, each with its per-entry `--timeout` and `PYTHONPYCACHEPREFIX` outside the checkout, and ensures `passed.json` exists before upload even after a setup failure. |

With `full: true`, the two jobs are the full recording's: `full-recording-jobs` in `automation.yml` calls `suite-jobs.yml` with `full: true`, which starts the recording with `--full --job`, names its artifacts `full-recording-shard-<number>` and `full-recording-windows`, and runs neither the Python language step nor the passed list. The runners, the fetch depth and the setups come from the suite list, not from the workflow.

The recording step is the first step after the checkout, so that it writes the job's environment before any setup. A setup that states a `tool` (`python`, `node` or `dotnet`) gets that tool from its pinned action before `start`: `actions/setup-python` runs with `update-environment: false`, and its interpreter reaches the `python` setup's `python=` line, which installs the [lookup report](#module-lookups); `actions/setup-node` and `actions/setup-dotnet` install the tool that the version or the version file of the suite list names, and `actions/setup-dotnet` installs into `DOTNET_INSTALL_DIR`. A tool that a pinned action installs can still change the job's environment; no entry file sees that change. Its repository reads are extra reads of the suites and entry files that state the setup. The other setups run as scripts: a setup with `recorded: false` runs in `setups --phase before`, outside the recording, and every other setup runs under the recording in `setups --phase recorded`, each in its own `$RUNNER_TEMP/setups/<setup>` directory. The pinned tools of the configuration file follow the same order, as [Pinned tools](#pinned-tools) states.

A setup states what it provides by appending lines to `$SETUP_PROVIDES`: `path=` for a directory, `variable=` for an environment variable, `python=` for the interpreter that receives the lookup report, and `write=` for an absolute writable directory that the setup grants. `setups --phase recorded` provides what every setup of the job states, in the suite list's order. A variable of the step that runs the entry files does not reach them. A variable that an entry file needs and no setup provides reaches it by its source: a value from the contexts that a job's `env` allows, `github`, `needs`, `strategy`, `matrix`, `vars`, `secrets` and `inputs`, goes in the job's `env`; a value from the `runner` context, which a job's `env` does not allow, reaches the entry file under its own name through its default environment variable, such as `RUNNER_TEMP` for `runner.temp`, which the job's environment already holds; and a value from the `runner` context under another name reaches it only through a setup's `provide --variable`. What a test writes outside its own temporary, bytecode-cache and lookup directories and what its setups provide through `write=`, such as `$HOME`, the shared checkout or per-user state on Windows, is no input of selection; a result that depends on such a write is a flaky result, and `check` reports such a write as a departure while the test still runs.

The recording step runs on the job's own `python3`, on Windows `python`. It runs `strace`, on Windows `dotnet`, as the job's environment before its setups finds them. A recording job restores no cache that holds build or test results and turns off build servers and node reuse: the ETW consumer's build in the `windows` job runs `dotnet publish` with `--disable-build-servers`, `-nodeReuse:false` and `-p:UseSharedCompilation=false`.

What each event runs in the pipeline of `ci.yml`:

| Event | Jobs |
| --- | --- |
| A pull request | `plan` (which runs selection and light hygiene checks), then the planned part jobs, selected shards and the Windows job side by side, then `verdict`. A later head reuses the passed results whose key did not change. |
| An `edited` pull request | `plan` and `repository-hygiene` run only the title and reference checks; no code check reruns and no code failure is cleared. |
| A push to `main` whose tree equals the tested tree | `plan` and Gitleaks in `repository-hygiene`, then `verdict`; no suite or other tree-based check runs again whose shared digests equal those the pull request was tested under. |
| A push whose pull request was behind | when the landing base has passed, the suites and hygiene checks whose reads meet both the pull request's changed files and the files changed on `main` between the tested base and the landing base; when the landing base has not passed, everything the newer base can affect. |
| A push without a passed tested tree | the suites and checks that its own changed files select. |
| A `dependabot-merged` dispatch | as a push of `client_payload.sha`. |

The selection of the pipeline's job `plan` runs with `--compare direct` between `github.event.before` and `github.sha` on any other event.

On `pull_request` events, a run of `CI` is cancelled when the workflow starts for a newer head of the same pull request; the hygiene job lets an `edited` run wait instead, as [Repository hygiene](#repository-hygiene) says. Push and dispatch runs are never cancelled, and a cancelled run stores no result.

### The `dependabot-merged` event

The fixed caller `ci.yml` and this repository's own `ci.yml` also run on a `repository_dispatch` event of type `dependabot-merged`, whose `client_payload.sha` names a merged commit on the default branch; `pipeline.yml` handles it for its callers. On that event every checkout of `pipeline.yml` takes `client_payload.sha`, and each comparison runs from that commit's first parent to the commit, as a push run compares the previous head with the pushed commit; it selects the same suites and checks that a push of that commit would. Before any lookup, selection or check, the step after the checkout of `pipeline.yml`'s `plan` job runs `suite_selection.py check-dispatch`, which fails the job unless `client_payload.sha` is a full 40-character commit ID in lowercase hexadecimal, names a commit in the checkout, and is reachable from the fetched default branch, `origin/<default branch>`; its message names the payload value and the reason. A failed dispatch check fails `plan`, so no part job (including `repository-hygiene` and `suite-selection-jobs`) runs and the verdict fails. A commit on the default branch below its tip passes. The check runs on every `repository_dispatch` event, so a dispatch with an empty `client_payload.sha` fails it. A `repository_dispatch` run runs the workflow from the default branch. The job `dependabot-auto-merge` of the [automation workflow](#the-automation-workflow) sends the event after each merge it makes. `tests/test_ci_policy_pipeline.py` fails when a checkout or the dispatch check is removed, and `tests/test_ci_policy_fixed_callers.py` when a version of the caller does not start on the event or starts on another dispatch type.

### Publication checks

The pipeline's hygiene checks (`plan` and `repository-hygiene` in `pipeline.yml`) run the convention checks on pull requests only, including `edited` events, with the scripts of `publication-checks/`; with Gitleaks, which runs on every non-edited event, they are the floor of [repository hygiene](#repository-hygiene). No separate publication-checks workflow or caller exists. It checks the branch name, every non-merge commit in the base-to-head range and the pull request title against these rules, and the commit messages, the title and the body for references to repositories that are not public, from the same revision as the workflow:

- Branch names follow a subset of Conventional Branch: short prefixes, dots only in release versions, no agent prefixes, and the `research/` exception. GitHub's revert branches `revert-<number>-<branch>` are accepted. The vendored official regex of [spec.json](../actions/publication-checks/spec.json) is an additional check.
- Commit subjects and pull request titles follow Conventional Commits as [commitlint.config.cjs](../actions/publication-checks/commitlint.config.cjs) states; the title step turns on `header-no-issue-number`, the rule for titles only. [check-commits.cjs](../actions/publication-checks/check-commits.cjs) reads the range in one `git log` call, skips merge commits by parent count (a merge-looking message on an ordinary commit grants no exception), lints every non-merge commit message in one Node.js process via `@commitlint/load`, `@commitlint/lint` and `@commitlint/format` loaded from `COMMITLINT_CLI`, and fails with a diagnostic on stderr when the linter cannot start or load. GitHub's revert subjects such as `Revert "feat: add a module"` are accepted.
- In a public repository, a reference `<owner>/<name>`, where `<owner>` is the owner of the repository the job runs in, names a public repository of that owner. In a repository whose event states `repository.private` as `true`, the check prints that it is skipped and passes, because the texts of a private repository are not public; without that statement it runs. [check-references.cjs](../actions/publication-checks/check-references.cjs) reads every commit message in the base-to-head range, merge commits included, from Git, and the pull request's title and body as they are now from `GET /repos/<owner>/<name>/pulls/<number>` with the job's token, so that a run started again after a correction checks the corrected text and no step environment shows the texts in the log. A pull request that cannot be read fails the check with that reason and without its texts; running the job again reads it again. The owner's name matches without regard to case and not as the end of a longer name; the repository name ends at the first character other than a letter, a digit, `.`, `_` or `-`, and a reference also passes when its name without closing full stops and `.git` is public. A reference to another owner's repository is not read. The script takes the public names from `GET /users/<owner>/repos` at `GITHUB_API_URL`, page by page, with the job's token like the read of the pull request, so GitHub limits the requests per repository and not per runner address; without `GITHUB_TOKEN` it reads the list without credentials. Only an entry whose `private` is `false` and whose `visibility` is `public` or absent counts as public, so a login never widens the list; no file holds a list of names. It requests the list only when a text holds a reference. A reference outside the list fails the check with its text, line and column, such as `pull request body, line 3, column 24`, and without the reference itself. A page answered with `403` or `429` is requested again at most twice: after `retry-after` seconds, otherwise at `x-ratelimit-reset` when `x-ratelimit-remaining` is `0`, otherwise after a short backoff; a wait of more than 60 seconds is not waited for. A list that cannot be read, as after a rate limit that outlasts the retries, a failed request or an unexpected answer, fails the check with that reason: the status, GitHub's message and the rate-limit headers that the answer carried, without the token or the checked texts; running the job again reads it again.
- A pull request authored by exactly `dependabot[bot]` or `renovate[bot]` skips all four checks with a visible message per check, also on events that a human triggers. Authorship is the deciding identity when a human edits or pushes to that pull request.

The job sets up the pinned Node.js and installs the locked commitlint toolchain from the part's `package.json` and `package-lock.json` in runner temporary storage with `npm ci --ignore-scripts`; a consumer's `.npmrc` does not configure that installation. Its Node.js version comes from the pins file through `$/actions/repository-hygiene`. The specification is read from [spec.json](../actions/publication-checks/spec.json) and [spec-metadata.json](../actions/publication-checks/spec-metadata.json), which records its version, SHA-256 and versioned source; no run requests it. `check-branch.cjs` loads [verify-spec.cjs](../actions/publication-checks/verify-spec.cjs) and checks the specification with it before any branch name: the SHA-256 of `spec.json` must equal the recorded one, its version the recorded version, and its regex must compile; otherwise the branch check fails. The specification is CC-BY-4.0; its attribution and source stay in the JSON and the metadata.

### Repository hygiene

Repository hygiene runs as part of `pipeline.yml` (light checks in `plan`, tool-installing checks in `repository-hygiene` on `ubuntu-26.04`, as [Repository hygiene in the pipeline](#repository-hygiene-in-the-pipeline) describes), reported in `ci / verdict`. It runs the floor, the [convention checks](#publication-checks) on pull requests and Gitleaks on every non-edited event, and the selected shared checks below. It holds read-only permissions, checks out the full history without persisted credentials and uses no secrets.

On a push and on a `dependabot-merged` run, `plan` restores the default-branch record and runs `suite_selection.py lookup --default-record`; on a `dependabot-merged` run, the [dispatch check](#the-dependabot-merged-event) runs before it, and unless that check passes, neither the tool installation nor any tool check runs. A missing or unreadable push lookup selects every check instead of none. An `identical` case runs no check but Gitleaks (unless a hygiene digest moved). A `behind` case whose landing base passed runs only the checks that both the landing base to the pushed commit and the tested base to the landing base select, or every check when either side cannot be read; when the landing base has not passed (or the default-branch record is missing or unreadable), it runs every check that the newer base can affect. The other cases decide over the landing base to the pushed commit.

The step `changes` in `plan` reads the changed files of the event, between the previous head and the pushed commit, between the merge base and the head of a pull request, or between the first parent of a [`dependabot-merged`](#the-dependabot-merged-event) commit and that commit, and decides which checks the change can affect. The Dependabot coverage check and the link check need no tool and run in `plan`. Each tool-installing check in `repository-hygiene` then installs its own pinned tools into the shared cached directory, so a download or checksum failure for one tool stops only that check while every other running check still runs and reports; the job fails when any install or check failed:

| Check | What runs |
| --- | --- |
| actionlint | `actionlint -shellcheck <pinned ShellCheck>`, with the two ignored diagnostics for `$/` and the one for the label `ubuntu-26.04` that [Same-repository references](#same-repository-references) states. A missing or broken ShellCheck fails the job instead of running actionlint without it. |
| zizmor | Offline, over workflows and action definitions, never Dependabot configuration. The repository's own `.github/zizmor.yml` or `zizmor.yml` (either with a `.yaml` extension) applies when present; otherwise the shared [zizmor policy](#zizmor-policy). |
| Gitleaks | `gitleaks git --log-opts=<start>..<head>` over the commits the run adds to the ref (`merge-base..head` on a pull request, `landing_base..head` on a push or `dependabot-merged` dispatch, an `identical` tree included), with the caller's configuration and baseline when given; falls back to `--log-opts=--all` over all reachable history when the range cannot be determined, when the Gitleaks configuration, the baseline or the in-repository Gitleaks pin (`tools.gitleaks.version` in `pins.json`) changes in the diff, or when the default-branch scan marker is missing, unreadable or differs from the current pinned version, configuration or baseline. The marker (`{"version", "config", "config_sha256", "baseline", "baseline_sha256"}`) is restored from the default-branch cache prefix `gitleaks-marker-` (`key: gitleaks-marker-lookup-<run_id>`, `restore-keys: gitleaks-marker-`) to `$RUNNER_TEMP/gitleaks-marker/marker.json`; only a default-branch run (`repository_dispatch`, or `push` to the default branch) passes `--record-marker` and saves `gitleaks-marker-<run_id>-<run_attempt>` after a passing full-history scan, while pull-request runs only restore it (ADR 0006). |
| Dependabot coverage | Every tracked package file that [package-ecosystems.json](../actions/repository-hygiene/package-ecosystems.json) recognises needs an `updates` entry for its ecosystem whose `directory` equals, or whose `directories` glob matches, the file's directory. A `.csproj`, `.fsproj` or `.vbproj` is also covered when a tracked `.sln` or `.slnx` directly in a directory that a `nuget` entry covers lists it, as Dependabot's NuGet discovery expands such a solution: a `.sln` by its `Project` lines without solution folders, a `.slnx` by the `Path` of every `Project` element, each path resolved against the solution's directory, with backslashes as separators and without regard to case. A solution in a subdirectory of a covered directory or in an uncovered directory, and a `ProjectReference`, cover nothing; `packages.config` and `Directory.Packages.props` keep the directory rule. A solution in a covered directory that cannot be read fails the job with its path; a solution under `coverage-exclude` is not read and covers nothing. Each uncovered file fails the job with its path and ecosystem. A repository without Dependabot configuration passes only when it has no recognised package file. |
| Relative links | [link_check.py](../actions/repository-hygiene/link_check.py): every relative link in tracked Markdown, inline links, images and reference definitions outside fenced code and inline code, points to an existing file or folder and, with an anchor into a Markdown file, to an existing heading in GitHub's anchor form or an HTML anchor. Links with a scheme and protocol-relative links are not checked. A link text followed by spaces, tabs or one line break before its parenthesis is no link under CommonMark and renders as plain text; unless its text matches a reference definition, is a task list marker, or the parenthesis starts another block, it is a split link, reported whatever its target. Each dead or split link fails the job with its file, line and target, as an annotation and in the step summary, which counts both; a split link is repaired by joining the link text and the parenthesis, `[text](target)`. `link-exclude` takes Markdown files out as sources; a link into them is still followed. |
| REUSE | `reuse --root . lint` with the pinned reuse. Each tracked file without a copyright and licence assignment fails the job, and the report of `reuse lint` names it. |

When each check runs:

| Check | Runs when the change touches |
| --- | --- |
| Gitleaks | anything: runs on every event over the commits added to the ref (an `identical` tree included), and scans the whole history when the configuration, the baseline, the pinned Gitleaks version or the default-branch scan marker changes |
| actionlint | a workflow, the metadata of a local action that a workflow uses as `uses: ./…`, the actionlint configuration, the scripts and pins that install and run it, or in the pipeline a moved `hygiene:actionlint` digest |
| zizmor | a workflow, an action definition, the zizmor configuration, the scripts, pins and shared policy that install and run it, or in the pipeline a moved `hygiene:zizmor` digest (and `hygiene:zizmor-policy` when the repository has no zizmor configuration of its own) |
| Dependabot coverage | a recognised package file, a `.sln` or `.slnx`, or the Dependabot configuration, the setting `parts.repository-hygiene.coverage-exclude` of the configuration file or a moved `hygiene:packages` digest, the `uses:` reference of the caller, or its scripts |
| Relative links | a Markdown file, a file that the change removes (a rename removes the old path), the setting `parts.repository-hygiene.link-exclude` of the configuration file or a moved `hygiene:markdown` digest, the `uses:` reference of the caller, or its scripts |
| REUSE | only in a repository with a `REUSE.toml`: an added, renamed or removed file, any `REUSE.toml`, a file under `LICENSES/`, a `.license` file, an SPDX or REUSE tag change in an existing file, a moved `hygiene:reuse` digest, or the scripts and pins that install and run it |

actionlint loads a local action's metadata only for a `uses: ./…` reference. It does not load an action referenced with `$/`, so a changed local `action.yml` changes nothing that actionlint checks; zizmor still runs on it. A workflow change that touches only ordinary comments starts neither actionlint nor zizmor; a changed linter directive counts as a change. A push, the first push of a branch included, and a `dependabot-merged` event decide over the landing base of the push lookup, which is the commit's first parent when the push has no previous head. Every check runs when the changed files cannot be determined, or when the change touches one of the three selected parts of the part's resource action: `#step:resources`, `#key:outputs.path.value` and `#key:runs.using` of `action.yml`. The pipeline names the two settings of the configuration file as `PATH#value:DOTTED.PATH`, which compares the value at that path as the reader of the configuration file reads it: a value that neither revision states is unchanged, so a changed configuration file without the setting starts nothing, and a revision that the reader cannot read counts as a change. When the file of a `step:` or `key:` selector differs between the two revisions and the selector names nothing in either, the step fails with the option name; a `PATH#INPUT` selector that names nothing passes. A `step:` selector on a caller without steps of its own reads the caller's `uses:` reference of the shared workflow instead, when either revision has such a reference; without one, the step fails as above. The step summary gives each decision and its reason; `workflows` remains as the union of the actionlint and zizmor decisions.

[hygiene.py](../actions/repository-hygiene/hygiene.py) runs each check as a subcommand and the decision as its `changes` subcommand. The [pipeline's consumer kit](../actions/pipeline/consumer-kit/README.md) explains to a consumer the two settings `coverage-exclude` and `link-exclude`, the merge settings and the default-branch ruleset that the shared parts rely on, with a link to the [ruleset template](../actions/repository-ruleset/README.md), and how a consumer fixes a check that a move of `v1` adds. A change to the job's checks, settings, reads or ruleset template changes that explanation in the same change; `tests/test_ci_policy_shared_surface.py` fails when the pipeline consumer kit no longer leads to the ruleset template.

### Dependabot auto-merge

Dependabot auto-merge runs as the job `dependabot-auto-merge` of [the automation workflow](#the-automation-workflow) (`automation.yml`), uses no third-party action, checks out nothing and runs no code from the pull request. The [consumer kit](../actions/dependabot-auto-merge/consumer-kit/README.md) of Dependabot auto-merge explains to a consumer how it switches the part on in `.github/ci-config.yml`, which updates merge on their own, the repository settings the part needs and when a repository adopts it. It holds no template: the fixed caller `automation.yml` reaches the part, and `.github/dependabot.yml` is generated from `dependabot` in `.github/ci-config.yml`. A change to the rules, the events, the rights of the job or the settings below changes that explanation in the same change.

The job `dependabot-auto-merge` in `automation.yml` runs on a `workflow_run` event of a `pull_request` run whose actor is exactly `dependabot[bot]`, or exactly the updater App `crankyant-publication-checks[bot]` ([Replace the App](#replace-the-app)) on a head branch that starts with `dependabot/`, judged by `github.event.workflow_run.actor.login` and `github.event.workflow_run.head_branch`, with `actions: read`, `contents: write` and `pull-requests: write` using the run's `GITHUB_TOKEN` and nothing else (`actions: write` is never granted). Every other event, author or actor skips the job by its job-level condition, without a runner; the job never judges by `github.actor`.

[auto_merge.py](../actions/dependabot-auto-merge/auto_merge.py) reads the pull request, its commits and the active rules of the base branch through `gh api`, and reads the update metadata from the pull request's first commit as `dependabot/fetch-metadata` v3.1.0 does:

- The pull request and its first commit are authored by `dependabot[bot]`, and that commit's signature is verified.
- The commit message holds the `updated-dependencies` block between a `---` line and a `...` line, and the head branch starts with `dependabot`. The block is a list of entries of `key: value` pairs, each with a non-empty `dependency-name`; a value in single or double quotes is read without them, and any other line in the block is an error.
- Each entry gives a dependency name and its `update-type`; the ecosystem is the second segment of the head branch, split at the character after `dependabot`. An entry without `update-type` takes the type derived from its previous and new version: a different major version is a major update, a different minor version or a version with fewer than two parts a minor update, any other difference a patch update, and a missing or unchanged version an empty type. The new version is the entry's `dependency-version`; otherwise both versions come from the message's ``Updates `<name>` from <old> to <new>`` line for that entry, and for the first entry from its `Bumps <name> from <old> to <new>.` line or the `Update <name> requirement from <old> to <new>` of its subject or of the pull request's title.

A failed check is an error. The decision is automatic only when all of these hold:

1. Every updated dependency has update type `version-update:semver-patch` or `version-update:semver-minor`. A major, empty or unknown type makes the pull request manual.
2. In the `github_actions` ecosystem, the owner segment of every dependency name is exactly `actions`. Any other owner makes the pull request manual; `someone/actions-tool` and `someone/actions` do not count.
3. Every commit on the pull request is authored by `dependabot[bot]`, or is a commit by the updater App `crankyant-publication-checks[bot]` ([Replace the App](#replace-the-app)) that changes only the suite list whose path the caller passes in `suites` or a path or prefix passed in `--updater-paths` (in this repository `tests/suites.json` and `actions/recording/etw-consumer/build/`, supplied from `.github/ci-config.yml`); a rename counts with its old and its new path. The decision reads the changed files of each such commit with `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/commits/{sha}`. In a consumer whose caller passes neither `suites` nor `updater-paths`, a commit of the App counts as any other author's. Any other commit not by `dependabot[bot]`, another App's included, makes the pull request manual.
4. An active ruleset rule on the base branch requires `ci / verdict`.

| Outcome | `dependabot-auto-merge` job | Job result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automatic (all four rules hold) | Disables auto-merge if it was enabled, requests the squash merge, then sends the dispatch. | Success, unless the merge or the dispatch fails |
| Manual (rule 1, 2 or 3 fails) | Disables auto-merge if it was enabled; nothing is merged or dispatched; the pull request is left to the user. | Success |
| Blocked (rules 1 to 3 hold, rule 4 fails) | Disables auto-merge if it was enabled; nothing is merged or dispatched. | Failure naming the missing ruleset |
| Error (the metadata is unverified or not in that form, or a read fails) | Disables auto-merge if it was enabled; nothing is merged or dispatched. | Failure with the reason |

Every run writes a summary that lists each updated dependency with its ecosystem and update type, and the decision with its reason. The job never enables auto-merge: a merge that GitHub makes later for an auto-merge enabled with the run's `GITHUB_TOKEN` starts no `on: push` run, so the merged commit would go unchecked. Because no `pull_request` event reaches the fixed caller `automation.yml`, the job passes `--disable-auto-merge` to `auto_merge.py workflow-run`: it disables any auto-merge that the pull request still has before attempting the merge, so auto-merge is also disabled when the decision is automatic but no merge happens (such as when a required check is still pending). A pull request without auto-merge gets no request, and a refused request fails the run. `ci-policy` fails when `--disable-auto-merge`, the `pull-requests: write` right or the job's event condition is removed. The disabling follows the next completed run of `CI` for the pull request, not the push itself.

The job runs `auto_merge.py workflow-run` with the part's `suites` as `--suites` and its `updater-paths` as `--updater-paths`. It takes the head branch of the completed run and decides for each open pull request of that branch by `dependabot[bot]`, on the pull request's current head commit; without such a pull request the run succeeds and merges nothing. On an automatic decision it requests a squash merge with `sha` set to the head commit that the decision read, so GitHub merges nothing once the head has moved; when the commits it read do not end at that head, it requests no merge. The run succeeds with the reason in its summary when GitHub refuses the merge because a required check is pending or failed (HTTP 405 naming a status check), because the pull request is a draft or not mergeable (HTTP 405), because the head moved (HTTP 409), or because the pull request is already merged, as when two completed workflows reach the merge together. Any other failure of the merge fails the run, and the summary names the step and the response. After a merge it made itself, the run sends one `repository_dispatch` event of type `dependabot-merged` with `client_payload.sha` set to the merged commit and confirms via `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/actions/runs` that a `repository_dispatch` run for that commit started, retrying transient errors; if the dispatch fails or no run starts, the run fails and its summary gives the manual `gh api` dispatch command, while an HTTP 403 on the run lookup reports that the start could not be confirmed with the same manual command without failing the merge. A `repository_dispatch` event caused by the run's token starts runs, and the fixed caller `ci.yml` runs the pipeline for the merged commit on it as [The `dependabot-merged` event](#the-dependabot-merged-event) says.

Rule 4 reads `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/rules/branches/{branch}`, which the run's token may read. Classic branch protection is not recognised: reading it needs an administration right the token does not have, so a repository that protects its default branch with classic branch protection alone is blocked until it has a ruleset. The auto-merge job itself need not be required: GitHub counts a skipped job as a success for required checks, and the job is skipped for every pull request that is not Dependabot's.

Limits:

- Dependabot security updates arrive without the cooldown that `dependabot.yml` sets for version updates. They follow the same rules as every other Dependabot pull request.
- In a repository whose required checks do not build or test the product, an automatic merge is checked only by those required checks. The trust decision is made when a dependency is added, not when it is updated.
- Merge queues, Dependabot alert lookup, and personal access tokens or App tokens for Dependabot runs are outside this workflow.

### Suite selection

The suite selection runs the validation suites of a repository only when a change can affect them. A repository states its suites in a suite list; the suite selection compares the changed files with each suite's stored path list and answers `run` or `skip` per suite, with the reason, as outputs and as a Markdown summary. The stored lists come from the [recording step](#recording-step); `select` reads them and the extra reads and never derives a list. A unit is a suite, or an entry file of a suite selected per entry file. `select` assigns the selected Linux entry files longest first to as few shards as keep each shard's estimated step time on its two workers within 90 s (the 3-minute goal less `FIXED_COST_SECONDS`, 90 s). When a language step runs, the least-loaded measured shard becomes shard 1 (falling back to all shards only when every shard is unmeasured) and reserves `LANGUAGE_STEP_ALLOWANCE_SECONDS` (15 s) inside that 90 s bound. Both `FIXED_COST_SECONDS` and `LANGUAGE_STEP_ALLOWANCE_SECONDS` are single shared constants across consumers, revised by hand when the Measurement reports a higher figure for any repository. An entry file without a duration estimate packs with an assumed estimate (`ASSUMED_ESTIMATE_SECONDS`, 60 s on normal runs; `FULL_ASSUMED_ESTIMATE_SECONDS`, 100 s on full recordings) while keeping no stored estimate for per-entry timeouts or reported shard estimates. A single entry file whose own estimate exceeds the bound stays allowed in its own shard. Linux shards stop at `MAX_LINUX_SHARDS` (20, within GitHub's 256-job matrix limit and at the lowest concurrent-job limit of a GitHub plan for a personal account, while pipeline parts, hygiene and the Windows job run at the same time); beyond the cap, remaining entry files spread across the existing shards. Every shard whose effective packing time exceeds the bound is reported as a departure (printed, emitted as `::warning::` on a runner, and listed in a Markdown table under `### Departures` in the job summary). `select` starts the Windows job only when it selects a Windows entry file, and reuses passed results whose key did not change. The suite selection gives the verdict of a validation run from the results of the selection and the jobs it gated. The suite selection depends on nothing specific to this repository: a repository passes its root, its suite list, and either its changed files or two commits.

#### Selection outputs in the pipeline

The legacy `suite-selection.yml` and `suite-verdict.yml` workflows are retired; the pipeline's `plan` job runs `select`, `suite-selection-jobs` calls `suite-jobs.yml`, `suite-selection-results` stores pull-request results, and `verdict` decides `ci / verdict` (see [Job layout](#job-layout)). `select` writes these outputs:

- `suites`: one JSON object with `run` or `skip` for every suite of the suite list.
- `entries`: one JSON object with the selected entry files of every suite selected per entry file, empty when the list has none.
- `shards`: one JSON list with one object per Linux shard: its number, its estimate, its setups, its `tools` when the suite list states `setups` (the tool name to its version), and its entry files, each with its suite and that suite's command. Empty when nothing runs on Linux.
- `windows`: one such object for the Windows job, empty when no Windows entry file is selected, with `tools` as a shard has them. Its estimate is the sum of the duration estimates of its entry files, since the job runs them one after another, in the full recording as well.
- `jobs`: the runner and the fetch depth of each system, from the suite list's `jobs` (see [Suite list](#suite-list)).
- `language-steps`: one JSON object with `run` or `skip` for every language step named in the input.
- `units`, `reused` and `reused-units`: on a pull request, the running units by reuse key (or an unlisted sentinel key for a unit without a stored list) with their suite, entry files, `context` and `always`, the reused units by reuse key with the run each passed in, and the reused units with their suite, entry files, `context` and `always` so `store --additions` can key them again under applied additions, as [Push lookup and reuse](#push-lookup-and-reuse) describes.
- `identical`: `true` on a push or `dependabot-merged` run whose tree equals a passed tested tree and whose shared digests did not move, so that it runs no suite; empty otherwise. `select` writes it last, so a step that fails leaves it unset.

Its job summary lists every suite once with its decision and reason. The [consumer kit](../actions/suite-selection/consumer-kit/README.md) of the suite selection explains how a consumer switches `suite-selection`, `additions` and `full-recording` on in `.github/ci-config.yml`.

The additions workflow is the part `additions` of the [automation workflow](#the-automation-workflow), which a consumer switches on with the path of its suite list. Its job runs when the fixed caller starts on a completed run of `CI`. When that run is a failed `pull_request` run from a branch of the consumer that uploaded at least one unexpired `recording-additions-*` artifact, and an open pull request still has the run's head commit, it downloads every `recording-additions-*` and `recording-entry-times-*` artifact of the run, applies every `additions.json` with `recording.py apply` of the recording step of the workflow's own revision before any write token exists (writing raw entry times for exit-0 entry files through the 25% threshold, without speed correction), and commits the changed suite list as the [GitHub App](#github-app) onto that branch, with the subject `chore: apply the recorded additions of run <run id>` and `[dependabot skip]` in the body. Runs that uploaded only `recording-entry-times-*` artifacts trigger no commit. The App's push starts the consumer's checks on the new head, where `select` reuses the passed result of every unit that passed in the failed run, with or without additions, unless the new head changes something that the unit read under the applied lists, and runs the units whose stored lists name the suite list, as [Push lookup and reuse](#push-lookup-and-reuse) says. A failed run whose head commit carries that subject applies nothing, so each push by a person or agent gets at most one automatic round. At that bound, when applying leaves the suite list unchanged, or when applying or a step after it fails, the job `additions-comment` comments once on the pull request with the reason and the manual route, `gh workflow run automation.yml -f part=additions -f run-id=<run id>`, which proposes the applied suite list as a pull request onto the branch. A moved head or a closed pull request stops both routes without writing, and ends the automatic route without a comment. [Dependabot auto-merge](#dependabot-auto-merge) accepts the App's commit when its part names the suite list's path. This repository runs the same part over its own suite list, as [Apply recorded additions](#apply-recorded-additions) says.

#### Suite list

JSON with the keys `always` and `suites`. The keys `entry_durations_version`, `jobs` and `setups` are optional, and `module_lookups` appears once a recording stores a module lookup. The keys are written in the order `always`, `entry_durations_version`, `jobs`, `setups`, `suites`, `module_lookups`:

```json
{
  "always": [],
  "suites": [
    {
      "name": "unit",
      "entries": ["checks/test_*.py"],
      "command": ["python3", "-m", "unittest", "-v"],
      "setups": ["python"],
      "extra_paths": ["fixtures/**"]
    },
    {
      "name": "stored",
      "entries": ["checks/test_stored.py"],
      "command": ["python3", "-m", "unittest", "-v"],
      "setups": ["python"],
      "entry_durations": {"checks/test_stored.py": 4.2},
      "paths": ["checks/test_stored.py", "src/**", "config/settings.toml"]
    },
    {
      "name": "mod",
      "entries": ["checks/run.lua"],
      "command": ["lua"],
      "setups": ["lua"],
      "paths": ["checks/run.lua", "src/**/*.lua"]
    }
  ]
}
```

- `always`: repository-wide patterns whose changes can affect every suite. A repository lists only such reads; this repository's suite list has none.
- `entry_durations_version`: optional positive integer (omitted in version 1). Records the measurement scale of stored `entry_durations`. When older than the code's `ENTRY_DURATIONS_VERSION` (`2`), the full recording rescales every exit-0 entry file directly to its measured entry time without applying a speed factor and writes the current version; pull-request applies keep the stored version unchanged so the next full recording still rescales the whole suite list. Run the full recording by hand right after merging a scale-version bump so the window of mixed scales stays short.
- `jobs`: optional. Per operating system, `linux` and `windows`, the `runner` label (default `ubuntu-26.04` and `windows-2025`) and the `fetch-depth` of the checkout (default `1`). A `runner` is a label of one GitHub-hosted runner of the `ubuntu-`, `windows-` or `macos-` family, as the definition `runner-label` of the configuration file's schema is, so no suite job runs on a self-hosted runner; any other label makes the suite list invalid. The suite jobs use them; this repository's `linux` has `fetch-depth: 0`. A change to an entry of `jobs` selects the entry files of that system, as [Unrecorded inputs](#unrecorded-inputs) says.
- `setups`: optional, an object with one setup per name, in the order the setups run. Each setup has exactly one `script`, a tracked repository path ending in `.py`, `.sh` or `.ps1`, which runs from the repository root on the job's own Python, in Bash or in PowerShell. `recorded` (default `true`): `false` runs the script before the recording starts, outside it, for a setup that cannot run traced, such as one that uses `sudo` or starts a service. `tool` (optional): `python`, `node` or `dotnet`, with exactly one of `version` (text) or `version-file` (`<tracked JSON file>#/<key>/<key>`). The suite job installs that tool with its pinned action before the recording starts, and the script gets its location. A suite list holds one version per tool. With `setups` present, every setup that a suite states must be defined, unless the configuration file names it as a pinned tool, as [Pinned tools](#pinned-tools) describes. Without it, the setups are steps of the repository's own validation workflow, the earlier form.
- `name`: lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, unique in the suite list; it is the output key.
- `entries`: the suite's entry files, as repository paths or as glob patterns over the tracked files, in the pattern syntax below. A glob pattern stands for every tracked file it matches, in path order. An entry file or pattern that matches no tracked file makes the suite list invalid. An entry file is a file in any language, such as a test module, a script or a C# test project file.
- `os`: `linux`, the default, or `windows`. A Windows suite's entry files run in the Windows job, the others in the Linux shards.
- `command`: the program and its arguments that run one entry file, a non-empty list; the job appends the entry file's path. The shards and the Windows job carry it with each entry file.
- `setups`: the names of the setups that the suite needs, such as `python` or `node`, defined by the top-level `setups` when the suite list has it, or named as a pinned tool by `pinned-tools` of the configuration file. A job runs the setups of all its entry files before them, each under `exec --setup` unless it runs outside the recording, and each entry file sees only the setups it states.
- `entry_selection`: `true` for a suite selected per entry file. Each entry file then runs when a changed file matches its own list, and the suite's step runs only the selected entry files; the suite keeps its subject, its step and its gate. With `--github-output` the selection writes two more outputs for such a suite, `<suite>-entries` (the selected entry files, space-separated) and `<suite>-modules` (the selected Python entry files as module names), and the step runs them, for example `python -m unittest $MODULES` with `MODULES: ${{ steps.select.outputs.<suite>-modules }}` in its `env`. With `--json-output` it writes the one `entries` object, and a step that receives it as an input runs that suite's list, for example `python -m unittest $ENTRIES` with `ENTRIES: ${{ join(fromJSON(inputs.entries).<suite>, ' ') }}` in its `env`. A suite without the key is selected as a whole.
- `entry_setups`: with `entry_selection`, more setups per entry file, an object keyed as [Keys of the per-entry fields](#keys-of-the-per-entry-fields) states. An entry file needs the suite's setups and its own.
- `entry_durations`: the duration estimate of each entry file, in seconds of entry time (from the start of the `recording.py exec` process through setup resolution, the traced command, trace post-processing and work-folder cleanup) under the mid trace set on Linux and under the one ETW session on Windows, zero or more, keyed by entry file. Every run measures each entry file it runs. The estimate of every entry file that exited with `0` is renewed when absent or differing from the stored estimate by more than 25%: with raw entry times (no speed correction) when the additions workflow applies a failed pull-request run that reported read additions, and with speed-corrected entry times (computed over clean entry files only) in the full recording. When the suite list's `entry_durations_version` is older than the code's version, the full recording instead writes each exit-0 entry time directly without the speed factor and sets `entry_durations_version`. Unrecorded or non-zero entry files keep their stored estimate or stay without one. `select` packs an entry file without a duration estimate under the assumed estimate (60 s on normal runs, 100 s on full recordings) until it has one.
- `paths`: the suite's stored path list, the repository path patterns the suite reads. A suite without it runs on every change.
- `directory_memberships`: directories whose direct child membership the suite reads, or glob patterns over observed names. A trailing slash restricts a glob to directory names. A matching name added or removed selects the suite; changing content inside an existing child does not.
- `existence_reads`: objects with a repository `path` and `kind`, one of `file`, `directory` or `any`. They select when the named path appears or disappears as that kind, not on edits to existing file contents. The operating system of the suite reads a backslash in a `path`, for `check`, for `select` and for the key of a reused result alike. In a Linux suite a backslash is a character of the name, as a Linux recording writes it: `D:\Games\Example\game.exe` names one file in the checkout root, the stored path covers only a recorded read of the same characters, and only a change to that name, to a path beneath it or to a directory above it changes the read. In a Windows suite a backslash separates directories as a slash does: `tools\cache` and `tools/cache` name the same path. A Windows recording writes its paths with slashes.
- `lookup_directories`: the checkout directories in which the suite looks up modules, as the [lookup report](#module-lookups) names them, the root as `.`. A recording writes them.
- `module_lookups` (on a suite): per checkout directory, the module names that this suite looked up there without finding them, outside the standard-library names. A recording writes them; when absent, selection and reuse fall back to the top-level `module_lookups`.
- `entry_paths`: with `entry_selection` and `paths`, the stored path list of each entry file alone, an object with one list per entry file, keyed as [Keys of the per-entry fields](#keys-of-the-per-entry-fields) states. `paths`, `directory_memberships`, `existence_reads`, `lookup_directories` and `module_lookups` of such a suite are the union of its entry files' lists.
- `entry_directory_memberships`: with `entry_selection`, the same membership metadata per entry file. It records which entry file reads a directory membership, separately from `entry_paths`, which records file-content reads.
- `entry_existence_reads`: with `entry_selection`, existence reads per entry file. Another entry file's existence read does not select this entry file.
- `entry_lookup_directories`: with `entry_selection`, the lookup directories per entry file.
- `entry_module_lookups`: with `entry_selection`, the module lookups per entry file (each an object from directory to sorted module names). When an entry file has its own entry, its selection and reuse key cover only those names; when absent, they fall back to the top-level `module_lookups`.
- `extra_paths`: the extra reads of file content, as path patterns for reads no recording sees, each no wider than its read. They add to the suite's stored path list and never remove from it.
- `entry_extra_paths`: with `entry_selection`, extra reads per entry file, an object with a list for the entry files that need one, for a read no recording sees that only some entry files make. `extra_paths` apply to every entry file.
- `extra_directory_memberships`: glob patterns over repository paths whose membership the suite reads and no recording sees, such as the existence of a file whose path the suite takes from a data file, in the form of the glob patterns of `directory_memberships`; a pattern with `**` reaches paths at any depth. They add to the suite's stored directory memberships, and to those of every entry file of a suite selected per entry file, and never remove from them. A matching path added, removed or renamed selects the suite, and a content change does not. A recording never writes them. A value that is not a list, or an item that is not a non-empty string, makes the suite list invalid; the error names such an item by its position.
- `extra_existence_reads`: existence reads that no recording sees, as objects in the form of `existence_reads`, such as a path that a Windows test checks with `Test-Path`; a backslash in such a path is read as `existence_reads` states. They add to the suite's existence reads, and to those of every entry file of a suite selected per entry file, and never remove from them. A recording never writes them. A value that is not a list, or an item that is not an object with exactly a non-empty `path` and a `kind` of `file`, `directory` or `any`, makes the suite list invalid; the error names such an item by its position.
- `module_lookups`, a key of the suite list beside `suites`: per checkout directory, the union of module names that its entry files looked up there without finding them, outside the standard-library names. A unit with per-unit `module_lookups` or `entry_module_lookups` uses only its own names; a unit without them falls back to this top-level map for each of its lookup directories. A value that is not an object of non-empty directories with lists of non-empty names makes the suite list invalid.

##### Keys of the per-entry fields

A key of a per-entry field is an entry file named by path in `entries`, or a tracked file that a glob pattern of `entries` matches. This holds for each of them: `entry_paths`, `entry_extra_paths`, `entry_directory_memberships`, `entry_existence_reads`, `entry_lookup_directories`, `entry_module_lookups`, `entry_setups` and `entry_durations`. `entries` keeps its patterns when `apply` writes the suite list, for additions and for a full recording; the stored lists are keyed by entry file.

- An entry file without a stored list. In a suite selected per entry file that has `paths`, an entry file, named by path or matched by a pattern, may lack its key in `entry_paths`, as a new or renamed test file does. The suite list stays valid, and the file runs on every change with the reason `` `<entry file>` runs on every change: no stored path list ``, while the other entry files of the suite are selected on their own lists. No passed result of it is reused until its additions are applied, and `check` reports every read of it as an addition; `select` emits it under an unlisted sentinel key so `store --additions` keys its pass under the applied lists. Once its additions are applied it has its key and is selected on its own list.
- A new entry file in a suite selected as a whole. A change that adds an entry file to such a suite runs the suite, with every entry file: a tracked file that a pattern of `entries` starts to match, with the reason `` `<entry file>` is a new entry file ``, and a file that the change names by path in `entries`, with the reason `` `<entry file>` is new in <suite list> ``. The comparison is between the tracked files of the two commits, or with a list of changed files between the checkout's `HEAD` and the index. A passed result of the suite is reused only when each of its entry files has the content it had in the run that passed, whether the stored lists match the entry file or not, as [Push lookup and reuse](#push-lookup-and-reuse) states.
- A key without an entry file. In every suite, whether it names its entry files by path or by pattern, a key that names no entry file any more, as the key of a deleted or renamed test file does, leaves the suite list valid in the fields that the recording writes: `entry_paths`, `entry_directory_memberships`, `entry_existence_reads`, `entry_lookup_directories`, `entry_module_lookups` and `entry_durations`. `select` ignores it. The next `apply`, of additions or of a full recording, removes it from each of those fields and prints `<suite>: removed the stored lists and the estimate of <entry file>: no entry names it`.
- A key of a field that the repository writes, `entry_extra_paths` or `entry_setups`, that is neither an entry path nor matched by a pattern makes the suite list invalid, in every suite.

Any other key makes the suite list invalid. A suite with `paths` is selected on its stored lists with the `extra_paths`, `extra_directory_memberships` and `extra_existence_reads` added.

Patterns use GitHub path-filter glob syntax: `**` crosses directory separators, `*` and `?` do not. A file pattern is the file's path; a tree pattern is `<directory>/**`; `**` alone is the whole repository.

#### Pinned tools

The configuration file names the pinned tools of the suite jobs under `pinned-tools` of the part `suite-selection`: `node`, for Node.js, and `gitleaks`, for Gitleaks, each once. The schema accepts these two names and no others, and the plan fails on another name or a repeated one. A pinned tool is a setup of that name that the suite jobs give, on Linux and on Windows, at the version of the pins file of the revision of Shared Workflows that the run uses. The suite list defines no setup of that name under `setups`. A suite states a pinned tool under `setups`, or an entry file under `entry_setups`, as any other setup. A job installs a pinned tool only when one of its entry files states it, and only the entry files that state it get it, on their `PATH`, in the order of the stated setups.

- `node`: the job installs Node.js with its pinned `actions/setup-node` step at `node.version` of the pins file, as a `tool: node` setup is installed, and the tool appears in the job's `tools`. The entry files get the directory of its programs. The suite list states no setup with `tool: node` beside a pinned `node`.
- `gitleaks`: the job loads `$/actions/repository-hygiene` and runs `install_tools.py --pins <pins file> --destination "$RUNNER_TEMP/setups/gitleaks" --tool gitleaks` before the recording starts, outside it, after the tools of the setups by pinned actions and before the setups with `recorded: false`. Without `--platform` the installer takes the platform of the machine, so the asset is the one of the job's system, such as `linux-x64` or `windows-x64`. A sha256 mismatch stops the job before anything is extracted, and a platform without an asset in the pins file fails it before any entry file runs, with `the pins file holds no <platform> entry for gitleaks <version>`.

The pipeline's job `plan` passes the names of `parts.suite-selection.pinned-tools` to `select` as `--pinned-tool <name>`, with `--pins` set to the pins file beside the suite selection in its own revision, `$HYGIENE_PATH/pins.json`. `select` needs `--pins` with `--pinned-tool`. Its outputs for a shard object and for the `windows` object gain a key `pinned`, the list of pinned tools among that job's setups, only when the job has any; `tools` then also holds `node` with the version of the pins file. Without pinned tools the outputs are unchanged. The full recording gives the same pinned tools, as [Full recording](#full-recording) states.

`select` exits with `2` with these messages:

- `<suite list>: setups (<name>): pinned-tools names <name>, so the suite jobs give this setup; remove it from setups`, when the suite list defines a setup of a name that the configuration file names as a pinned tool;
- `<suite list>: setups (<setup>): the tool node stands beside node in pinned-tools; a suite list holds one version per tool`, when a setup states `tool: node` while `pinned-tools` names `node`;
- `<suite list>: suites[<n>] (<suite>): setups names the setup <name>, which setups lacks`, or the same message with `entry_setups (<entry>) names` in place of `setups names`, when a suite states `node` or `gitleaks` and neither the suite list defines the setup nor `pinned-tools` names it.

The other commands that only read the suite list, such as `check` and `apply`, accept the two names without a definition; `select` decides on them.

The reuse key holds, for each pinned tool that a unit states, the pin: its version and, for Gitleaks, its assets with their sha256. A passed result with another pin is not reused. A moved pin alone selects no unit. This is an accepted miss: the full recording, and the next change that selects the unit, run it with the new version. A setup of the suite list that becomes a pinned tool, by the removal of its definition, runs the entry files that state it, as a changed setup definition does.

The recording sees no installation of a pinned tool, since the job makes it before the recording starts. The suite jobs give `recording.py setups` a `--pinned <tool>=<directory>` for each pinned tool of the job, with the directory of Node.js's programs or `$RUNNER_TEMP/setups/gitleaks`, and the command records that directory as what the pinned tool provides, as [Recording step](#recording-step) describes.

#### Selection and fallback

- Every suite runs when the changed files cannot be determined.
- With `--workflow`, changed parts of the [CI definition](#the-ci-definition) select their consuming suites and entry files.
- A change to an [unrecorded input](#unrecorded-inputs), such as the suite list's own fields, a part of a suite job or the recording step's code, selects the units it shapes.
- The suite list file itself is matched like any other changed file, against the `always` patterns and the stored lists, unless its parsed content differs between the two sides only in `entry_durations` and `entry_durations_version`. Such a change selects nothing by itself, as the [Unrecorded inputs](#unrecorded-inputs) table states, and the report lists the suite list as read by no suite. A formatting change leaves the parsed content as it was. A suite list that is absent or does not parse on either side counts as changed, and so does one named as changed whose text is the same on both sides, or, with a list of changed files, differs from `HEAD` only in line endings, as a checkout that already holds the change gives.
- A changed file that matches an `always` pattern runs every suite.
- A change that adds, removes or changes a tracked `.gitignore` file runs every suite with every entry file, and so does a change that adds a path that the ignore rules of its head commit match, as `git add -f` does. With `--changed-file`, the ignore rules are the tracked `.gitignore` files of `HEAD`, every changed file that the index tracks counts as added, and a tracked `.gitignore` file whose staged or working-tree state differs from `HEAD` counts as changed.
- Otherwise a suite without a stored path list runs, and a suite with one runs when a changed file matches a pattern in it or in its `extra_paths`, changes a directory membership it reads, changes an existence read it makes, or adds a file or directory whose module name is a standard-library name or a stored module lookup of one of its lookup directories, in that directory. A suite selected per entry file runs the entry files whose own lists match.
- An added path's module name in a lookup directory is a file's name up to its first dot, or the name of a directory that the change adds; a path added in a directory that existed before counts only as its `__init__` file, which makes that directory a package. The standard-library names are the union of `sys.stdlib_module_names` over the Python versions that Shared Workflows supports, held in [stdlib_module_names.json](../actions/recording/stdlib_module_names.json), which [Renew the standard-library names](#renew-the-standard-library-names) renews. With `--changed-file` every changed file that exists counts as added.
- A glob directory membership selects on the same changes with a list of changed files as between two commits: an added, removed or renamed matching path, never a content change.
- A changed file that affects no content read, directory membership or existence read selects no suite. When every changed file is of that kind, no listed suite runs.
- An invalid suite list or a step gated on a suite that the suite list lacks fails `select`; it never broadens the selection. The caller decides whether the failed selection blocks the run.

With `--base` and `--head` the suite selection reads the changed files from Git. `--compare merge-base` is for a pull request: the files that differ between the merge base of the base and head commits and the head commit. `--compare direct` gives the files that differ between the two commits; `pipeline.yml` uses it for any event other than a pull request, a push or a dispatch; a push and a dispatch go through `--lookup`. A renamed file counts under its old and its new path, a deleted file under its path. When the previous head is the all-zero commit, when either commit is absent from the checkout, or when the two commits have no merge base, the changed files cannot be determined.

#### The CI definition

With `--workflow`, the selector follows the calling workflow, reused workflows and local actions through their executable references. Repository paths in `run`, `with` and `working-directory` fields are attributed to their consuming gates. Local action paths resolve from `github.action_path` or an environment variable bound to it. A `run` path that starts with `..` resolves against the step's working directory: the step's `working-directory`, otherwise `defaults.run.working-directory` of the job, otherwise that of the workflow, otherwise the repository root. A working directory that names no repository path, such as `${{ runner.temp }}`, leaves such a path unresolved. Other `run` paths read from the repository root. `..` segments in any reference resolve after the action path is substituted, and a reference above the repository root names no file. Comments and shell assignment names are not reads. An unreferenced file in an action folder is not part of the definition. The reader reports an unreadable or malformed known definition as an error; that error does not become the changed-files-unknown fallback.

Each part carries its suite and entry-file gates. A gate is `<output>.<suite> == 'run'`, `fromJSON(<input or output>).<suite> == 'run'`, `contains(<output>.<suite>-entries, '<file>')`, `contains(<output>.<suite>-modules, '<module>')` or `contains(fromJSON(<input or output>).<suite>, '<file>')` on an input or output named `entries`, and several gates join only with `||`. A term `fromJSON(<input or output>).<language step> == 'run'` on an input or output named `language-steps` may join them as a control term and adds no suite. Any other condition with a selection term fails `select` with "unsupported suite-selection condition". A child step gate narrows an aggregate job gate. An action used by multiple gated steps retains all of their consumers. Between two commits, a changed step selects its old and new consumers; adding or removing a step does not select unchanged neighboring steps. Job configuration changes select that job's consumers. The other suites and entry files continue to decide from their content and metadata reads. An ungated selection-control field is not an automatic all-suite trigger.

The workflow reader covers the YAML that GitHub workflows use: mappings, sequences, plain and quoted scalars, block scalars and flow collections kept as text.

#### Unrecorded inputs

Some inputs shape how a unit runs although no recording sees them; `select` compares each of them between the two commits, or with a list of changed files between the checkout's `HEAD` and its working tree, and a change selects the units it shapes:

| Input | A change selects |
| --- | --- |
| A suite's `command`, `setups`, `os` or `entry_selection` in the suite list | every entry file of the suite |
| A suite's `entries`, or the `entry_setups` of one entry file | the entry files it adds or whose setups it changes |
| The tracked files that the patterns of a suite's `entries` match | an entry file that the change adds as a tracked file; in a suite selected as a whole, every entry file of the suite |
| An entry of `jobs` in the suite list | the entry files of that operating system |
| A top-level `setups` entry, or its `script`, `recorded`, `tool`, `version` or `version-file`, as a suite's setups state it, a removed definition that makes its name a pinned tool included | the entry files that state that setup |
| The version of a pinned tool in the pins file | nothing by itself, as [Pinned tools](#pinned-tools) states: a passed result with the other pin is not reused, and the unit runs with the new version when a change selects it or the full recording runs it |
| A removal from the stored lists or the extra reads of an entry file or a suite, such as the pull request of the full recording, a module lookup of one of its lookup directories included | that entry file, or the suite |
| `entry_durations`, `entry_durations_version`, an addition to a stored list or to the extra reads, or `always` | nothing by itself |
| A step of a suite job whose condition names a tool, such as `matrix.shard.tools.python` or `fromJSON(env.TOOLS).dotnet`, its installed version included | the entry files of that job that state any of those setups, in the Linux shards and in the Windows job |
| A step whose condition names a language step, `fromJSON(<input or output>.language-steps).<step> == 'run'` | that language step, and no unit |
| A step with a suite or entry-file gate | the units of its gate, as [The CI definition](#the-ci-definition) states |
| Any other part of a suite job that applies before or while its entry files run: the job's condition, `runs-on`, strategy, environment, time limit, the checkout, the recording step's steps and the step that runs the entry files | every unit that the job's gate names; every Linux unit for a job driven by the `shards` output, every Windows unit for one driven by the `windows` output |
| A job above a suite job, its `permissions`, inputs and outputs included; `permissions`, `env` or `defaults` of the workflow that holds suite jobs; `permissions` of the caller of the suite jobs | every unit of the suite jobs below it, or of that workflow, or that the caller reaches |
| A file of a local recording step's folder, or a module that the recording step loads when it runs: the files of the shared digest | every unit of the jobs that use that recording step; a file of its ETW consumer, `etw-consumer/` or `etw_consumer_build.py`, only their Windows units |
| `on`, `concurrency`, names and comments | nothing |
| The rest of the suite selection, the jobs that run `select`, the pipeline's jobs `suite-selection-results` and `verdict`, a step after the entry files that only stores or reports results, and `automation.yml` with its caller | the entry files whose stored lists read them |

The modules that the recording step loads when it runs are `recording.py` and the modules it reaches through imports: the module-level imports of each module it loads, outside `if TYPE_CHECKING:`, and the imports inside the functions of `recording.py` itself. A change to one of them can change the verdict of every entry file through the recording check. `suite_selection.py` imports [revisions.py](../actions/suite-selection/revisions.py), the Git calls that the selection and the reuse of passed results share, at module level, so the recording step loads it too. It imports `reuse.py` and `unrecorded.py` only inside the functions of `select`, the lookup and the store, which the recording step never calls, so they count with the rest of the scripts of the suite selection. [recording_modules.py](../actions/suite-selection/recording_modules.py) names this set for the selection and for the shared digest alike, and a test runs the recording step's commands and fails when a module of Shared Workflows outside the set is loaded.

A suite job is a job that runs entry files under the selection: in this repository a Linux shard or the Windows job, in a consumer a job of its validation workflow whose gate names its suites or entry files. Every suite job records its entry files, so `select` recognises a suite job as a job that uses the recording step; a test on the validation workflow template shows that the two agree. The jobs above it are the jobs it needs through `needs`, directly or through other jobs, other than the jobs that run `select`, and the job that calls the workflow holding it, with the jobs above that job. A step uses a local action with its action file's content, so a changed local action counts as a changed step; the recording step's own files count under their row. A part of a suite job that names a suite, an entry file, a setup or a language step selects by its own row.

The summary names every changed file that no suite reads as read by no suite. A failed selection writes no summary; `select` prints the error and exits `2`.

#### Push lookup and reuse

Every non-edited pull-request run writes its structured tested state (`suite_selection.py tested-state`) and uploads it as the `tested-state` artifact (`tested-state.json`, holding `version`, `pull_request`, `run_id`, `run_attempt`, `head`, `tested_base`, `tested_merge`, `tree` and `digests`). On a push and on a [`dependabot-merged`](#the-dependabot-merged-event) run, after restoring the default-branch record, `lookup` runs on the pushed commit, on `client_payload.sha`, or, on a dispatch without a payload, on the default branch's tip. `lookup` finds the pull request through `commits/<sha>/pulls`, iterates recent successful non-cancelled `pull_request` runs of the calling workflow on the pull request's head to find the newest run with a `tested-state` artifact (skipping `edited` runs, which upload none), reads `tested-state.json` without scraping job logs, and verifies `tested_merge` through `git/commits/<merge>`, which answers only a full id: its parents must be `tested_base` and `head` and its tree must equal `tree`. For a `behind` merge, `lookup` checks the restored default-branch record (`--default-record`) to verify whether the landing base has passed (`record.commit == landing_base`). It writes one of four cases, which `select --lookup` reads in place of `--base` and `--head`, along with `tested_run`, `tested_digests`, `landing_base_passed` and any `fallback` reason:

| Case | When | `select` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `identical` | the pushed tree equals the tested tree | no suite runs, unless the shared CI digests moved or cannot be read ([A move of v1 in a consumer](#a-move-of-v1-in-a-consumer)) |
| `behind` | the tested base is an earlier commit of the landing base | when the landing base passed (`landing_base_passed: true`), a suite runs the entry files selected both over the landing base to the pushed commit and over the tested base to the landing base; when the landing base has not passed (or the default-branch record is missing or unreadable), it runs everything the newer base can affect and counts a fallback |
| `pull-request` | the pull request has no successful run, no readable `tested-state` artifact, the merge commit does not verify, a request failed, or the pushed tree differs from the tested tree while the tested base is no earlier commit of the landing base | over the landing base to the pushed commit, counting a fallback |
| `own` | no pull request, the pull request lookup failed, the pull request names no head commit, or `github.run_attempt` is above 1 | over the landing base to the pushed commit |

The landing base is the push's previous head, or the commit's first parent on a dispatch run. The job needs `actions: read` and `pull-requests: read` for the lookup; the hygiene job runs the same command.

On a pull request, `select --results --default-record` takes over passed results from two sources: the pull request's own result record (`suite-results-<number>-run-`) and the default-branch record (`default-branch-record-`) restored from the default branch's cache scope. No pull-request run ever writes to the default-branch record scope. Reuse applies only to a running unit that already has a stored path list; a new suite without `paths` or a new entry file without `entry_paths` is never looked up or stored directly, and is emitted in `units` under an unlisted sentinel key (`unlisted: true`) with its `context` and `always` so `store --additions` can key its pass once its additions are applied. A unit's key is the SHA-256 of the content of its reads in the checked-out tree (the tracked paths that its patterns and the `always` patterns match, with their mode and object, the names its directory memberships see, the value of its existence reads, the names in each of its lookup directories that carry a standard-library name or one of its own stored module lookups of that directory — falling back to top-level `module_lookups` when per-unit names are absent — and the calling workflow when a changed step of it selected the suite), for a suite selected as a whole each of its entry files with its mode and object, whether its stored lists match the entry file or not, its command, the version `--setup-version` gives for each setup it needs, the pin of each pinned tool it states (its version and, for Gitleaks, its assets with their sha256), the `--runner-label` of its system, the shared CI digests from `--shared-lookup`, and the tracked `.gitignore` files and the tracked paths that their rules match, with their mode and object, and the content of every [unrecorded input](#unrecorded-inputs) that selects it: its suite's fields and its entry files' setups, and the parts of the workflows and the files of the recording step that apply to it. A later head that changes any of them therefore takes over no earlier pass of that unit. A running unit whose key is in either record is skipped with ``reuses the passed result of run <id>``; with `--run-attempt` above 1 nothing is taken over. The output `units` names each running unit by key, with its suite, its entry files, `context`, the SHA-256 of every part of its key other than its reads, and `always`, the always-run patterns and calling-workflow files it reads with its own; `reused` names the reused (or on the default branch, carried-forward) units by key with the run each passed in; and `reused-units` carries the suite, entry files, `context` and `always` of each reused unit so `store --additions` can key it again under the applied lists.

The pull-request record is one cache entry of the pull request's scope, `suite-results-<number>-run-<run id>-<attempt>`. `select` restores the newest with the prefix `suite-results-<number>-run-`, once per run and not on a re-run. Each job that runs units uploads its passed entry files as an artifact `suite-results-*` when its recording check passed (on pull requests and on passing default-branch runs). On pull requests, when the check reported additions, the job still uploads them, after `keep` has marked the entry files that the additions name, and still fails; after a recording error, or when `keep` fails, it uploads none. The pull-request store step (`suite-selection-results` in `pipeline.yml`), unless the run was cancelled, runs `store`: the record holds the reused keys and every running unit with a stored list all of whose entry files passed without the mark, and nothing when any job was cancelled or when the selection job did not succeed (accepting a failed `plan` job whose `plan` output is non-empty and whose `selection` output is `success`, so a failed light hygiene check in `plan` does not drop passed unit results). When the run uploaded `recording-additions-*` artifacts, the job also downloads any `recording-entry-times-*` artifacts into `additions/entry-times` and checks out the tested tree, and `store --additions` applies every `additions.json` with `recording.py apply` to a copy of the tested tree's suite list, as the additions workflow applies them. Runs that uploaded only `recording-entry-times-*` artifacts skip that download, checkout and apply. It keys each running unit all of whose entry files passed, marked or not (including newly listed suites and entry files), and each reused unit from `reused-units`, under that copy: its reads in the tested tree under the applied lists, with the part of its key that `select` outputs as `context` and the always-run patterns it outputs as `always`. The record holds each running unit under that key as well, and each reused unit under its applied key with the run id it originally passed in. A later head whose suite list is the applied one, and whose content of every read of the unit is unchanged, then has the same key and reuses the unit; a unit whose stored lists name the suite list does not, since the suite list's own object differs. A marked entry file or an unlisted unit is never stored under the key of the lists without its additions, and a marked entry file counts as not passed when no downloaded `additions.json` names it. When `apply` fails or the documents name more than one suite list, `store` prints why and keys nothing under the additions.

Only a passed default-branch run (`repository_dispatch`, or `push` to the default branch) writes the default-branch record: in `pipeline.yml`'s `verdict` job, after `plan.py verdict` passes and under `!cancelled()`, `suite_selection.py store --default-branch` writes `${{ runner.temp }}/default-branch-record/record.json` (`{"version": 1, "run_id", "commit", "tree", "digests", "passed"}`) and saves it to the default branch's cache scope under `default-branch-record-<run id>-<attempt>`. Non-edited runs restore it via `key: default-branch-record-lookup-<run id>` and `restore-keys: default-branch-record-`. A cancelled or failing default-branch run stores nothing. Every fallback to running more because a record, digest or lookup is missing, evicted or unreadable is emitted as `::notice title=ci-fallback::<reason>: <detail>` and counted in `unit-decisions.json` and the `plan` output JSON.

#### A move of v1 in a consumer

In a consumer, the recording step, the modules it loads and the ETW consumer come from Shared Workflows at `v1`, not from its own tree, so a move of `v1` changes them without a changed file. The `select` job therefore runs every affected check on a consumer's first run after a move:

- The job loads the recording step through `$/actions/recording`, the revision of the running workflow, and its step `Take the shared CI digests` runs `recording.py digest`, which prints the JSON map of digests in the log line `shared CI digests <json>` and writes the output `shared-digests`. The map holds one platform digest per operating system (`platform:linux`, `platform:windows`), one tool digest per pinned tool in `pins.json` (`tool:<name>`), one language-step digest per shared language step (`language:<name>`), one part digest per pipeline part (`part:custom`, `part:dotnet-test`, `part:node-test`, `part:python-test`, `part:python-verify`), and one hygiene-check digest per hygiene check (`hygiene:actionlint`, `hygiene:zizmor`, `hygiene:zizmor-policy`, `hygiene:packages`, `hygiene:markdown`, `hygiene:reuse`). `platform:linux` covers the recording step's folder without the Windows-only ETW consumer files (`etw-consumer/` and `etw_consumer_build.py`), the modules the recording step loads (`recording_modules.py`, `suite_list.py`, without the selection code `suite_selection.py`), and `.github/workflows/suite-jobs.yml`; `platform:windows` adds the Windows-only ETW consumer files. Each `part:<name>` digest covers what the part's job runs: the files of its shared folder outside `consumer-kit/` (`actions/dotnet-test/`, `actions/node-test/`, `actions/python-tests/` for both `python-test` and `python-verify`; `custom` has none and adds `actions/pipeline/schema/v1/setups.json`), its plan-set time limit in `actions/pipeline/timeouts.json`, and its job in `.github/workflows/pipeline.yml` with the workflow's lines before `jobs:`, from the job's line to the next job line (`  <name>:`), the next top-level key or the end of the file, without trailing blank lines and without trailing comments at the job's indentation or less, so a comment inside the job keeps the rest of the job in the digest. Each `hygiene:<name>` digest covers that check's scripts in `actions/repository-hygiene/`, the pin entries of its tools in `pins.json` (`actionlint` and `shellcheck` for `hygiene:actionlint`, `zizmor` for `hygiene:zizmor`, `reuse` for `hygiene:reuse`), and its steps in `.github/workflows/pipeline.yml` (the `repository-hygiene` job block for `actionlint`, `zizmor` and `reuse`, or the `coverage` / `links` step in `plan` for `packages` and `markdown`); `hygiene:zizmor-policy` covers `actions/repository-hygiene/zizmor-policy.yml` separately and applies only when the consumer has no zizmor configuration of its own. A README, a schema document outside `setups.json`, `actions/pipeline/plan.py` or another job of the workflow therefore moves no part or hygiene digest. A missing pipeline workflow, a missing part folder, or a pipeline workflow that lacks a part's job or a hygiene check's job or step fails the digest loudly, in `recording.py digest` and in `promote_v1.py`, naming the missing part and file, so that no part digest covers less than its job. `python-test` and `python-verify` also depend on `language:python`, which covers the files of `actions/python-language-step/` outside `consumer-kit/`, the tool installer and the pins of ruff and mypy, so an edit of that kit moves no digest. `promote_v1.py` takes the same digests over the same files from a commit, and `SharedDigestScopeTests` in `tests/test_suite_selection_reuse.py` fails when the two differ. The files enter in the code-point order of their paths relative to the `actions/` folder of Shared Workflows, so a revision has the same digests on Linux and Windows, fetched at a tag or used by local path.
- `shared-lookup` runs right after `digest` and before hygiene change detection, comparing the current digests with those stored in the restored default-branch record (`--default-record`), with no log scraping, no fixed commit walk limit and no GitHub API requests. A pull request targeting a non-default base branch uses the same default-branch record and never trusts another pull request's run. A digest present on either side and missing on the other counts as moved.
- When the default-branch record or its digests are missing, evicted or unreadable, `shared-lookup` returns no previous digests, records a fallback reason (`missing-default-branch-record`, `unreadable-default-branch-record` or `unreadable-default-branch-digests`), emits `::notice title=ci-fallback::<reason>: <detail>` and causes `select` to run every suite and every language step, `plan` to run every pipeline part with a digest, and `hygiene.py changes` to run every applicable hygiene check.
- `plan.py plan` counts its own fallbacks beside those of the suite selection: a part with a part digest that runs because the shared digests could not be compared counts `unreadable-shared-digests` (or `unreadable-tested-digests` on an `identical` or `behind` push when `tested_digests` is absent or unreadable), and a part with `paths` that runs because the changed files are unknown counts `changed-files-unknown`, once per part. Each prints `::notice title=ci-fallback::<reason>: <detail>`. The hygiene change detection in `plan` counts the hygiene fallbacks with `hygiene.py changes --fallbacks`, which writes them to `hygiene-fallbacks.json` under the runner's temporary folder, and `plan.py plan --hygiene-fallbacks` reads that file: `hygiene-unreadable-push-lookup` and `hygiene-changed-files-unknown` when every hygiene check runs because the push lookup or the changed files cannot be read, `hygiene-unreadable-shared-digests` or `hygiene-unreadable-tested-digests` when hygiene checks fall back to running because the shared or tested digests cannot be read, each with its notice from `changes`, and `gitleaks-unreadable-push-lookup` or `gitleaks-range-unknown` when Gitleaks will scan the full history because it cannot read the range of added commits, which `changes` reads from the same arguments as the Gitleaks step and whose notice the Gitleaks step prints. A full Gitleaks scan because its configuration, baseline or pin changed, and a hygiene check run because its digest or a local input changed, are the rule and count nothing. The plan's `fallbacks` holds the sum by reason of these, its own and the suite selection's counts, which the plan's summary lists as `Fallbacks:` and the measurement reads.
- `select --shared-lookup`, `plan --shared-lookup` and `hygiene.py changes --shared-lookup` rerun only the checks whose digest moved: a moved `platform:<os>` digest runs the suites of that operating system, a moved `tool:<name>` digest runs only the units that state that tool, a moved `language:<name>` digest runs only that language step (and the Python pipeline parts), a moved `part:<name>` digest runs only that pipeline part, and a moved `hygiene:<name>` digest runs only that hygiene check (with `hygiene:zizmor-policy` ignored by a consumer that has its own zizmor configuration). Each unit's reuse key holds the digests of the platform, tools and language steps it uses, so a pass from before a relevant move is not reused while unaffected units keep their passes. Because `hygiene:actionlint` includes the actionlint and ShellCheck pins and `hygiene:zizmor` includes the zizmor pin, a moved actionlint, ShellCheck or zizmor pin (including an automatic weekly pins promotion) reruns those checks in every pipeline consumer on its next run.
- On a push whose lookup is `identical` or `behind`, the `tested_digests` stored in the passed pull-request run's `tested-state` artifact decide for every digest kind (suites, pipeline parts and hygiene checks) in place of the default-branch record's digests: matching digests run no extra check, also when the default-branch record still holds older digests, and moved digests (including after a rollback of `v1`) run only the checks they affect. On `behind`, a hygiene check that the default branch's side of the change selects also compares its digest with the default-branch record's digests (failing closed with `hygiene-unreadable-shared-digests` when those cannot be read), so changes on the default branch's side are not skipped after `v1` moved between the landing base and the pull request. When `tested_digests` is missing or unreadable, selection, planning and hygiene change detection fail closed, count `unreadable-tested-digests` and `hygiene-unreadable-tested-digests`, and run every affected suite, part and applicable hygiene check instead of falling back to the default-branch record's digests.
- No move of `v1` sends a consumer an event, by hand or automatically: Shared Workflows asks GitHub about no consumer and holds no token for one ([Who moves v1](#who-moves-v1)). After a move that changes a digest, each pull-request run of a consumer reruns the checks affected by that digest until a run on its default branch, a push or a `dependabot-merged` run, has passed under the new digests and stored a new default-branch record. Every pull-request run that starts after that run reads that record, reports the digests as unchanged and runs its normal selection.
- Every suite job calls the recording step with `digests: ${{ inputs.shared-digests }}`. The action's step `digest` runs `recording.py digest --expect` on the files it fetched before any setup or entry file runs and fails the job when any digest differs from the ones `select` took: `v1` moved during the run, and the whole run is started again, since a re-run of the failed jobs keeps the earlier `select`.

This repository's pipeline job `suite-selection` runs the same steps, so its own runs use them before `v1` can move to them. Its recording step comes from its own tree, where a change to those files is also a changed file that runs every entry file of the jobs that use them, so the digest selects nothing more there in practice.

#### Verdict

`verdict` reads a file with the `needs` context of the job that needs the selection job and every job it gated, as `toJSON(needs)` renders it: one entry per job with its `result` (`success`, `failure`, `cancelled` or `skipped`) and its `outputs`. The verdict fails when the result of the job `select` is not `success`, or when the `needs` context has no `select` job, or when any other job ended `failure` or `cancelled`; otherwise it passes. A skipped job is a suite the selection did not run.

The command prints the verdict and, with `--summary`, appends a Markdown summary that states the verdict with its reasons, lists every suite with its decision from the `suites` output of `select` when that output is present, and lists every job of the context with its result.

#### Entrypoint

```text
python suite_selection.py select --root <repository> --suites <suite list> --base <commit> --head <commit> --compare merge-base|direct [--workflow <calling workflow>] [--language-step python] [--pinned-tool <node|gitleaks> ...] [--pins <pins file>] [--github-output <file>] [--json-output <file>] [--summary <file>]
python suite_selection.py select --root <repository> --suites <suite list> --base <commit> --head <commit> --compare merge-base --results <record file> [--run-attempt <n>] [--runner-label <os>=<label> ...] [--setup-version <setup>=<version> ...]
python suite_selection.py select --root <repository> --suites <suite list> --changed-file <path> [--changed-file <path> ...]
python suite_selection.py select --root <repository> --suites <suite list> --changed-list <file with one path per line>
python suite_selection.py select --root <repository> --suites <suite list> --lookup <file from lookup> [--json-output <file>] [--summary <file>]
python suite_selection.py select --root <repository> --suites <suite list> --full [--pinned-tool <node|gitleaks> ...] [--pins <pins file>] [--json-output <file>] [--summary <file>]
python suite_selection.py select ... [--shared-lookup <file from shared-lookup>]
python suite_selection.py lookup --root <repository> --sha <commit> [--before <commit>] --workflow <calling workflow> [--run-attempt <n>] --output <file> [--summary <file>]
python suite_selection.py shared-lookup --root <repository> --digests <digests json> --branch <base branch> [--default-branch <default branch>] [--pushed <commit>] --workflow <calling workflow> --output <file> [--summary <file>]
python suite_selection.py store --needs <file with the needs context as JSON> --passed <directory> --run-id <id> [--additions <directory> --root <checkout>] --output <record file> [--github-output <file>]
python suite_selection.py verdict --needs <file with the needs context as JSON> [--summary <file>]
python suite_selection.py check-dispatch --root <repository> --sha <client_payload.sha> --branch <default branch>
```

`select` prints one line per suite. With `--github-output` it appends `<suite>=run` or `<suite>=skip` per suite to that file, so a later step can carry `if: steps.<id>.outputs.<suite> == 'run'`. For a suite with `entry_selection` it also appends `<suite>-entries=` and `<suite>-modules=` with the selected entry files, space-separated, empty when the suite is skipped.

With `--json-output` it appends one line `suites=<JSON object>`, the object holding `run` or `skip` for every suite, so a job output can carry the whole selection to other jobs. When a suite has `entry_selection`, a second line `entries=<JSON object>` holds the selected entry files of each such suite. It also writes `shards=`, `windows=`, with `--language-step` `language-steps=`, with `--results` `units=` and `reused=`, and with `--lookup` of an `identical` case whose shared digests did not move `identical=true`, as [Selection outputs in the pipeline](#selection-outputs-in-the-pipeline) describes them.

With `--summary` it appends the Markdown summary (including `### Departures` for any shard over its bound). With `--workflow` it applies the CI-definition rule for the calling workflow, given as its repository path or as `github.workflow_ref` (`<owner>/<repository>/<path>@<ref>`). `--full` selects every entry file of every suite for the [full recording](#full-recording) and takes no changed files. Its `shards` use each stored duration estimate directly, pack unmeasured entry files at 100 s (`FULL_ASSUMED_ESTIMATE_SECONDS`), apply no language-step allowance, stop at `MAX_LINUX_SHARDS` (20) and keep each shard under 300 s on its two workers; its `windows` lists every Windows entry file. `--pinned-tool <name>` (repeatable, `node` or `gitleaks`) gives a pinned tool of the configuration file to the selection, with `--pins <pins file>` for the pins file that holds its version; `--pins` is needed with `--pinned-tool`, as [Pinned tools](#pinned-tools) states.

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `0` | Selection completed successfully, or `verdict` reports that the run passes. |
| `1` | `verdict` only: the run fails. |
| `2` | Invalid arguments or suite list, an entry file or pattern that matches no tracked file, a missing entry file, a gate on a suite the suite list lacks, a malformed or unreadable CI definition, or an unreadable `needs` context. |

### Recording step

The recording step records what a job's setups and entry files read and compares it with the suite list. It runs inside the job that runs the suites, on Linux under `strace -f --seccomp-bpf`, which needs no root and no BPF program, and on Windows through a live Kernel-File ETW session, which needs administrator rights or an account with the ETW access that [`tests/etw_access.ps1`](../tests/etw_access.ps1) sets up, as [tests/README.md](../tests/README.md) describes. It installs nothing and stops when `strace`, or on Windows `dotnet`, is missing. A job calls [recording/action.yml](../actions/recording/action.yml) once for its path, as its first step after the checkout, and once with `check: "true"`, `suites` and an `artifact-name` unique in the run, after its entry files. The first call also runs `base`. The second call's outputs tell a caller additions apart from a recording error: `status` is the exit status of `check`, empty when it did not run, and `additions` is the path of the additions document when `check` exited with `1` after writing it, empty otherwise. In a consumer, the first call also takes `digest`, the digest of the shared recording step that `select` took, as [A move of v1 in a consumer](#a-move-of-v1-in-a-consumer) says.

```text
python3 recording.py base [--file <file>]
python3 recording.py start [--output <directory>] [--root <repository>] [--environment <file>] [--full [--job <name>]]
python3 recording.py exec [--output <directory>] --setup <name> -- <command> ...
python3 recording.py exec [--output <directory>] --entry <file> --suites <suite list> [--root <repository>] [--suite <name>] -- <command> ...
python3 recording.py provide [--output <directory>] --setup <name> [--path <directory>] ... [--variable <name>=<value>] ... [--python <interpreter>] ... [--write <directory>] ...
python3 recording.py setups --suites <suite list> --phase before|recorded [--setup <name>] ... [--tool <tool>=<path>] ... [--pinned <tool>=<directory>] ... [--work <directory>]
python3 recording.py check --suites <suite list> [--output <directory>] [--root <repository>] [--additions <file>] [--artifact-name <name>] [--summary <file>] [--github-output <file>]
python3 recording.py keep --additions <additions> <passed list>
python3 recording.py apply [--root <repository>] [--suites <suite list>] [--summary <file>] [--expect-job <name>] ... [--keep-lists <selection> | --keep-all-lists] <additions or full-recording results> ...
python3 recording.py digest [--expect <digest>] [--github-output <file>]
```

- `base` writes the job's environment to `--file`, `$RUNNER_TEMP/recording-environment.json` by default; the action runs it before any setup, on Windows from `pwsh`.
- `start` prepares the output directory, `$RUNNER_TEMP/recording` unless `--output` names another. It takes the job's environment before its setups from `--environment`, or from `$RUNNER_TEMP/recording-environment.json` when that exists, and otherwise from its own environment, and keeps it in the output directory. It finds `strace`, on Windows `dotnet`, on that environment's `PATH`, runs the ETW consumer in it, and stops when it runs on another interpreter than that environment's `python3`, on Windows `python`. Pull-request and push runs record the mid trace set: opens (including write opens and resolved symlink targets), `execve`, listings, `access`, `faccessat*`, `stat`, `lstat`, `newfstatat`, `statx`, `readlink` and `readlinkat`, with the calls that follow processes and working directories. `--full` selects the full-recording mode, which records the wide trace set (the same syscalls as the mid trace set) and writes full-recording results. `--job` goes with `--full` and names the shard job, which `exec` writes into each unit's record and `check` into each measurement of `full-recording.json`. On Windows `start` starts the ETW consumer on the checkout (`--root`, the working directory by default, in its given and its resolved form) and the output directory, waits until its session runs, and records in both modes through that one session.
- `exec` runs one setup or one entry file as its own process, with its own empty temporary directory (`TMPDIR`, and on Windows `TEMP` and `TMP`), bytecode cache (`PYTHONPYCACHEPREFIX`) and directory of lookup reports (`SHARED_LOOKUPS`, under `lookups/` of the output directory), passes its output through and exits with its exit code. Before writing the unit's record, `exec` removes the unit's work folder, `work/<unit>` of the output directory, which holds that temporary directory and bytecode cache: a file or directory that it cannot remove loses its read-only attribute, as Git keeps its object files on Windows, and on Linux its parent directory becomes writable, before another try, and on Windows a removal that another process's handle blocks is retried for up to 5 s. A work folder that it still cannot remove yields a warning that names the path and the error, a `::warning::` annotation on a runner; the exit code of `exec` and the unit's record stay those of the command. The recorded `seconds` measure the entry time from the start of the `recording.py exec` process (using the operating system's process start time, covering Python startup and imports) through setup resolution, the traced command, Linux trace filtering, log writing and work-folder removal. On Linux the process runs under `strace`, which prints the environment of each exec in full, and once the process ends `exec` keeps only the pathspec variables of each such environment in the trace; on Windows `exec` records the process id of the entry file's process and of `exec` itself. A setup runs in the current environment of the job, with the earlier setups, and a setup that exits with `0` counts as installed in the job. An entry file runs in the job's environment before its setups, as `start` kept it, with what the setups that the suite list `--suites` states for it provide, its suite's and its own: their tool directories first on `PATH`, in the order the suite list states them, and their variables. No other change of the job's environment reaches it, neither that of another setup nor a variable of the step that runs `exec`. `exec` refuses an entry file whose setup the job did not install. `--root` names the repository whose tracked files the suite list's entry patterns match, the working directory by default; `--suite` names the suite when the suite list names the entry file in several suites.
- `provide` adds to what one setup gives the entry files that state it: each `--path`, an absolute directory that must exist, each `--variable`, and each `--write`, an absolute writable directory that must exist and is stored under `"writes"` in `setups/<name>.json` when non-empty. Each `--python` installs the lookup report into the site directory of that interpreter. A job calls it only for a setup that gives something. A tool that a pinned action installs has no `exec`: the job passes its location to `setups` as `--tool <tool>=<path>`, and it counts as installed once the setup that states it is provided.
- `setups` runs the setups that a job states in the suite list's `setups`, in the list's order. With `--phase before`, before `start`, it runs the setups with `recorded: false` outside the recording; with `--phase recorded`, after `start`, it runs the others as `exec --setup` under the recording, and then provides what every setup of the job states, as `provide` does. `--setup` names the setups of the job, and `--work` gives the directory that holds each setup's `SETUP_DIRECTORY`, `$RUNNER_TEMP/setups/<name>` by default. A script gets `SETUP_NAME`, `SETUP_DIRECTORY`, `SETUP_PROVIDES`, a file to which it appends `path=<directory>`, `variable=<name>=<value>`, `python=<interpreter>` and `write=<directory>` lines, and, for a tool setup, `SETUP_TOOL`: the interpreter for `python`, the directory of its programs for `node`, its root for `dotnet`. `--pinned <tool>=<directory>` (repeatable) names a pinned tool of the job, `node` or `gitleaks`, that the job installed itself, with the directory that the entry files which state it get on their `PATH`. A pinned tool is a setup without a script: in `--phase recorded` the command records that directory as what the setup provides, and a `--setup` name that the suite list does not define and `--pinned` does not give fails as an undefined setup.
- `check` parses each `strace` log, skipping system-path lines, classifies the reads (recording both a symbolic link and its resolved target on opens, and keeping the read of an `O_RDWR` open without `O_TRUNC`), maps them to repository paths, gives each entry file the reads of the setups that its suite or the entry file itself states, and compares the result with the stored lists of that entry file. It also checks each entry file's recorded write opens against its own temporary, bytecode-cache and lookup directories and the writable directories that its stated setups grant through `provide --write` or `write=`; on Windows, writes outside the checkout to operating-system directories (`<drive>:/Windows` and `%SystemRoot%`) and OS-managed caches (`<drive>:/ProgramData/Microsoft/Crypto`, `AppData/LocalLow/Microsoft/CryptnetUrlCache` and `AppData/Roaming/Microsoft/SystemCertificates`) are excluded as system paths. Any remaining write outside those grants is reported as a departure (printed, emitted as a `::warning::` annotation on a runner, and listed under `### Departures` in the job summary) while the test still runs; when an entry file has more than 10 departures, `check` prints and tabulates the first 10 with the omitted count and total, and emits one `::warning::` annotation for that entry file on a runner instead of one per path. In a pull-request or push run it writes `additions.json` in the output directory, including measured entry times under `durations` for every entry file that exited with `0` and full reads under `replace_entries` for each clean entry file, prints any additions, lists them in the job summary with the route that applies them, and exits with `1` when there are read additions and `0` otherwise. Whenever `additions.json` holds read additions, entry times or list replacements, `check` writes `file=<path>` to `--github-output`, and with `--artifact-name` writes `artifact-name=<name>` on read additions or switches the `recording-additions` prefix to `recording-entry-times` when no read additions were reported (failing with exit `2` if `--artifact-name` lacks the `recording-additions` prefix); without any of them `file=` and `artifact-name=` stay empty. The action uploads the file under `steps.check.outputs.artifact-name` whenever `file` is non-empty, and fails the job only when `check` exited non-zero: on additions with an error annotation that names them and the job summary, and on any other non-zero status with one that quotes the message of `check`, which `check` also writes to `--github-output` as `error`, or, without a message, names the log of the check step. The route is "Validation" below where the checkout tracks `actions/recording/recording.py`, and otherwise the additions workflow of the consumer kit: its automatic route, and its manual route with the run id on a `pull_request` run. The manual route is the one of the form that the repository runs: where its `.github/workflows/automation.yml` calls the shared automation workflow, so that it is the fixed caller, `gh workflow run automation.yml -f part=additions -f run-id=<run id>`; in a repository without that caller, which runs the suite selection in its earlier form, the start by hand of its own workflow `Apply recorded additions`; a suite without a stored path list always gets one. In the full-recording mode it writes `full-recording.json` with every entry file's classified reads, the shard job that `start --job` named, exit code, reported skip and deselected counts, why its TAP report was incomplete when it has no skip count, and measured seconds, and exits with `0`.
- `keep` rewrites a job's passed list, the JSON list of `suite` and `entry` that the job uploads, with `additions: true` on each entry file that the job's additions document names with additions: each entry file listed under `entries` in a suite with `entry_selection`, and every recorded entry file of a pooled suite that has `reads` or `create: true` (replacement-only entries without additions stay unmarked). `store` counts a marked entry file only under the suite list with the run's additions applied. It fails with `2` and leaves the list as it was when either file is no such document.
- `apply` applies one or more additions documents or full-recording results to the suite list. With additions documents, it replaces the stored lists of each clean entry file recorded in `replace_entries` (and of a pooled suite when every entry file of the suite ran clean across the applied documents), removing reads the run no longer made and clearing module lookups of directories used only by replaced entries, while adding only for incomplete entries; for each entry file in `durations` (every entry file that exited with `0`), it writes raw entry times rounded to hundredths of a second through the 25% threshold, without speed-factor correction, and leaves `entry_durations_version` unchanged. In every suite it removes the stored lists and the estimate of a file that no entry names any more, as [Keys of the per-entry fields](#keys-of-the-per-entry-fields) states. It fails with `2` and changes nothing when an additions or full-recording document is not in the form that `check` writes, an unknown field included. With additions documents, it refuses to give a suite without a stored path list a list until every entry file of that suite is recorded in the files it is given. With full-recording results, `--expect-job <name>` (repeatable) fails with `2` and changes nothing when any expected shard job has no recorded entry across the result files. It replaces the recorded lists of each clean entry file (one that exited with `0` and whose test report in an accepted format reports no skipped or deselected test), and of a suite selected as a whole only when every entry file did. It leaves a partly recorded suite without stored lists as it is and reports the missing entry files while still applying complete suites, keeps every other incomplete or unrecorded list (adding recorded reads where present) and reports each kept entry file with the reason. Two options, for full-recording results only, keep more lists: `--keep-lists <selection>` takes the output of `select --json-output` for the change since the recorded commit and keeps the lists of each entry file that the selection selects, with the reason `the change since the recorded commit selects it`; an entry file of a suite that the selection runs counts as selected, unless the selection's `entries` names the suite's selected entry files. `--keep-all-lists` keeps the lists of every entry file, with the reason `the change since the recorded commit could not be selected`. A result for a suite or an entry file that the suite list lacks is left out, and the report names it. When the suite list's `entry_durations_version` is older than the code's `ENTRY_DURATIONS_VERSION`, `apply` writes each exit-0 entry file's measured entry time directly (rounded to hundredths of a second, without a speed factor or the 25% threshold) and sets `entry_durations_version` to the current version; run the full recording by hand right after merging a scale-version bump so the window of mixed scales stays short. When the stored version matches the code's version, the corrected value is each entry file's entry time divided by the speed factor of its shard job: the median, over that job's clean entry files with a stored estimate, of the entry time divided by the stored estimate, or 1 for a job without such an entry file or whose median is not positive. A clean entry file is one that exited with `0` and whose test report in an accepted format reports no skipped or deselected test, judged per entry file, also in a suite selected as a whole whose lists another entry file keeps; the speed factor stays computed over clean entry files only. Every entry file that exited with `0` gets a new estimate when absent or when the corrected value differs from the stored estimate by more than 25% of the stored estimate (rounded to hundredths of a second); otherwise the stored estimate stays. Every unrecorded entry file or entry file with a non-zero exit code keeps its stored estimate or stays without one, and `apply` reports each kept estimate with the reason. A result without a shard job counts as one job per result file. Extra reads stay as they are. It writes every recorded list and every module lookup sorted, and each per-entry field in the order of the suite's entry files, the entry files named by path first and the files that a pattern matches after them in path order, so the suite list it writes does not depend on the order of the results it is given or of earlier applies.
- `digest` prints the shared CI digests JSON (`platform:linux`, `platform:windows`, `tool:<name>`, `language:<name>`, `part:<name>`, `hygiene:<name>`) that [A move of v1 in a consumer](#a-move-of-v1-in-a-consumer) defines, and with `--github-output` writes it as `shared-digests`; with `--expect` it exits with `1` when any digest differs.

On Windows, `check` first stops the ETW session and waits until the consumer has drained it; it fails when the session lost events. `start` stores the consumer's process id and start time in `recording.json`, since the consumer runs on after `start` exits. `check` passes on the consumer's summary line in `logs/etw.log`, also when the consumer's process has ended. Without that line, it fails at once when no process with that id and start time runs, and fails when the consumer still runs 60 s after the stop file; each message names the consumer's process id and quotes its log. It reads the one neutral event file of the job and attributes each line to an entry file or setup by process id: a process belongs to the entry file or setup whose root process, started by its `exec`, it descends from, each process id by its latest start before the line until its `stop` event, and a root process whose id no other unit reuses forms the group of its own entry file or setup even without a start event. It turns the lines into the records that the Linux parser produces, with the recorded checkout mapped to the checked one and each path spelled as Git tracks it, and classifies them with the same rules: an open is a content read, an attribute-only open a stat, a write open is checked against the unit's write grants (excluding Windows system paths outside the checkout), an open of a missing path or under a non-directory is an existence read, an enumeration with a wildcard pattern a listing, and an enumeration of one literal name a stat or a failed probe of that path. It prints and summarizes how long the stop and the parse took.

A test runner's skip and deselected counts come from the complete test reports in an accepted format in the entry file's output (after stripping ANSI colour escapes): a `python -m unittest` summary, a framed or quiet pytest summary with at least one test-outcome count (such as `34 passed in 1.23s` or `======== 3 passed, 1 deselected in 0.45s ========`), or a TAP report. A TAP report is a plan line `1..N`, before or after its test lines, and its test lines, each starting with `ok` or `not ok`. Its skip count is the number of test lines with a `# SKIP` directive: a `#` after the description, then the word `SKIP` in any case, then the end of the line or a reason. A plan of `1..0`, with or without a comment, skips the whole entry file and counts as one skipped test. A TAP report with more than one plan, or whose plan states another number than its test lines, is incomplete and counts as no report, since the run may have stopped early; the other reports in the output still count. An entry file without a complete report in an accepted format has no skip count, and an entry file with skipped or deselected tests counts as incomplete and only adds to its stored lists; the kept list of an entry file with an incomplete TAP report is reported with the reason.

Classification:

- A read or a stat of a path that exists in the checkout is a content read, except a stat-only read; a failed probe is an existence read of kind `any`, and a stat or an open of a directory is an existence read of kind `directory`.
- A stat-only read is an existence read of kind `any`: a stat of an existing checkout file that is not a directory, made by a process for which the unit has a lookup report, a Python interpreter process of the recording step, of a path that the unit neither opens, reads nor runs and that no lookup report names as a found module. A report names its process by id alone, so the stat counts only under a process id that runs at most one program in the unit and only after that program's start; a shell that starts Python in its own process, a Python process that runs another program in its place, and on Windows a process id that a later process reuses stat as processes without a report. A module that Python loads from its cached bytecode stats its source without opening it and stays a content read through its found module. A stat by any other process stays a content read, since a build tool such as MSBuild decides from a stat's timestamps whether to consume a file's content.
- On Windows a stat is an enumeration of one literal name or an attribute-only open: an open of an existing path with the create disposition `FILE_OPEN`, without `FILE_DIRECTORY_FILE` and `FILE_NON_DIRECTORY_FILE`, and with `FILE_OPEN_REPARSE_POINT`. CPython's stat makes such an open, through `GetFileInformationByName` or its `CreateFileW` fallback, for `os.stat`, `os.path.exists`, `isfile`, `isdir` and `pathlib`'s `exists`, `is_file` and `is_dir`, while every content open of a Python process sets `FILE_NON_DIRECTORY_FILE`. The Create event carries no desired access, so the open's options stand in for it: share mode 0 alone does not downgrade an open, so an exclusive-share content read with backup semantics stays a content open; a Node process that reads content, or PowerShell, can still make an open with `FILE_OPEN_REPARSE_POINT`, and it stays a content read as a stat of a process without a report. A stat that reaches the file system without a create, as CPython's `GetFileInformationByName` of a file does on the hosted Windows runner, leaves no line; the accepted misses below cover it.
- An existence read of a path below a file that the checked commit tracks, such as `tests/test_b.py/pyvenv.cfg`, is no read when the same unit holds the directory membership of that file's directory, since removing or renaming the file changes that membership. Nor is an existence read of kind `any` of a direct child of a directory whose membership the unit holds, which that membership covers. Both rules hold for every process, on Linux and on Windows.
- A listing is a directory membership, unless the lookup report of the listing process attributes it to the import system, as [Module lookups](#module-lookups) states. Every listing of a process without a report is a directory membership, an interpreter's listing of its own module search path and PowerShell's listing of its module path included.
- A path that the ignore rules match and that is not tracked is no read (derived existence reads from toolchain probe rules and preceding module candidates included), and no path with a `.git` component is, so the Git directory of a nested repository drops as well. The ignore rules are the tracked `.gitignore` files of the checked commit, matched case-sensitively on Linux and case-insensitively on Windows against the repository path; a pattern that ends in `/` matches a path that the recording, the tracked set or an existing untracked directory hit by `stat` or `exists` shows as a directory, and every path beneath it. `.git/info/exclude`, `core.excludesFile` and untracked `.gitignore` files have no effect, so a recording classified on a machine with local ignore settings reports the reads that only those settings match as additions.
- Any process that reads the index, objects or refs (`index`, `objects`, `refs`, `packed-refs`, `*HEAD`) of the Git directory of the checkout or of a repository copy, the directory of a linked worktree in it included, reads what its command depends on, although the `.git` rule drops each such path. When the checkout is itself a linked worktree, its Git directories are the directory that the `gitdir:` line of its `.git` file names and the common directory that the `commondir` file there names; on Windows `start` adds both to the paths of the ETW session and records them in its settings. A `git ls-files` whose options keep its output to the names of tracked paths (`-z`, `-c`, `--cached`, `--full-name`, `--deduplicate`, `--error-unmatch`, `--sparse`, `--exclude-standard`, `--pathspec-from-file`, `--pathspec-file-nul`) and that passes no pathspec is a directory membership of `**`. One that passes a pathspec reads what that pathspec can list: a literal path is an existence read of that path, of kind `any`, and a directory membership `<path>/**`; a pathspec with `*` or `?` is a membership in which each of them is `**`, and `<pattern>/**` of that pattern; a `:(glob)` pathspec is a membership of its own pattern and `<pattern>/**`, and without `*` or `?` a literal path; an `:(exclude)` pathspec, also as `:!` or `:^`, adds nothing. Any other form is a directory membership of `**`: other magic, a bracket class or a backslash, a `.` or `..` part, a listing whose every pathspec is an exclude, a pathspec relative to a working directory other than the root of the checkout or of a repository copy, an option of Git's own before the command such as `-C`, a pathspec file, and a pathspec variable (`GIT_LITERAL_PATHSPECS`, `GIT_GLOB_PATHSPECS`, `GIT_NOGLOB_PATHSPECS` or `GIT_ICASE_PATHSPECS`) in the environment of the process. On Windows the recording has no environment of a process, so every pathspec there is a directory membership of `**`. A `git cat-file` without a `--batch` option whose every object argument is a full object id, with or without a `^{<type>}` suffix, is a content read of the tracked paths of the checked commit that hold each object: the paths of a blob, `<directory>/**` for the tree of a directory, and `**` for the checked commit and its tree; an id that no tracked path of the checked commit holds falls back to `**`. Every other reader of `.git/` state — including non-`git` processes such as Python, `dulwich` or `pygit2` — is a content read of `**`, so any change to a tracked path selects it. Git reads `HEAD` whenever it finds the Git directory, so a command that needs only the configuration, such as `git remote get-url`, falls under that last case too; a read of `.git/config` alone stays dropped. `check` pairs each read with the active `execve` (Linux) or process start (Windows) of its process id in event order.
- An absent probe that a PowerShell process (`pwsh` or `powershell` by its executable) makes in its working directory for a name ending in `.ps1` or an extension of the default Windows `PATHEXT` (`.com`, `.exe`, `.bat`, `.cmd`, `.vbs`, `.vbe`, `.js`, `.jse`, `.wsf`, `.wsh`, `.msc`, `.cpl`) is its command search and no repository path. On Windows the recording knows only the working directory of an entry file's or setup's own process; a process that it starts has none, so that process's probes stay existence reads, which may over-select but never skips a suite. Every other absent probe stays an existence read: one by another program, one of a name that a started program shares, a whole command line probed as one name, and an absent path that a process tries to run.
- On Windows, a path that ends in `::$DATA` is a read of the file itself; a path that names another stream, such as `<file>:Zone.Identifier` or `<file>:<name>:$DATA`, is no repository path. On Linux a colon is an ordinary character of a path.
- Under the temporary directory of `exec`, a directory that a `git clone` or `git worktree add` with the checkout as source creates is a repository copy; its paths map back by their relative path, tracked paths and absent probes alike. `check` finds such a command in the arguments of each `execve` on Linux and in the command line of each process start in the neutral event file on Windows. A relative source or destination resolves against the process's working directory where the recording knows it: on Linux always, on Windows only for an entry file's or setup's own process, from the record that its `exec` wrote. `check` fails and names the process and its command when a `git clone` or `git worktree add` has a relative source or destination in a working directory that the recording does not know, or when a `git` process's start has no command line; the command then gets absolute paths, `git worktree add` with `-C <checkout>`. A directory filled by copying files is no repository copy: the copier's own reads in the checkout name the copied files. A fixture repository that is no clone or worktree of the checkout is none either, so its reads and probes stay out of the path lists. On Windows a local `git clone`, `fetch`, `push`, `ls-remote` or `archive --remote` runs with a working directory outside the checkout, so that it makes no probe there, just as a Git call that ends its revision arguments with `--` in the next bullet makes none. Git starts its transport helper through an MSYS2 shell, and the shell looks up the quoted command text as a name in its working directory. With an absolute source those names change on every run, and no classification rule drops a name because of its shape. On Windows the inner clone of `git submodule add` with a local source runs in the submodule's working directory, which Git chooses, so `check` reports its names as additions, like any name that changes on every run.
- An absent probe made by a `git` process for a `<revision>:<path>` argument before `--` adds no existence read (the `git` process still counts as reading every tracked path through its `.git/` state reads). A bare revision argument without a colon (such as `HEAD`), made with the checkout as working directory, stays an existence read, since a file of that name changes what Git does; a probe name that changes on every run shows as an addition, and ending revision arguments with `--` stops the probe. On Windows the probe is an attribute query, which the recording does not see, so it is the accepted miss of an existence read through an attribute query.
- A start of another language's program counts through the process tree; the suite list has no field for edges between languages.

Toolchain probe rules: [toolchain_probes.json](../actions/recording/toolchain_probes.json) holds, per toolchain, the names that the toolchain looks for to configure its run and the directories it looks in, each rule with its source. A toolchain is a language toolchain of the languages that Shared Workflows supports that an entry file or setup runs; a tool that a setup installs, such as ruff, mypy, gitleaks or zizmor, is none. When a recording shows a process of a toolchain's program, `check` adds to the reads of that entry file or setup an existence read of kind `any` of every name of every rule in every directory the rule names, unless the recording already holds that path or the tracked `.gitignore` rules match it; it does so on Linux and Windows, in every trace set. A rule starts from one or more anchors, and an upward rule also covers every directory above it up to the checkout root:

- project or solution: the directory of each project file (`.csproj`, `.fsproj`, `.vbproj`, `.proj`) or solution file (`.sln`, `.slnx`, `.slnf`) that a process of the toolchain reads in the checkout or that a start of its program names as an argument (including `--project <file>`);
- argument: a directory that a start of the toolchain's program names as a positional argument (skipping option values after value-taking non-project flags such as `--results-directory` or `-o`, and stopping at `--`);
- working: the working directory of each process of the toolchain.

An argument is placed when it is absolute or when the recording knows the working directory of the start that names it. On Linux the recording knows the working directory and the arguments of every process; a process starts in its parent's working directory also when `strace` writes its own lines before the parent's clone or fork return, as it can for a `vfork` child. On Windows it knows the working directory only of the process that `exec` starts: a toolchain process that another process starts places only its absolute arguments, and its working-directory rules start from the working directory of the recorded command and from every checkout directory in which that process read.

- The .NET SDK with MSBuild and NuGet (`dotnet`): `Directory.Build.props`, `Directory.Build.targets` and `Directory.Packages.props` upward from the project and argument directories; `Directory.Build.rsp` upward from the project, solution and argument directories; `Directory.Solution.props` and `Directory.Solution.targets` upward from the solution and argument directories; `before.<solution file>.targets` and `after.<solution file>.targets` in the solution directory; `nuget.config`, `NuGet.config` and `NuGet.Config`, and `global.json`, upward from the project, solution, argument and working directories.
- Python, PowerShell, Node.js with npm, and Lua have no rules: their configuration lies outside the checkout or the recording sees it as an open, and their other probes are module lookups or existence reads of the code.

A toolchain gets its rules when a consumer, or this repository, first runs it.

The classification rules are tested on stored strace recordings and on recorded neutral ETW event files, and the tests of the recording step start no tracer of their own: they run under the recording like every other entry file, so real `strace` output is parsed in every recorded run. [Renew the stored strace recordings](#renew-the-stored-strace-recordings) says how the stored recordings are made.

These misses are accepted: a branch that no run takes; a change of the content of a file that a Python process with a lookup report only stats, such as one whose timestamp it compares, since such a stat-only read is an existence read; on Windows, any other existence read through an attribute query or a stat call, present or absent, outside a toolchain's probe rules, and a Node process's absent lookup in a checkout directory in which it opened nothing; the reads of a test skipped or deselected; and any read that no recording sees. The change that adds a read no recording sees states it as an extra read.

A read is inside the stored lists when a stored pattern or an always-run pattern matches it; an existence read also when the same path is stored with the same kind or kind `any`, its parent directory is a stored membership (for kind `any`), a stored `<directory>/**` pattern covers it (`<directory>` itself only for kind `directory`, and paths beneath `<directory>/` for any kind), or, for a directory, a stored pattern matches a tracked file beneath it; a membership when the same directory, `<directory>/*` or `<directory>/**` is stored as a membership or covered by a stored `<directory>/**` pattern. The existence reads that a unit's own directory memberships cover, as the classification states, are not among its reads, so its stored lists hold the membership in their place.

| Code | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `0` | The command succeeded; `check` found every read inside the stored lists or wrote a full-recording result; `exec` passes the command's own exit code. |
| `1` | `check`: the recording reads outside the stored lists; `digest --expect`: the digest differs from the expected one. |
| `2` | Invalid arguments, an entry file whose stated setup the job did not install, a missing tool or writable directory for `provide`, an interpreter for `provide --python` that names no site directory, a `start` on another interpreter than the job's, no `strace` on Linux, no `dotnet`, an ETW consumer that exited before its session ran, for want of administrator rights or the ETW access of `tests/etw_access.ps1`, or no committed consumer on Windows, or another system for `start`, an ETW session that lost events or whose consumer ended without its summary line or did not stop, no recording in the output directory, an unreadable suite list or input file, an additions document or passed list that `keep` cannot read, an additions or full-recording document that `apply` finds in another form than `check` writes, a full recording missing an expected job (`--expect-job`), an entry file that the suite list lacks or names in several suites without `--suite`, or a suite without a stored path list whose entry files are not all recorded when applying additions. |

#### Module lookups

A Python interpreter's path finder looks for a module file only when its name is in its cached listing of the directory, so a lookup that finds nothing in a directory leaves a listing and no failed open. The lookup report [shared_lookup_report.py](../actions/recording/shared_lookup_report.py) names these lookups. `provide --python` copies it into the site directory of the interpreter and writes `shared-lookups.pth` there, whose one import line loads it and adds no directory to the module search path. It loads into every process of that interpreter that runs `site`, `-I` and `-E` included, and into no process started with `-S` or in a virtual environment without the system site directory. While `SHARED_LOOKUPS` names a directory, it appends to `<process id>.tsv` there, as the lookups happen, every directory whose listing the import system makes and every module name it looks up in a directory, found, absent or as a namespace portion, with the file suffixes the path finder tries. `check` reads the reports beside the recording:

- A listing of a directory by a thread on Linux, or by a process on Windows where ETW events carry process ids only, counts against the listings of that directory that its report credits to that thread or process; each listing beyond them is a directory membership. A listing is one `getdents` sequence on a descriptor since its open on Linux, and one enumeration line of the neutral event file on Windows, where the consumer writes the first enumeration of each open of a directory however often the process lists it.
- A module name looked up in a checkout directory without being found there, or found there as a namespace portion, is a module lookup. The entry file's lookup directories gain the directory, and the suite list's module lookups of that directory gain the name unless it is a standard-library name.
- A found module in a checkout directory is a content read of its file and makes the directory a lookup directory; `check` adds an existence read of kind `directory` of its package directory and of kind `any` of its name with each suffix that the path finder tries before the found file's suffix, such as its extension-module names for a source file, unless the recording already holds them or the tracked `.gitignore` rules match them. A found package has none.
- Node has no lookup report. On Windows, the entry file of a Node process, `node` or `tsc` by its program, including a process of a setup whose reads reach that entry file, reads as a directory membership every checkout directory in which that process opened a file, except a directory that the ignore rules match. An absent lookup in a checkout directory in which the process opened nothing, such as a `node_modules` directory that does not exist, is not seen. On Linux, `strace` records Node's candidate and extension probes as existence reads.

`apply` adds the lookup directories and the module lookups. With full-recording results the module lookups of a directory become those that the recorded entry files report, and keep the stored ones while an entry file whose lists stay looks up modules there.

#### ETW consumer

The ETW consumer is a C# program in `recording/etw-consumer/` on TraceEvent, framework-dependent on the .NET runtime that its project names; the project file, `global.json` and `packages.lock.json` hold the versions. It only reads the live session, with the Kernel-File keywords `0xF0` (file names, file I/O, operation ends and creates), the Kernel-Process keyword `0x10` and the kernel process events, which carry a process's command line, and writes the neutral event file: one line per event in stream order, with the process id, the parent process id, the operation and the NT device path, separated by tabs. It checks the event data length of every event before reading its fields and rejects truncated events without corrupting the recording. Its operations are `start` (a process start, with its image and, in a fifth column, its command line paired by process id and parent process id, with backslashes, tabs and line breaks escaped as `\\`, `\t`, `\n` and `\r`; a start written before its command line came, because another line of the process or of a child came first, has no fifth column), `stop` (a process stop from `ProcessStop`, which clears the consumer's per-process maps when its sequence number matches so a reused process id does not inherit earlier state), `open` and `opendir` (an open of an existing file or directory that neither creates nor overwrites it), `openattr` (such an open that is attribute-only, as the classification rules of the [recording step](#recording-step) state, deduplicated apart from `open`, so a process that stats and reads a file writes both lines), `write` (a successful create or overwrite open under a path prefix or any DOS volume; a `FILE_OPEN_IF` open under a path prefix that opened an existing path also writes its read operation), `absent` (a read open of a missing path or one that fails with `STATUS_NOT_A_DIRECTORY`), and `enum` and `enum-absent` (a directory enumeration, with the directory, a backslash and the queried pattern). It writes read events only under the paths that `start` names, write events under those paths and any DOS drive's NT device, and each process's repeated event once, except an enumeration (the first enumeration of each open of a directory, the one that starts a listing, every time, and none of the queries that continue it) and `stop`. On stop it prints the time it took to drain the session, the counts of events and lines, and the events the session lost.

The built consumer is committed in `recording/etw-consumer/build/`, which a consumer's job receives with the checkout of the step at `v1`; no recording job builds or downloads it. The SDK is pinned in `etw-consumer/global.json` and the packages in `etw-consumer/packages.lock.json`. The build is byte-reproducible on Windows: the same source gives the same files on any Windows machine with the pinned SDK, while on Linux the SDK writes other line endings into its generated files and the assembly differs. `.gitattributes` keeps Git from converting the committed files.

The check runs once, as the Windows suite `etw-consumer-build` of this repository's `tests/suites.json`, whose entry file `tests/test_etw_consumer_build.py` runs `etw_consumer_build.py check` in the `windows` job of `suite-jobs.yml`, which the pipeline calls, after the `dotnet` setup, `actions/setup-dotnet` with the SDK that `global.json` pins. `check` publishes the source with the pinned SDK from the locked packages and compares the result file by file and byte by byte with the committed build; it fails and names each differing, missing or extra file. The job keeps no rebuilt consumer: the repair is a local rebuild, as [Change the ETW consumer](#change-the-etw-consumer) describes, or on a Dependabot pull request the [rebuild workflow](#the-rebuild-workflow). The suite is selected on its recorded reads, and its result reaches `ci / suite-selection-jobs / windows` through that job, so a change of the consumer's source without a matching build fails the required check.

```text
python recording/etw_consumer_build.py build
python recording/etw_consumer_build.py check
```

#### The rebuild workflow

`rebuild-etw-consumer.yml` commits the rebuilt consumer to a Dependabot pull request that changes the consumer's source, so that an update of its package or its SDK passes CI on its own branch. It runs on `workflow_run` when `CI` completes, one run per head branch at a time, and is the only workflow that runs `etw_consumer_build.py build`; `ci-policy` keeps it so. Its jobs:

| Job | Runner | Rights | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `plan` | `ubuntu-slim` | `contents: read`, `pull-requests: read` | Runs only for a failed `pull_request` run of a `dependabot/` branch of this repository. A run whose head commit carries the subject `chore: rebuild the ETW consumer` rebuilds nothing. Otherwise it acts when the open pull request by `dependabot[bot]` at the run's head changes a file under `etw-consumer/` outside `etw-consumer/build/`. The `comment` job holds the same condition, and `ci-policy` keeps both equal. |
| `build` | `windows-2025` | `contents: read` | Checks out the run's head without credentials, sets up the SDK that its `global.json` pins, runs `python actions/recording/etw_consumer_build.py build` and uploads `etw-consumer/build/` as the artifact `etw-consumer-build`. It holds no App token. |
| `push` | `ubuntu-slim` | `actions: read`, `contents: read` | Runs the workflow's own tools on a checkout of the run's head: when the run uploaded at least one unexpired `recording-additions-*` artifact, downloads every `recording-additions-*` and `recording-entry-times-*` artifact of the run and applies them to the suite list with `recording.py apply`, and replaces `etw-consumer/build/` with the rebuild. When nothing changed, it commits nothing. A changed file other than `etw-consumer/build/` and `tests/suites.json` fails the job. Otherwise it commits `chore: rebuild the ETW consumer`, with `[dependabot skip]` in the body, as the [GitHub App](#github-app), scans the commit with the pinned Gitleaks and pushes it to the pull request's branch without force, unless the branch moved since the run. A push refused onto a branch that moved pushes nothing and ends the job without an error. |
| `comment` | `ubuntu-slim` | `pull-requests: write` | Runs when `plan`, `build` or `push` failed or was cancelled, a job past its time limit included. It evaluates the condition of `plan` again, so it also decides after a failed `plan`, and finds no open pull request at the run's head once the branch moved or the pull request closed. When the rebuild acts on the run, it comments once with the failed workflow run and the manual route for the run's additions, `gh workflow run automation-caller.yml -f part=additions -f run-id=<run-id>`. A comment needs the same API as the condition, so while that API fails for this job too, no comment follows and the failed workflow run is the only sign. It checks out nothing and holds no App token. |

The push uses the App's installation token, so it starts `CI` on the new head. When the head's build equals the rebuild and the run uploaded no additions, it commits nothing, whatever else failed. The App's key is a repository secret and no Dependabot secret, so only a `workflow_run` run reaches it. [Dependabot auto-merge](#dependabot-auto-merge) accepts the App's commit.

The bound: a failed run on the workflow's own rebuild commit rebuilds nothing, so each push gets at most one rebuild.

The shared automation workflow contains no rebuild logic; this repository rebuilds the ETW consumer from `rebuild-etw-consumer.yml` alone. Each workflow pushes only onto the run's head, the rebuild workflow without force and the additions workflow with a lease on that head, so on a branch that moved since the run neither pushes nor comments, and the new head's own run decides.

The rebuild commit carries `[dependabot skip]` in its body, as the additions commit does, so Dependabot keeps rebasing the pull request over both App commits, also on `@dependabot rebase`, and the workflows run again on each new head.

#### Full recording

The full recording is the periodic safety net of the stored lists and renews duration estimates; complete pull-request runs also replace stored lists when applied. Its shared part is the full-recording mode of the recording step. The part `full-recording` of the [automation workflow](#the-automation-workflow) runs it for a repository that switches it on with `suites`, the path of its suite list, and `validation-workflow`, the path of the workflow that runs its suites on a pull request. This repository switches it on with `tests/suites.json` and `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, the pipeline, which runs the suites on a pull request through the part `suite-selection`. The caller's schedule starts the workflow on Mondays, and the `plan` job enables `full-recording` only in even ISO weeks, so `full-recording` starts only in its scheduled weeks and runs every two weeks on the default branch. A run started by hand records the branch it starts on in any week. This repository's caller is `automation-caller.yml`; a consumer's is the fixed caller `automation.yml`, which `--repo` names with its repository:

```text
gh workflow run automation-caller.yml --ref <branch> -f part=full-recording [-f verify-token=true]
gh workflow run automation.yml --repo <repository> --ref <branch> -f part=full-recording [-f verify-token=true]
```

`verify-token=true` asks for the token verification: on the default branch, `full-recording-close` mints the [GitHub App](#github-app)'s token even when the suite list did not change, and its step `Report the token verification` writes in the run summary whether that succeeded. A run without a change proposes no pull request.

The jobs:

| Job | Runner | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `full-recording` | `ubuntu-26.04` | Runs for the schedule, and for a start by hand that names the part, when the plan switches the part on. Runs `select --full` with the selection script of the workflow's own revision, with the pinned tools of the configuration file, and passes its `shards` and `windows` outputs on, with the part's two values. |
| `full-recording-jobs` | the suite list's `jobs` | Calls `suite-jobs.yml` with `full: true`, the suite list, the shards and the Windows output of `full-recording`, and the `jobs` of the suite list. Its shard jobs start the recording with `start --full --job shard-<number>` and upload `full-recording-shard-<number>`; its Windows job runs every Windows entry file under ETW, with `start --full --job windows`, and uploads `full-recording-windows`. Its checks are named `automation / full-recording-jobs / <shared job>`. |
| `full-recording-close` | `ubuntu-slim` | Checks out the default branch's tip on the default branch and the run's own commit elsewhere, downloads every `full-recording-*` artifact, checks with `nullglob` and `--expect-job` that every planned shard and Windows job produced a result, runs `apply` over all of them and uploads the suite list and the report as `full-recording-joined`. One closing job per branch runs at a time, and none is cancelled. |

The full recording's jobs run the steps of the suite jobs of `suite-jobs.yml`, with the suite list's path, which the part gives, and the setups, runners and fetch depth that the suite list states; no setup or runner label stands in the workflow. The pinned tools come from the part `suite-selection` of the configuration file, which the workflow's plan leaves out: `select --full` and the selection of the moved default branch read them with `plan.py setting --part suite-selection --setting pinned-tools` and pass them with the pins file beside the selection in the workflow's revision, so the full recording gives the same pinned tools as the suite jobs of a pull request or a push. A suite list whose suites state setups defines each of them under `setups`: for a stated setup without a definition, the `setups` command fails the job and names the setup.

`full-recording-close` runs unless the run was cancelled, also after a failed shard. When no result files were downloaded or any planned shard or Windows job has no recorded entry (`--expect-job`), the apply step fails visibly with an error annotation and a summary entry, leaves the suite list untouched and opens no pull request. When every planned job produced a result, `apply` applies complete suites, leaves partly recorded suites without stored lists untouched and reports them, and keeps the lists of every entry file that failed, skipped, deselected or was not recorded, naming each one in the report. On the default branch, it applies the results to the suite list of the default branch's tip as it fetches it, so the review branch starts from that tip. When the tip moved past the recorded commit during the run, the report names both commits, and the job runs `select --base <recorded commit> --head <tip> --compare direct --workflow <validation-workflow>` on the tip's suite list, as a push of that change would select it, and passes the result to `apply` as `--keep-lists`: what the selection cannot observe selects more, so no read that the tip added disappears. When that selection fails, it passes `--keep-all-lists`, and the report says so. An entry file that the tip added has no result and keeps its lists; a result for a suite or an entry file that the tip removed is left out.

On the default branch, when the suite list changed, and no other file did, the job commits it onto the tip as the [GitHub App](#github-app), scans the unpublished commits with the pinned Gitleaks, with `.gitleaks.toml` as its configuration and `.gitleaks-baseline.json` as its baseline when the repository holds them under those names, pushes `chore/update-recorded-lists` with `--force-with-lease` on the remote tip it read, and opens or updates the pull request `chore: update the recorded path lists` from `chore/update-recorded-lists`, with the report as its body. It then asks GitHub to merge that pull request by squash once its required checks have passed: `gh pr merge --auto --squash --match-head-commit <pushed commit>` with the App's token, so a head that moved since the push does not merge on this request, and the merge, made for the App, starts the runs of the merged commit. The pull request starts CI like any other. The request needs the repository setting that allows auto-merge. A run on another branch applies to the commit it started from and makes no pull request; its result is the artifact `full-recording-joined`. When the App's token, the push, the pull request or the auto-merge request fails, the run stops with an error: the workflow uses the App's existing installation and credentials and the repository's settings only, and any other setting is a question to the user (`docs/agents/publication.md`).

### Python language step

The Python language step runs the shared Python checks in a consumer's job that installs Python, on that job's `python3` (`python` on Windows); it installs no interpreter. The step is [python-language-step/action.yml](../actions/python-language-step/action.yml); it installs its pinned tools from the pins file into the runner's temporary directory with that interpreter.

Every check runs from the step's root: the input `root`, a directory relative to the repository root, which is `.` by default, so that a caller without it checks the repository root.

- `ruff check` runs over the root with the repository's ruff configuration, or ruff's default rules without one, and writes no cache into the checkout.
- `mypy` runs only when the repository configures it at the step's root: a `mypy.ini` or `.mypy.ini`, a `[tool.mypy]` table in `pyproject.toml` or a `[mypy]` section in `setup.cfg`. It runs on the job's own `python3`, not on a Python that a setup installed outside the job's environment, and checks the configured `files`, or the root without them, against the packages of that interpreter.
- `ruff format --check` runs when the job passes `format-check: "true"`.

Every check runs; the step fails when one fails or when a tool is missing. `select` decides whether a caller's step runs: the repository names it in the `language-steps` setting of the part `suite-selection` in its configuration file, and the `language-steps` output holds `run` when a changed file is a Python file or Python configuration. When the step runs and no Linux suite does, `select` returns one shard without suites for it.

This repository calls the step in shard 1 of `suite-jobs.yml` (the least-loaded measured Linux shard, with `LANGUAGE_STEP_ALLOWANCE_SECONDS` reserved inside the 90 s bound), which the pipeline calls, after the recording's `check` and outside the recording, so that its reads join no suite's path list. Its `ruff.toml` selects the rule sets `F` and `E9`, and it configures no mypy. [The Python parts](#the-python-parts) call the step in `python-verify` with `root` set to the part's `working-directory`. The [consumer kit](../actions/python-language-step/consumer-kit/README.md) of the Python language step holds the step's template, `consumer-kit/python-language-step.yml`, and explains to a consumer what the step runs, which repository configuration turns each check on and how a validation workflow calls it. A change to the checks, their configuration or the step's inputs changes that explanation in the same change. The `language-steps` input of a validation workflow names no suite in the [CI definition](#the-ci-definition).

```text
python3 python_language_step.py [--root <directory>] [--tools <directory> [--install <pins file>]] [--format-check]
```

The shared Python code supports the oldest system `python3` of the images that shared jobs and steps run on, Python 3.12 today. The language step and the `install` and `test` subcommands of Python tests run on the job's own Python instead, so they also need it to be Python 3.11 or newer, for `tomllib`. The `python-floor` suite checks the code under `actions/` with the pinned mypy against 3.12 and fails on each error that mypy reports under 3.12 and not under the interpreter that runs mypy, such as a call of `PurePosixPath.full_match`.

### Python tests

The part folder `actions/python-tests/` provides [python_tests.py](../actions/python-tests/python_tests.py) for the pipeline parts `python-verify` and `python-test` ([The Python parts](#the-python-parts)). A repository runs its Python tests through either the suite selection or `python-test`, never both, as [ADR 0005](adr/0005-a-repository-runs-its-python-tests-through-either-the-suite-selection-or-python-tests.md) states; the catalogue entry's `applies` excludes a repository that uses the suite selection.

`python_tests.py` has three subcommands, run on the Python that `actions/setup-python` installed (3.11 or newer):

- `install`: installs dependencies with pip from `--root`: an `--install` value ending in `.txt` with `pip install -r <file>`, any other value, which must be an extra name, with `pip install ".[<extra>]"`, and an empty value nothing.
- `cache-key`: computes a deterministic pip cache key from the Python minor version, the `--install` value, and lock/manifest files in `--root`.
- `test`: runs `python -m pytest` in `--root` when the project configures pytest there, with a `[tool.pytest]` or `[tool.pytest.ini_options]` table in `pyproject.toml`, a `pytest.toml`, `.pytest.toml`, `pytest.ini` or `.pytest.ini`, a `[pytest]` section in `tox.ini`, a `[tool:pytest]` section in `setup.cfg` or a `conftest.py`, and otherwise `python -m unittest discover`. It fails with an error that names `--root` when the runner finds no tests (pytest's exit code 5, or unittest's exit code 5 or its report line `Ran 0 tests`) or when pytest is configured but not installed; pytest and its plugins come from the consumer's dependencies, not from the pins file.

This repository runs the pipeline's `python-verify` and `python-test` parts on the fixture project `tests/fixtures/python_tests`, with `python-version: "3.14"` and `install: dev`: a `pyproject.toml` whose `dev` extra pins pytest, one pytest test, a ruff configuration and a mypy configuration. The fixture is installed, so its `pyproject.toml` has an `updates` entry in `.github/dependabot.yml`.

```text
python3 python_tests.py install   --root <directory> [--install=<value>]
python3 python_tests.py cache-key --root <directory> [--install=<value>] [--github-output <file>]
python3 python_tests.py test      --root <directory>
```

The exit code is 0 when the step passed, 1 when it failed, and 2 for an input that cannot be read, such as a `pyproject.toml` that is no TOML, a missing working directory or an `install` value that is neither a requirements file nor an extra name. The workflow passes `--install=<value>`, so a value that starts with `-` stays a value.

### Pipeline

`pipeline.yml` is the pipeline: the shared workflow that the caller `ci.yml` calls on pull requests, on pushes to `main` and on the [`dependabot-merged` event](#the-dependabot-merged-event). It takes no input and no secret. Its jobs are `plan`, one job per shared part under that part's name, and `verdict`. In a caller whose job is `ci`, the verdict's check is `ci / verdict`, the one check of the pipeline that a ruleset requires. Its part `suite-selection` calls `suite-jobs.yml`, the one reusable workflow below the pipeline, so the suite jobs are two levels below the caller: the caller, the pipeline and `suite-jobs.yml`. [Job layout](#job-layout) gives its jobs.

A consumer calls the pipeline from the fixed caller `ci.yml`, one of the three [fixed callers](#fixed-callers). This repository's own `ci.yml` equals that template apart from the local path of the pipeline.

[plan.py](../actions/pipeline/plan.py) in the part folder `actions/pipeline/` holds the four commands `plan`, `verdict`, `schema` and `setting`, which prints the items of one list setting of a part for the [hygiene job](#repository-hygiene-in-the-pipeline). [consumer_checks.py](../actions/pipeline/consumer_checks.py) holds the three [consumer checks](#the-consumer-checks) of the hygiene job.

#### The catalogue

[catalogue.json](../catalogue.json) is what an agent in a consumer reads at `v1`, through the CI guide. `tests/test_ci_policy_catalogue.py` holds its form:

| Key | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `decision_rule` | The four steps by which an agent places CI work, each with `step`, `when` and `then`, in the order in which they are tried. |
| `requests` | The label `shared-ci` with the description and colour to create it with, where a request is filed, what it holds, how it is answered and who closes it. |
| `local_workflows` | The temporary and the permanent entry of the register, and what the hygiene job checks of them, the switch-over check included. |
| `routes` | What a consumer does where no run starts by itself, such as after a corrected title. |
| `parts` | One entry per shared part, each with the same keys. |

An entry of `parts` holds `name`, `purpose`, `configuration`, `workflows`, `steps`, `required_checks`, `needs`, `applies`, `consumer_kit` and `audience`. A part of the configuration file has a page: an entry under the part's name whose `configuration` is the path of its schema fragment and whose `needs` names the key `parts.<part>`, each required setting and that path; a part that always runs says so, in place of the key and the path when its fragment has no configuration. The test fails while a fragment has no page, while a page names another fragment, and while an entry without a fragment states a `configuration`. An entry that a repository adopts through its consumer kit, such as the pipeline itself, has `"configuration": null`. `audience` is `consumer`, or `management repository` for an entry that no consumer adopts: the sync stays an entry of `parts`, so that the shared-surface test covers its workflow and its step, and an agent in a consumer skips it.

Every path that the catalogue names, in a path key or in its prose, is a tracked path of this repository; the test fails on one that is not. The paths `docs/agents/ci.md` and `docs/agents/triage-labels.md` are files of a consumer.

The CI guide is the template `actions/pipeline/consumer-kit/docs/agents/ci.md`, which every consumer holds as `docs/agents/ci.md`. Its fixed part holds the pointer to the catalogue and the assembled schema at `v1` by their full addresses, the CI goal and the CI principles. The principles apply to the CI that a consumer builds itself, the script of the free job and its registered local workflows. They repeat the goal and the ten principles of this repository's `docs/agents/ci.md` word for word; the catalogue remains the one entry for the decision rule, shared parts, local workflow register and requests. It ends with the heading `## Repository choices`, below which a consumer records the choices that no file states. The ruleset and the merge settings are none: the sync keeps both equal to their templates, as [What the sync compares](#what-the-sync-compares) says. The hygiene job and the sync compare a consumer's document only up to and including that heading, and the sync keeps what follows it. `tests/test_consumer_kit_fixed_callers.py` fails on a relative link, a heading other than `# CI`, `## Goal`, `## Principles` and `## Repository choices`, a placeholder, a missing or changed goal or principle, and when the template does not end with that heading. A change to it is a template-class change and follows [Change a fixed caller](#change-a-fixed-caller), with its previous version.

A request reaches this repository through its triage, as `## Repository vocabularies and intake rules` in [docs/agents/issue-tracker.md](agents/issue-tracker.md) says. The consumer kit of the pipeline states the vocabulary entry with which a consumer keeps `shared-ci` out of its own triage; its row holds the description and the colour of `requests` in the catalogue, and `tests/test_ci_policy_catalogue.py` fails while either differs.

#### The configuration file

A repository's configuration file is `.github/ci-config.yml`. It holds `version`, the shared parts it switches on under `parts`, each under its name with its values, the repository's Dependabot ecosystems under `dependabot`, each with the directories of its package files, and the local workflow register `local-workflows`. The smallest valid file is the line `version: 1`. A part that the file does not state is switched off. An entry of the register is temporary, with `workflow`, `request` and `waits-for`, or permanent, with `workflow` and `declined-request`. `waits-for` is the name of a part, or `<part>.<setting>` for a setting that an existing part lacks.

The schema lives in `actions/pipeline/schema/`, one folder `v<N>` per schema version:

| File | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `base.json` | The schema of the file apart from the parts: `version`, `parts`, `dependabot`, `local-workflows`, and the definitions that a fragment refers to: `paths` as `#/$defs/paths`, `setups` as `#/$defs/setups`, whose items are the definition `setup`, and `script` as `#/$defs/script`. |
| `setups.json` | The known setups by name, each with the `tool` and the `version` that a job installs. The definition `setup` accepts these names only. |
| `parts/<part>.json` | The fragment of one shared part: its `description` and `configuration`, the schema of the part's values as an object without additional properties, `"always": true` for a part that runs in every pipeline run, or both. A fragment with `"workflow": "<file>.yml"` is a part whose job is in that shared workflow instead of the pipeline. |
| `ci-config.schema.json` | The schema assembled from the base and the fragments, for a reader and an editor. `plan.py schema` writes it, and `plan.py schema --check` fails while it differs. |

The highest version folder is the current version. `plan` reads a configuration file of the current version and of the one before it, each against its own folder, and fails on any other version. The check reads a fixed set of JSON Schema keywords; a base or a fragment with another keyword fails instead of being skipped.

A part that always runs cannot be switched off. When its fragment has no configuration, the configuration file cannot state it, and `plan` fails on the part with any value. When the fragment has a configuration, the file may state the part with those settings alone, or as `{}`, and `plan` checks them as it checks any part's values; the part runs whether the file states it or not, and removing it switches nothing off. Repository hygiene is such a part, with the two settings of [Repository hygiene in the pipeline](#repository-hygiene-in-the-pipeline).

A part of another trigger class runs in the shared workflow of that class, which its fragment names under `workflow`, such as `release.yml` for [the release part](#the-release-part). The configuration file states it under `parts` like any other part and `plan` checks its values, but the pipeline's plan and verdict leave it out; `switched-off` still names it when a change removes it. `tests/test_ci_policy_pipeline.py` fails while the named workflow is no shared workflow or holds no job under the part's name that needs that workflow's own `plan` job and runs on `fromJSON(needs.plan.outputs.plan).parts['<part>'].run`.

#### Plan

The job `plan` checks out the full history once and runs, in one job: `check-dispatch` on `dependabot-merged`, restoring the default-branch record on every run except an edited pull request, the push lookup (`suite_selection.py lookup`) on pushes and `dependabot-merged` runs, the shared CI digests (`recording.py digest`) and shared lookup (`suite_selection.py shared-lookup`) on every run except an edited pull request, hygiene change detection (`hygiene.py changes --shared-lookup`) on every run except an edited pull request, an initial configuration check (`plan.py plan`) followed by suite selection (`suite_selection.py select --unit-decisions`) when `suite-selection` is configured, the final plan (`plan.py plan --shared-lookup --hygiene-fallbacks`, plus `--unit-decisions` when suite selection ran), and the light hygiene checks (branch and reference checks, consumer checks, Dependabot coverage and Markdown links). `plan.py plan` fails, with every mismatch and its place in the file in the job summary, when the configuration file is missing, is not YAML that the reader of the Dependabot coverage check reads, names a version it does not read, or does not match the schema.

Per part it decides:

| Part | Runs |
| --- | --- |
| A part that always runs (`repository-hygiene`) | always (Gitleaks scans added commits even on an identical tree) |
| A part that the file does not state | never |
| Any other stated part on an identical-tree push (`--lookup`) | never (`reused: true`), unless a part with a part digest has a digest that differs from the tested digests (or cannot be compared with them), or `suite-selection` receives a moved shared digest |
| A stated part without `paths` | always (on a non-identical change) |
| A stated part with `paths` | when its part digest differs from the compared digests (or cannot be compared), when a changed path matches one of its `paths` (on a `behind` push, on either the pull request's side or the newer base's side), when the part's own configuration differs from the base, or when the changed files are unknown |

In Shared Workflows, which calls the pipeline by local path, a part with `paths` also runs when a file of its own shared code changes (`PART_SHARED_INPUTS` in `plan.py`): `actions/dotnet-test/`, `actions/node-test/`, and for `python-verify` and `python-test` `actions/python-tests/`, `actions/python-language-step/`, `pins.json` and `install_tools.py`, each folder without its `consumer-kit/`, which no job runs, as for the part digests. The pipeline's own folder `actions/pipeline/` and `.github/workflows/pipeline.yml` are not among them: the part digests cover each part's job and the files it runs.

A pattern of `paths` that ends in `/` matches everything under that folder; `*` matches within one path segment, `?` one character of a segment and `**` any number of segments; any other pattern matches that one path. A renamed file counts under its old and its new path. The changed files are unknown when the base is empty, all zeros or absent from the checkout, or when `--lookup` cannot be read.

`plan` gives the output `plan`: a JSON document with `version`, per part `run`, optional `reused`, `reason`, `config` (the part's values) and, for a stated part with the setting `setups`, `setups` (the `tool` and `version` of each setup it names, in the listed order), per unit `units` (`run`, optional `reused`, `reason`) when suite selection ran, `compared`, `switched-off` (the parts that the base states and the head does not) and `local-workflows-added` (the registered workflows that the base does not register). Without a base to compare with, `compared` is false. Concurrency lives at workflow level on the fixed caller `ci.yml`: one group per pull request for code pull-request events where a newer push cancels the older run, and a unique group per run id that is never cancelled on pushes, dispatches and `edited` runs, so an `edited` run cancels no running or pending code run.

#### Repository hygiene in the pipeline

The light hygiene checks (`hygiene.py changes`, `check-branch.cjs`, `check-references.cjs`, `consumer_checks.py`, `hygiene.py coverage` and `hygiene.py links`) run inside `plan` under `!cancelled()` once their own inputs loaded, without gating on `steps.plan.outcome == 'success'`, so an unrelated earlier failure in `plan` stops no light check, and `plan` gives the outcome of each as an output (`light-branch`, `light-references`, `light-consumer-checks`, `light-coverage`, `light-links`). A failed light check fails `plan` and skips no other job: every job that needs `plan`, the part jobs, `suite-selection-jobs` and `suite-selection-results`, runs under `!cancelled() && needs.plan.outputs.plan != ''`, so whenever the plan step wrote its plan, and the verdict names the failed light check (also when `plan` gave no plan output). The job `repository-hygiene` needs `plan`, runs on the plan's decision (`!cancelled() && needs.plan.outputs.plan != '' && fromJSON(needs.plan.outputs.plan).parts['repository-hygiene'].run`), and runs the tool-installing checks (`commitlint`, `check-commits.cjs`, `check-title`, `install_tools.py`, `actionlint`, `zizmor`, `reuse` and `gitleaks`), reading the push lookup, bot-author decision and hygiene change flags from `needs.plan.outputs`. It runs the checks of [Repository hygiene](#repository-hygiene) as follows:

- It has no inputs. Gitleaks takes `.gitleaks.toml` as its configuration and `.gitleaks-baseline.json` as its baseline when the repository holds them under those names, and zizmor runs with its default persona.
- The coverage check and the link check take their exclusions from the configuration file, under `parts.repository-hygiene`: `coverage-exclude`, paths of package files that are test fixtures or of folders of them, and `link-exclude`, globs of Markdown files whose links the repository does not maintain. Each is a list of at least one string, and means what `--exclude` of `hygiene.py coverage` and `hygiene.py links` means: a package file at or under a listed path is left out and needs no entry under `dependabot`, and a solution there is not read and covers nothing, and a matching Markdown file is checked as no source while a link into it is still checked. Without the part or a setting, both checks read every file. Each step reads its setting with `plan.py setting` against the schema of the pipeline's own revision and pipes it to `hygiene.py` with `--exclude-from -`: the job reads the file itself, because it runs after a failed plan as well. When the configuration file cannot be used, `plan.py setting` prints nothing and says why, so both checks leave no file out, and the plan fails with the file's problem. The step `changes` names each setting as a `value:` definition of its check, so a change to `coverage-exclude` starts the coverage check and a change to `link-exclude` the link check, and a change to the configuration file that leaves both values equal starts neither.
- The title check reads the pull request's current title through the API, and the reference check reads the current title and body. `ci.yml` starts on `edited` in a concurrency group of its own, runs only those two checks, and carries the code results of the same head from the pull request's latest completed code run into the verdict, failures included.
- `plan` runs the unified [consumer checks](#the-consumer-checks), which read the configuration file themselves.

#### Fixed callers

The consumer kit of the pipeline, `actions/pipeline/consumer-kit/`, holds the templates of the three fixed callers, which every consumer holds byte for byte:

| Template | Trigger class | Events | Calls at `v1` | Permissions of its job | Secrets |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ci.yml` | read | `pull_request`, `push` to `main`, `repository_dispatch` of type `dependabot-merged` | `pipeline.yml` | `actions`, `contents`, `issues` and `pull-requests`, all `read` | none |
| `automation.yml` | write | `workflow_run` of `CI`, `schedule`, `workflow_dispatch` with the inputs `part`, `run-id` and `verify-token`, `issues` of type `opened` | `automation.yml` | `actions: read`, `contents: write`, `issues: write`, `pull-requests: write` | `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`, by name |
| `release.yml` | release | `push` of a tag, `workflow_dispatch` | `release.yml` | `attestations: write`, `checks: read`, `contents: write`, `id-token: write` | none |

Each template sets `permissions: {}` at the top and holds one job, named after the file, that runs no step. `tests/test_ci_policy_fixed_callers.py` fails on `pull_request_target`, on `secrets: inherit`, on a secret passed under another name, on an event outside the caller's trigger class, on a write permission in the read caller and on any other rights of the release caller than the four in the table, for the templates and for each previous version. It also fails when the template or the previous version of `ci.yml` does not grant a permission that a job of `pipeline.yml` holds, so a consumer on either version starts every job of the pipeline. `tests/test_ci_policy_automation.py` does the same for `automation.yml` and the [automation workflow](#the-automation-workflow).

The kit's record `fixed-callers.json` holds two keys. `previous` names each caller whose template a promotion changed, with the date of that promotion as `YYYY-MM-DD`; the version before the change is `previous/<caller>` in the kit. It names the CI guide the same way, under its path `docs/agents/ci.md`, with the version before the change as `previous/docs/agents/ci.md`. `sync-branch` is the branch of the pull request with which the sync brings a consumer's fixed callers up to date. [Change a fixed caller](#change-a-fixed-caller) keeps the record.

#### The consumer checks

Three steps of the job `repository-hygiene` run a command of `consumer_checks.py` each, on every event and after any earlier failure. They read the templates, the record and the schema from the pipeline's own revision, and GitHub with the job's token, for which the job holds `issues: read` and `pull-requests: read`.

| Command | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| `callers` | A fixed caller under `.github/workflows/` is missing or differs from its template. A caller that equals `previous/<caller>` passes up to and including the same day of the month after the promotion date in the record, or that month's last day when it is shorter, with a notice; after that day it is a finding. With such a finding the summary links the consumer's open pull request on the sync's branch, or says that none is open and links the templates at `v1`. |
| `callers` | The CI guide `docs/agents/ci.md` is missing or differs from its template `docs/agents/ci.md` in the kit, compared up to and including the first heading `## Repository choices`, with the same link. A document that equals `previous/docs/agents/ci.md` up to that heading passes for the same month as a caller does. A kit without that template compares no CI guide. |
| `callers` | A file `*.yml` or `*.yaml` directly under `.github/workflows/` that is no fixed caller and that no entry of `local-workflows` names, with the link to the catalogue at `v1`, which holds the decision rule; any non-fixed workflow with a job whose `uses` calls a shared workflow of this repository, even when registered; any non-fixed workflow whose `on` section is absent or empty or holds only `workflow_call` while no other workflow under `.github/workflows/` calls `./.github/workflows/<filename>` or `$/.github/workflows/<filename>` (a self-call does not count); and any non-fixed workflow file that cannot be parsed as YAML. |
| `register` | A temporary entry whose `request`, or a permanent entry whose `declined-request`, is no issue of the repository or is a pull request; a permanent entry whose issue is open; a temporary entry whose issue was closed as not planned (`state_reason` `not_planned`), with the instruction to make the entry permanent: `declined-request` with that number in place of `request` and `waits-for`; an issue that GitHub does not let the job read. The free job's `request` and `waits-for` under `parts.custom` are checked as a temporary entry is, the switch-over check included; one of the two without the other is a finding, and a request closed as not planned is reported with the instruction to remove both. |
| `register` | The switch-over check: a temporary entry whose `waits-for` names a part of the current schema version of the pipeline's own revision, which for a consumer is `v1`. An entry that waits for `<part>.<setting>` is a finding once that part's configuration there knows the setting, among its own properties or those of the items of its lists. The finding tells the consumer to switch the part on or to state the setting, to remove the workflow with its entry and to close the request, and the summary links the catalogue at `v1`. It needs no answer of GitHub. |
| `dependabot` | `.github/dependabot.yml` is missing or differs from the file generated from `dependabot`, `.github/dependabot.yaml` exists, or either exists while the configuration file states no ecosystems. The summary holds the expected content. |

The generated file holds one `updates` entry per ecosystem, in the order of the configuration file, with the weekly schedule and the cooldown of seven days of this repository's own entries; the `github-actions` entry ignores the repository of the shared parts in either shape:

- An ecosystem with one directory has `directory` and the group `minor-and-patch` (`patterns: ["*"]`, `update-types: [minor, patch]`), so a major update arrives as a pull request of its own.
- An ecosystem with several directories has `directories`, in the order of the configuration file, and the one group `per-dependency` (`group-by: dependency-name`, `patterns: ["*"]`), which takes every update type. Dependabot opens one pull request per dependency update across the directories that declare the dependency; directories whose version constraints for the dependency do not agree get separate pull requests. `group-by` covers version updates only. Such a pull request's branch holds the ecosystem as its second segment, which [Dependabot auto-merge](#dependabot-auto-merge) reads, and the group's name with the dependency after it; its title names the dependency and the number of directories.

A configuration file that is missing or does not match its schema is a finding of each command; `plan` names every mismatch. A finding fails the step only on a pull request whose author is not `dependabot[bot]`. On a Dependabot pull request and on a push, each finding is a notice and the summary says why it does not fail the run. The exit code is 0 when the command passed, 1 when a finding failed it and 2 when the kit or its record cannot be read.

In the repository that the template of `ci.yml` calls, which is this repository, each command passes without a check and says so: it holds the templates and the shared workflows, not a consumer's callers. The commands take that repository's name from the template, so no consumer can claim it.

#### The free job

The job `custom` is the free job. A repository switches it on with `script`, the path of a script of its own, and may name `setups`, a list of known setups without duplicates, `paths`, `runner-label`, and `request` with `waits-for`:

```yaml
parts:
  custom:
    setups: [dotnet-10, python-3.14]
    script: ci/check-licences.sh
    runner-label: windows-2025
```

`runner-label` is the definition `runner-label` of the base, a label of GitHub's `ubuntu`, `windows` or `macos` runners, and the job runs on `ubuntu-26.04` when the part does not state it. The job checks out the repository without persisted credentials, installs each named setup in the listed order, and runs `bash -- "$SCRIPT"` from the repository root, with `bash` as the step's shell on every runner, so also on Windows, where the default shell is PowerShell. On a Windows runner Git can check the script out with CRLF line endings, which Bash does not read; a repository that runs its script there keeps it at LF, for example with `eol=lf` in `.gitattributes`. Without `setups`, or with an empty list, it installs nothing. Of two setups of the same tool, the later one is the one that the tool's command names. The script's exit status is the job's result: no later step and no `continue-on-error` changes it. A setup installs with its action's defaults, so without a dependency cache.

The known setups are the names in `setups.json`: `python-3.11` to `python-3.14` and `dotnet-8` and `dotnet-10` today. `plan.py schema` writes them into the assembled schema as the accepted values of the definition `setup`, so a name that `setups.json` does not hold fails `plan` with the list of the known ones. `plan` gives the job the `tool` and the `version` of each named setup in the listed order. A workflow has no loop over steps, so the job holds one place per known setup, as many as a list without duplicates can name, and each place holds one install step per tool, which runs when the setup at that place in the list has that tool. A `script` is a relative path without a `..` segment.

The job has the limits of the read class: `contents: read` as its only right, no secret and no token in any step, no checkout credentials left in the checkout, and no `cache-mode` that writes, on the job or on the workflow. `tests/test_ci_policy_pipeline.py` fails when one of them changes. The same limits hold for every job that runs code of the repository through a hook: a setting of a part that names such code refers to `#/$defs/script`, and the test applies the limits to the job of every part whose fragment holds that reference. It also fails when a job of another part reads a script from the plan.

`request` and `waits-for` are the definitions `request` and `waits-for` of the base, which a temporary entry of the register uses too: the number of the request issue in the repository for the part that takes over the work of the script, and the name of that part, or `<part>.<setting>`. The job does not read them. The [consumer check](#the-consumer-checks) `register` does, as it reads a temporary entry: the request is an issue of the repository, the switch-over check reports when the part exists or knows the setting, and a request that was closed as not planned is reported with the instruction to remove the two settings. One of the two without the other is a finding of that check, since the schema check reads no keyword that ties two settings together.

This repository's own configuration file switches the free job on with `runner-label: windows-2025`, the setups `python-3.13` and `python-3.14` in that order, and the fixture script `tests/fixtures/custom/pass.sh`, which fails unless it runs in Bash from the repository root and the Python command names Python 3.14, the later setup. The script also fails unless the folder of `python-3.14` comes before that of `python-3.13` in `PATH`, so a run shows the job on Windows with both setups installed in their listed order. Its `paths` start the job on a change to the fixture scripts, the part folder `actions/pipeline/`, `pipeline.yml` or `ci.yml`. `tests/fixtures/custom/fail.sh` is the fixture script that fails, for a run that shows the verdict failing.

#### The release part

`release.yml` is the shared workflow that the fixed caller `release.yml` calls on a pushed tag and on a start by hand. It takes no input and no secret, and it runs the part `github-release`, the one part whose fragment names it under `workflow`. A repository switches the part on in its configuration file:

```yaml
parts:
  github-release:
    tags: ["v*"]
    allowed-signers: .github/allowed_signers
    files: [dist/tool.zip]
    script: ci/build-release.sh
    setups: [dotnet-10]
    runner-label: windows-2025
    title: "Tool {version}"
    notes: docs/release-notes.md
```

`tags`, `allowed-signers` and `files` are required. A pattern of `tags` follows the rules of `paths`, with the segments of a tag between `/`. `files` are paths relative to the repository root, each published under its file name, so no two share one. `script` is a hook; `setups` and `runner-label` belong to its job and fail the plan without it. `runner-label` is a label of GitHub's `ubuntu`, `windows` or `macos` runners, the definition `runner-label` of the base, and the job runs on `ubuntu-26.04` when the part does not state it. `allowed-signers` and each item of `files` are the definition `relative-path`. In `title`, `{tag}` stands for the tag and `{version}` for the tag without a leading `v`; without `title` the release is named after the tag. `notes` is the definition `relative-path` too: a tracked file of the commit that the verified tag names, whose content becomes the release notes, the body of the release. It is UTF-8 text of at most 125000 characters, the length that GitHub accepts for a body. Without `notes` the body is the list of merged pull requests that GitHub generates for the tag, filtered by `publish` as described below.

[release.py](../actions/pipeline/release.py) in the part folder `actions/pipeline/` holds the commands `preflight`, `plan` and `publish`. The workflow has three jobs:

| Job | Rights | Runs | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `plan` | `checks: read`, `contents: read` | always | Runs `release.py preflight` against the GitHub API before cloning; only when the ref is a tag that matches a configured `github-release` pattern does it check out the ref with its full history and run `release.py plan`, which verifies the tag signature, binds the tag object and name, and requires a completed, passed `ci / verdict` on the tagged commit. |
| `github-release-files` | `contents: read` | when the plan decides to publish | Checks out the commit that the verified tag names, installs the named setups as the free job does, runs `script` when the part names one, and uploads the files of `files` as one artifact, each under its file name. A named file that does not exist fails the job. |
| `github-release` | `attestations: write`, `checks: read`, `contents: write`, `id-token: write` | when the plan decides to publish and the files were collected | Downloads the artifact, attests the build provenance of the files in a repository that is not private, and runs `release.py publish`. |

`plan` decides from the configuration file of the checked-out commit:

| Run | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| The ref is no tag, such as a start by hand on a branch | Nothing is published; the run passes. |
| The repository has no configuration file, or the file does not state `github-release` | Nothing is published; the run passes. |
| The tag matches no pattern of `tags` | Nothing is published; the run passes. |
| The configuration file does not match its schema, or `setups` or `runner-label` lacks `script`, or two files share a file name | The run fails with every mismatch in the job summary. |
| The tag matches and carries a valid signature, and the commit it names holds no file `notes`, or one that is not UTF-8 text of at most 125000 characters | The run fails with the reason in the job summary. |
| The tag matches and is not annotated, carries no signature, or carries one that no allowed key made | The run fails with the reason in the job summary. |
| The tag matches and carries a valid signature of an allowed key | The run publishes. |

The allowed-signers file is read from `origin/<default branch>` of the checkout, never from the tagged commit, so a commit that adds a key and is tagged in the same push does not approve its own tag. The signature is an SSH signature, verified with `git verify-tag` against that file. A start by hand on a tag ref is decided like a push of that tag.

The output `plan` is one JSON document with the part under `parts`, as the pipeline's plan has it: `run` and `reason`, and for a run that publishes `tag`, `commit`, `title`, `config`, `script` (empty without a hook), `notes` (the path of the file of the release notes, empty without one), `runner`, `setups` (the `tool` and `version` of each named setup), `files` (one path per line) and `earlier-tags` (the tags that match a pattern of `tags` and name a commit that the released commit reaches, other than that commit itself, nearest first: in the topological order of `git log --topo-order`, so no tag comes before a tag on a later commit of its own history, and on one commit the newest tag first). The two later jobs run on `parts['github-release'].run`. A failed plan gives no output, so neither starts.

`release.py publish` reads the plan and the folder of collected files. It fails before any request when a named file is missing, and when a release of the tag exists. It reads the tag from GitHub again twice, through `git/ref/tags/<tag>` and the tag objects that ref names: right before it creates the release, and right before the request that publishes the draft, after the uploads. A tag that no longer names the plan's `commit`, the commit whose signature `plan` verified, or that no longer exists, fails the run and publishes nothing: at the first reading the command creates nothing, and at the second it deletes the draft with the files it holds, so a tag that a push moved while the files were collected or uploaded is not released under the earlier signature. A tag that cannot be read fails the run the same way. When the draft cannot be deleted, it stays unpublished and the summary says to remove it. Otherwise the command creates the release as a draft, with the tag, the title and the body, uploads each file, reads the tag that second time and then publishes the draft. With `notes` the body is the content of that file, which the command reads from GitHub at the plan's `commit` (`contents/<path>?ref=<commit>`) before it reads the tag the first time; the job checks nothing out, and a file that `script` wrote in the other job never reaches it. A file that GitHub does not return, or whose content is not UTF-8 text of at most 125000 characters, fails the run and creates nothing. Without `notes` the body is GitHub's generated list, as the next paragraphs describe. A refused upload leaves the draft, which is not published, and fails the run with the step that failed. The exit code is 0 when the command passed, 1 when it failed and 2 when its input cannot be read.

Without `notes`, `publish` finds the previous release first: the first tag of `earlier-tags` that has a published release, from the list of the repository's releases (`releases?per_page=100`, page by page, until the nearest tag is found or the list ends; a draft is no published release). It then asks GitHub for the generated notes (`POST releases/generate-notes` with `tag_name`, `target_commitish` the plan's `commit`, and `previous_tag_name` the previous release when there is one), reads the owner of the repository (`GET repos/<owner>/<name>`, its `owner.login`), filters the answer's `body` and publishes the result as the body. Without an earlier tag the command lists no releases, and without a previous release the request names no `previous_tag_name`: GitHub then gives the list for a tag without a named previous tag, which for a first release is the whole history. All of this happens before the tag is read the first time, so the tag is still read last before the release is created. A request that fails, or an answer without a body, fails the run and creates nothing, with the step that failed in the job summary. The summary of a published release says after which release the list starts, or that it names no previous release.

The filter reads each line of the answer:

| Line | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| A change line, `* <title> by <authors> in <address>`, read from its end, so a title that holds ` by @x in <address>` itself stays whole | It goes when its title has the type `docs`, `style`, `refactor`, `test`, `build`, `ci` or `chore` without `!`, compared without regard to case and with or without a scope, or when every author is a bot, a login that ends in `[bot]`. Otherwise it stays, as `* <title> by <external contributors> in <address>`, or `* <title> in <address>` without an external contributor. An external contributor is an author that is not the owner, not a bot and not `Copilot`; logins are compared without regard to case. |
| A line of "New Contributors", `* @<login> made their first contribution in <address>` | It stays for an external contributor and goes otherwise. |
| A heading, `## ` or a category heading `### ` of the repository's `.github/release.yml` | It goes when no line stands under it any more. |
| `**Full Changelog**: ...`, ending in `/compare/<previous>...<tag>` or `/commits/<tag>` | It stays as the last line of the body. |
| Any other line | It stays as it is. |

When no change line stays, the body is "No user-facing changes are listed for this release.", a blank line and the line "Full Changelog". A filtered body of more than 125000 characters keeps the most change lines from the start of the list that fit, with the sentence "The list is shortened to its first <n> of <total> changes to fit the length that GitHub accepts for a release." above "Full Changelog". The types that the filter hides and the sentence of an empty list are fixed; no setting changes them.

The job `github-release-files` is the only one that runs code of the repository, and it has the limits of [the free job](#the-free-job): `contents: read` as its only right, no secret and no token, no checkout credentials, and no cache. The job `github-release` holds the writing rights and runs no code of the repository: it checks nothing out, and the files reach it as data. Only its last step holds the run's token. No job restores or saves a cache, and the workflow reads no secret. `tests/test_ci_policy_release.py` fails when one of these changes, when a later job stops following the plan, and when the template of the caller or this repository's `release-caller.yml` does not grant a right that a job holds.

Artifact attestations are not available to a private repository of a personal account, so the attestation step runs only when the repository is not private.

This repository's own caller is `release-caller.yml`, which equals the template apart from the local path. Its configuration file does not state the part, so a pushed tag here, a move of `v1` included, runs `plan` alone and passes.

#### The pipeline's verdict

The job `verdict` needs `plan` and every part and runs under `!cancelled()`, so a cancelled run gives no verdict. `plan.py verdict` reads its `needs` context and fails when `plan` did not end `success`, the context has no `plan` job, the plan output is missing or invalid, or any planned check failed, ended `skipped`, timed out or never started; a check the plan did not select counts as not run. When `plan` failed after it wrote its plan, as a failed light check makes it, the verdict names each failed light check from the outputs `light-*` of `plan` and judges the planned checks as after a passed `plan`. Its summary states the verdict with its reasons, lists the parts switched off and the local workflows registered compared with the base, or says that the comparison is missing, lists every check with `ran`, `skipped` or `taken over` and its reason, and gives each job's result.

#### The Python parts

`python-verify` and `python-test` are the Python language checks and the Python tests as parts of the pipeline: the phases `verify` and `test` of the ecosystem `python`, which the configuration file switches on. Each part takes `projects`, a list of one or more projects with their own settings, and `paths` for the whole part, as [Plan](#plan) says. The smallest form is one project that names its `python-version`. A part's job is a matrix over its projects without `fail-fast`: every project runs as a job of its own, named after its working directory and Python version, `python-test (backend, 3.13)`, with the runner label added when the project states one. A failing project fails its own job and stops no other, and the verdict fails when any of them failed.

Every job holds `contents: read` only, takes no secret, checks out without persisted credentials and restores a dependency cache keyed by its pin or lock hash. It reads the projects from the plan's `config` of its own part and every value from its project. When both `python-verify` and `python-test` run for the same `ubuntu-26.04` project (`python-version` and `working-directory`, with matching or empty `python-verify` `install`), the plan folds that project into `python-test` so it installs once per run and runs both the language step and the tests in that job; `python-verify` runs only its remaining unmatched projects.

| Setting of a project | Part | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `python-version` | both, required | The version that `actions/setup-python` installs as the job's own Python: `3.11` or newer, as a minor version or one patch of it. |
| `working-directory` | both | The project's directory, from which the dependencies install and the checks or the tests run; the repository root without it. |
| `install` | both | A requirements file ending in `.txt` or an extra of the project, installed with `python_tests.py install` as [Python tests](#python-tests) describes; nothing without it. |
| `format-check` | `python-verify` | `true` adds `ruff format --check`. |
| `runner-label` | `python-test` | The label of the GitHub-hosted runner of the project's job; `ubuntu-26.04` without it. The job's shell is `bash` on every runner. |

A `runner-label` is one label of the `ubuntu-`, `windows-` or `macos-` family, such as `windows-2025`. The schema rejects every other label and a list of labels, so no configuration file sends the code of a pull request to a self-hosted runner.

`python-verify` loads `python-tests/` for the installation, installs the dependencies so that mypy sees the project's packages, and then runs the [Python language step](#python-language-step) by `$/actions/python-language-step` with `root` set to `working-directory`: the same checks as the step in a consumer's own job. `python-test` runs `python_tests.py install` and then `python_tests.py test`, choosing pytest where the project configures it and otherwise `unittest discover`, and failing when no test is found or when pytest is configured and not installed. A failed installation skips the checks and the tests. A repository with several test roots lists them as projects of the one part. Without `paths`, a part runs on every non-identical change, a Markdown-only one included; `paths` select the whole part, not one project.

#### The .NET test part

`dotnet-test` builds .NET test projects or solutions and runs their tests. It takes `projects` and `paths` as the Python parts do, and its job is the same matrix with the same rights and limits: one job per item, named after its project, `dotnet-test (tests/Shop.Tests/Shop.Tests.csproj)`. Each item states `sdk-version` and `project`, and may state `runner-label`, with the values of the Python parts' `runner-label`, and `console-runner`; the job runs on `ubuntu-26.04` without a `runner-label`. `actions/setup-dotnet` installs the `sdk-version`: one SDK such as `10.0.403`, or the newest patch of a feature band (`10.0.4xx`), a minor version (`10.0.x`) or a major version (`10.x`). The shared step [dotnet-test/action.yml](../actions/dotnet-test/action.yml) then runs [dotnet_test.py](../actions/dotnet-test/dotnet_test.py) with the `project`, a path relative to the repository root:

```text
python3 dotnet_test.py --project <path> [--console-runner] [--root <checkout>]
```

It runs `dotnet build <project>` and, after a build that passed, `dotnet test <project> --no-build --logger trx --results-directory <directory>`, from the repository root, without the logo and without telemetry, with NuGet packages cached by SDK version and lock/project file hashes. The TRX reports go to a directory of their own under `RUNNER_TEMP`, which the script removes afterwards. After tests that passed, the script adds up the executed tests of every report, fails on any corrupt or unreadable TRX report even when another report is readable, and fails with `no test ran in <project>` when the sum is zero or no report exists: a test project without a test, a project that is no test project, such as a wrong path to a library, or a test runner that writes no TRX report for `--logger trx`. The exit code is 0 when both commands passed and a test ran, 1 when the build or the tests failed, a report was corrupt or no test ran, and 2 when the project is no file of the repository or `dotnet` is not on `PATH`. The step installs no SDK. A `global.json` at or above the project still decides which installed SDK `dotnet` runs, so it must allow the configured version.

An item with `console-runner: true` names a console test program: a project without a test framework, with `OutputType` `Exe`, that exits with a non-zero code when a check fails. `dotnet test` runs no test in such a project, so the job passes the item's `console-runner` to the step as `true` or `false`, and the step starts the script with `--console-runner` for `true`; it fails on any other value. The script runs `dotnet build <project>` and, after a build that passed, `dotnet run --project <project> --no-build` in place of `dotnet test`, from the repository root, without the logo and without telemetry. The program's exit code is the result: the script asks for no TRX report and makes no check that a test ran, so a program that runs no check passes. The exit code is 0 when the build passed and the program ended with 0, 1 when the build failed or the program ended with another code, and 2 as for every item; the error annotation names the failed build or the failed program with its exit code. The item builds and runs in the default configuration, as every item does, and its `project` is a project, since `dotnet run` takes no solution. Without `console-runner`, or with `console-runner: false`, an item builds and tests as described above. Items of both kinds stand in one `projects` list, each in its own job.

#### The Node test part

`node-test` installs, builds and tests Node projects with npm. It takes `projects` and `paths` as the Python parts do, and its job is the same matrix with the same rights and limits: one job per project, named after its working directory, `node-test (web)`. Each project states `working-directory`, the project's directory relative to the repository root, and may state `runner-label`, with the values of the Python parts' `runner-label`; the job runs on `ubuntu-26.04` without it. The job reaches the shared step [node-test/action.yml](../actions/node-test/action.yml) twice, each time running [node_test.py](../actions/node-test/node_test.py) with the `working-directory`:

```text
python3 node_test.py check --working-directory <directory> [--root <checkout>]
python3 node_test.py test --working-directory <directory> [--root <checkout>]
```

`check` runs before Node is installed and runs no command. It fails with a message that names the cause when the directory is no directory of the repository, holds no `package.json` or no `.nvmrc`, when `package.json` cannot be read, when its `packageManager` names another package manager than npm, a scoped package such as `@yarnpkg/cli` included, or no exact version `npm@<major>.<minor>.<patch>`, or when it defines no non-empty script `test`; a Corepack hash after the version, such as `+sha512.<hex>`, is accepted. `actions/setup-node` then installs the Node version that `.nvmrc` names, with `package-manager-cache: false`, and the job restores an npm cache keyed by `.nvmrc` and `package-lock.json`. `test` makes the same checks and then, from the project's directory: it prints `node --version` and fails when the first line of `.nvmrc` is an exact version, `<major>.<minor>.<patch>` with or without a leading `v`, and Node reports another; a version that `actions/setup-node` resolves, such as `24` or `lts/*`, is not compared. For `npm@<version>` it checks `npm --version` first and runs `npm install --global npm@<version>` only when the installed version is missing or differs, failing unless `npm --version` then reports that version; it does not verify a Corepack hash and says so in the log; without `packageManager` it keeps the npm of the installed Node. It then runs `npm ci`, `npm run build` when `package.json` defines a non-empty script `build`, and `npm test`, and stops at the first command that fails; a missing or outdated `package-lock.json` fails `npm ci`. The exit code is 0 when the checks and every command passed, 1 when a command failed or Node or npm reports another version, and 2 when a check failed or `node` or `npm` is not on `PATH`. The part reads no other Node version file and runs no other package manager.

#### The automation workflow

`automation.yml` is the automation workflow: the shared workflow that the fixed caller `automation.yml` calls on a completed run of `CI`, on its weekly schedule, on a start by hand and on an opened issue. It declares the one secret that the caller passes by name, `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`, as optional, and takes no input: a start by hand reaches it as `github.event.inputs` of the caller, with `part`, `run-id` and `verify-token`. Its job `full-recording-jobs` calls `suite-jobs.yml` with `full: true`, as [Full recording](#full-recording) says.

It runs the parts whose schema fragment holds `"workflow": "automation.yml"`. A repository states such a part under `parts` like any other. The pipeline's plan and verdict leave these parts out, apart from the list of the parts switched off compared with the base, and `plan.py plan --shared-workflow automation.yml` decides on them alone.

| Part | Values | Jobs | Runs for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `additions` | `suites` | `additions`, `additions-comment` | a failed `pull_request` run from a branch of the same repository; a start by hand with `part=additions` and `run-id` |
| `dependabot-auto-merge` | `suites`, optional | `dependabot-auto-merge` | a passed `pull_request` run whose actor is `dependabot[bot]`, or the updater App on a branch that starts with `dependabot/` |
| `full-recording` | `suites`, `validation-workflow` | `full-recording`, `full-recording-jobs`, `full-recording-close` | the schedule; a start by hand with `part=full-recording` |
| `issue-title` | `categories` | `issue-title` | an opened issue |

Every trigger of the fixed caller starts a run in every repository that holds it, so no job starts a runner without work:

- The job `plan` runs only on an event that some part runs for: its condition is the event conditions of the parts, joined with `||`. It runs on `ubuntu-slim`, checks out only the configuration file, and runs the plan without a comparison, since no part of the workflow names `paths`.
- The job of each part needs `plan` alone and holds the condition `(<its events>) && fromJSON(needs.plan.outputs.plan).parts['<part>'].run`, which reads only the event and the repository beside the plan. On any other event GitHub skips it without a runner, and with it every later job of the part, which needs it.
- The configuration file is that of the event's own commit: the default branch's for a completed run, the schedule and an issue, so a pull request cannot switch a part on for itself.

`tests/test_ci_policy_automation.py` fails while a part's job lacks that condition, reads anything else in it or needs another job than `plan`, while the plan's condition differs from the events of the parts, and while the fixed caller or this repository's caller withholds an event, a right, the secret or an input that a job needs. It evaluates the conditions on each kind of event a caller can start on: a passed run of a person's pull request starts no runner.

[Dependabot auto-merge](#dependabot-auto-merge), [Apply recorded additions](#apply-recorded-additions) and [Full recording](#full-recording) describe three of the parts.

Issue-title normalisation is the fourth. The job `issue-title` holds `contents: read` and `issues: write`, checks out nothing and runs [issue_title.py](../actions/issue-title/issue_title.py) with the event file, the run's commit ref and the part's `categories`, reading only the category file (and any component markers when `product_categories` is non-empty) from the GitHub API. The title and the body of the issue reach the program only through the event file. The category file is a JSON object with the component categories under `product_categories` and the categories without components under `generic_forms`; the program reads `key`, `title_prefix` and `labels` of each, and of a component category also `source_directory`, `marker`, `dynamic_title_prefix`, `component_label` and `required_metadata`, and ignores every other key. A component is a direct subfolder of the category's `source_directory` that holds its `marker`, a file that starts with `key: value` lines between two `---` lines.

The category of an opened issue is the one category whose label it carries, otherwise the component category whose `component_label` heads a filled section of the body, otherwise the one category that holds the component under the heading `Component`. The new title is the prefix of the category and then the title without its earlier prefix:

| Issue | Prefix |
| --- | --- |
| A category without components | its `title_prefix` |
| A component section that names a component of the category, in any letter case | `dynamic_title_prefix` with the component as its folder spells it |
| A component section with `Not sure`, another value or no value | `dynamic_title_prefix` with `unsure` |
| No component section, and the title starts with the prefix of a current component | that prefix stays |
| No component section otherwise | `dynamic_title_prefix` with `unsure` |
| No category, and the title starts with a known prefix | that prefix in its canonical spelling |
| No category otherwise | the title stays |

The program re-reads the issue before updating so it never overwrites a title changed since the event, retries transient API failures, changes the title with one `PATCH` of the issue and changes nothing else. It fails on a category file or a marker that it cannot use, and names the reason.

This repository calls the workflow from `automation-caller.yml`, which equals the fixed caller apart from the local path. Its configuration file switches the four parts on. `issue-title` reads the fixture category file `tests/fixtures/issue_title/categories.json`, whose one category has the label `fixture:issue-title` and the prefix `[fixture-form]`, so an issue of this repository keeps its title unless it carries that label or starts with that prefix. Dependabot auto-merge runs solely through `automation-caller.yml`.

### Sync

`sync.yml` is the sync: the shared workflow that the private management repository calls every twelve hours and on a start by hand. No consumer calls it and this repository runs no caller of it, because it needs the [sync App](#sync-app), which is never installed here; its program is tested on fixture repositories behind a stand-in for the GitHub API, and its first run in the management repository shows what only GitHub can show.

The workflow has one job, `sync`, which runs only on `main`, in the environment `sync` of the calling repository, with `issues: write`. It loads [sync.py](../actions/sync/sync.py) from its own revision, gets an installation token for every repository of the App's installation with `actions/create-github-app-token`, pinned to a full commit, from the environment's variable `SYNC_APP_CLIENT_ID` and secret `SYNC_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`, limited to the App's four permissions (Contents, Pull requests and Workflows write, Issues read), and runs `sync.py sync --own-repository <the calling repository>`. The App's token goes to that step alone. When a step fails, the last step runs `sync.py failure`. No file of the sync names a repository other than this one; `tests/test_ci_policy_sync.py` fails when one does, and on each rule of this paragraph.

#### The sync caller

[caller.yml](../actions/sync/caller.yml) is the caller that the management repository holds as `.github/workflows/sync.yml` on its `main`. The sync has no consumer kit: the caller lies in the part folder, outside one, and the management repository owns its other files, its instructions for agents among them. The caller starts on a schedule every twelve hours and on `workflow_dispatch`, sets `permissions: {}` at the top and holds one job, `sync`, that calls `CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/.github/workflows/sync.yml@v1` with `issues: write` and passes the secret `SYNC_APP_PRIVATE_KEY` under its own name. The job runs no step of its own. `issues: write` is for the token of the run, with which the sync opens issues in the management repository. `tests/test_ci_policy_sync.py` holds the caller against the shared workflow: it grants what the shared job holds and passes the secrets that the workflow declares.

The file in the management repository is never edited there. A promotion by hand that changes the template class compares it with the caller at the commit that `v1` names and replaces it when the two differ, as [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand) says, so a change made there by hand is undone by the next such promotion.

#### What the sync compares

`sync.py sync` asks GitHub for the repositories of the installation. An archived repository and the calling repository are left alone. The repository that the template of `ci.yml` calls, this one, is never synced: when the installation holds it, the run fails before any write. A repository without `.github/ci-config.yml` has not adopted the pipeline; the summary says so, still inspects direct `.yml` and `.yaml` files under `.github/workflows/` for calls to retired shared workflows (absent from `actions/repository-hygiene/shared-surface.json`), uncallable workflows and unreadable YAML, and makes no other check or write.

For every other repository the sync compares, on the default branch:

| What | With | A difference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The fixed callers `ci.yml`, `automation.yml` and `release.yml` | their templates in `actions/pipeline/consumer-kit/` | joins the sync pull request |
| The CI guide `docs/agents/ci.md`, up to and including the first heading `## Repository choices` | its template `docs/agents/ci.md` in the same kit, when the kit holds it | joins the sync pull request, with what the repository recorded below that heading kept |
| `.github/dependabot.yml` | the file that [the consumer checks](#the-consumer-checks) generate from `dependabot` in the configuration file; `.github/dependabot.yaml` leaves | joins the sync pull request |
| Direct `.yml` and `.yaml` files under `.github/workflows/` | the local workflow register, the fixed callers and the workflow callability rules of [the consumer checks](#the-consumer-checks) | is a workflow finding; the sync never removes or edits non-fixed workflow files |
| The default-branch ruleset | `actions/repository-ruleset/ruleset-main-template.json` with `ci / verdict` as its one required check | is a settings difference |
| The merge settings | `actions/sync/merge-settings.json`: the merge settings of the hygiene kit and `allow_auto_merge` | is a settings difference |

A configuration file that does not match its schema leaves the Dependabot configuration uncompared, and a Dependabot configuration in a repository whose configuration file states no ecosystems stays; the summary says either, and hygiene in that repository fails on it.

The ruleset is found by the template's name and target. It matches when it holds everything the template states: further fields that GitHub adds are no difference, and rules and checks may stand in any order. GitHub shows the bypass list only to a token that administers the repository, so the App's run does not compare it and the local mode does.

#### The sync pull request

For file differences the sync puts one commit with the expected files on the tip of the default branch, onto the branch that `sync-branch` in the kit's record `fixed-callers.json` names, opens one pull request with the title `chore: sync the fixed files with their templates`, requests auto-merge with a squash, and goes on to the next repository: it waits for nothing. A pull request that is already open is kept; its branch moves only when the files it should hold changed, and auto-merge is requested again when it is off. When GitHub refuses auto-merge, the summary says why. A repository that matches gets no request that writes and no line in the summary.

A repository that GitHub refuses fails the run after the other repositories were synced.

#### Settings and the local mode

With the App's token, which holds no Administration permission, a settings difference is reported and never changed. `sync.py sync --local --repository <owner>/<name>` is the local mode: it runs with the owner's own token in `GH_TOKEN` on the repositories it is given, does the same comparison and pull request, and also corrects each settings difference, the merge settings with one `PATCH` of the differing values and the ruleset by creating it or replacing it with the expected one. It asks for no installation and opens no issue.

#### Issues in the management repository

With `--own-repository`, the sync opens issues there with the token of the run, `GITHUB_TOKEN`; the App's token is never sent to that repository.

| Finding | Issue title | One issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A sync pull request is still open six hours after it was opened | `Sync pull request not merged after six hours: <repository>#<number>` | ever, per pull request |
| An open issue with the label `shared-ci` in a repository of the installation | `Request for shared CI: <repository>#<number>` | ever, per request |
| A repository's settings differ | `Settings differ: <repository>` | while an issue with the same differences is open |
| A repository holds unwanted workflows | `Unwanted workflows: <repository>` | while an issue with the same findings is open |
| The run failed | `The sync failed` | while one is open |

Each issue carries the marker `<!-- sync-finding: <key> -->` in its body, by which a later run finds it among all issues of the repository. The key of a settings difference or of unwanted workflows holds a digest of its findings, so changed findings open a new issue. A run without findings opens none.

The sync comments on and closes its own finding issues when it confirms the referenced request or pull request closed, the relevant check matches again, or a new issue supersedes the old one. It leaves a repository's issues open after a read or sync failure, and leaves settings issues open when it skips the settings check. It closes a repository's findings when GitHub confirms that the repository is archived or a complete installation listing confirms that it has left the installation. The final step of a successful run closes the sync's failure issue.

### Measurement

`measurement.yml` is the shared workflow of the CI goal measurement, called daily and on a start by hand by the private management repository through [caller.yml](../actions/measurement/caller.yml). No consumer calls it and this repository runs no caller of it; its program [measurement.py](../actions/measurement/measurement.py) is tested on fixture repositories behind a stand-in for the GitHub API.

The workflow has one job, `measurement`, which runs only on `main`, on `ubuntu-slim` with `timeout-minutes: 13`, in the environment `measurement` of the calling repository, with `actions: read` and `issues: write`. It gets an installation token of the [Measurement App](#measurement-app) (`MEASUREMENT_APP_CLIENT_ID` and `MEASUREMENT_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`) limited to `Actions: read` across the installation, and runs `measurement.py measure --own-repository <the calling repository> --store-out measurement-records.json`.

The measurement works incrementally from one JSON store uploaded as the artifact `measurement-records` (30-day artifact retention; records kept for 90 days; the goal and its issues use a rolling four-week window):

- **Reading the store.** At start, `measure` lists `measurement-records` artifacts on `main` of `--own-repository` with `GITHUB_TOKEN` (`actions: read`), sorts them newest first, and loads the newest readable artifact, falling back to older unexpired artifacts when the newest is corrupt or fails validation. Schema version upgrades migrate forward rather than failing as unreadable. An HTTP or network error while reading, any malformed unexpired artifact entry with no readable fallback, or a repository whose unexpired artifacts are all unreadable fails the run before measuring and writes no store file. When the listing succeeds with no unexpired artifact on `main`, `measure` starts an empty store and builds up from the window start.
- **Listing and fetching.** Per installed non-archived consumer repository (skipping `--own-repository`), `measure` lists pipeline runs (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`, `exclude_pull_requests=true`, excluding `workflow_dispatch` and runs created after `--now`) over the uncovered part of the window plus a 1-day overlap, splitting `created` ranges in halves whenever `total_count` reaches GitHub's 1000-result cap (failing loudly only when a 1-second range still hits the cap). Every newly listed run is upserted by `(repository, run_id)` as a `pending` stub. Detail fetching runs newest first with bounded parallel workers (6 by default), HTTP keep-alive reuse, global pacing (at most 10 requests per second), a global time budget (8 minutes), a request budget (4,000 requests), and a rate-limit reserve (`x-ratelimit-remaining` below 500). A budget stop ends fetching cleanly, writes the store and report, and exits 0. On a secondary rate limit (403/429), `measure` waits once for the response's full `retry-after` duration (or at least 60 s when `retry-after` is absent) if it fits within the run's remaining deadline, then checkpoints and exits 1; if the remaining deadline does not allow the wait, it checkpoints and exits 1 without sleeping. On a primary rate limit (`x-ratelimit-remaining: 0`), it checkpoints and exits 1.
- **Judging and freezing.** Every run is judged through `attempts/1/jobs` (fetching `attempts/1` only when `run_attempt > 1`), and its `ci-plan` artifact is read with `max_created_at` set to attempt 1's verdict completion time from a repository-scoped `actions/artifacts?name=ci-plan` index when cheaper than per-run calls. Each record carries an explicit state (`pending`, `unfinished`, `verdict`, `no-verdict`, `excluded-cancelled`, `deleted`) and a `judge_version`. A 404 on `GET runs/{id}` for an unfinished record marks it `deleted`, leaves it out of the goal figures, and counts it separately in the report. A record freezes only once its first attempt completed more than 30 minutes before `--now`; until then it stays `unfinished` and is re-checked on the next run. Records judged by an older `judge_version` are re-fetched while within the 30-day `ci-plan` retention; if a previously recorded `ci-plan` artifact has already expired, the old record and its `judge_version` are kept unchanged.
- **Checkpointing and upload.** `measure` writes the store atomically after listing, periodically during detail fetching (every 25 items or 30 s), after each repository finishes, and at the end of fetching; a repository that raises `MeasurementError` leaves its prior coverage unchanged while finished repositories are kept. The workflow uploads `measurement-records` in a step with `if: ${{ always() && hashFiles('measurement-records.json') != '' }}`.

From the stored records over the rolling four weeks (28 days), `measure` reports the store read and its age, coverage per repository (as its own line and column, never counted as a fallback), and per repository:

- the share of verdict runs within 3 minutes from trigger to verdict (target: 99%),
- the median duration of runs whose plan selected no tests (target: 1 minute): no run of `python-test`, `dotnet-test` or `node-test` and no running unit of the suite selection, so a run whose suite-selection job runs only the language step counts, and one whose plan holds no units while the suite selection runs does not,
- the p95 fixed cost with run count, over runs whose last Linux shard job with entry files decided the verdict, excluding the Python language step (whose own p95 duration is reported alongside `LANGUAGE_STEP_ALLOWANCE_SECONDS` over the same run set), split into the time outside that shard's entry-file step and the step's excess over its planned estimate, and by phase (plan, queue from plan end to shard start, shard start to entry step, after entry step, results, verdict); the planned estimate leaves out unmeasured entry files, so their time counts as step excess; runs decided by a part, hygiene or the Windows job and unmatched runs are left out and counted,
- fallbacks by reason,
- parts that ran with unknown inputs.

A repository whose listing does not cover the full window or that still has `pending`, unchecked `unfinished`, or stale-`judge_version` runs in the window is reported as partial coverage and opens no finding except `no-workflow`. For fully covered repositories, a missed target, a p95 fixed cost above `FIXED_COST_SECONDS`, a Python language-step p95 above `LANGUAGE_STEP_ALLOWANCE_SECONDS`, an installed repository without `ci.yml`, or a repository with completed runs none of which reached a verdict opens an issue in the management repository with the run's `GITHUB_TOKEN`, naming the repository and the finding. Issues are deduplicated by an HTML marker against both open issues and issues closed within the last 7 days, so a persisting miss opens at most one issue per week. A repository that raises an error gets a report row stating the reason. When the measurement job fails or is cancelled (`failure() || cancelled()`), `measurement.py failure` opens one failure issue while none is open.

The measurement comments on and closes its own finding issues when complete coverage confirms the workflow exists again, a run reached a verdict in the current window, or the affected target is met with current data. A skipped repository, a read error or partial coverage leaves its issues open. A complete installation listing also lets the measurement close findings for repositories that have left the installation. Automatic and manual closures both start the seven-day cooldown. The final step of a successful run closes the measurement's failure issue.

`measurement.py measure --local --repository <owner>/<name>` runs with the owner's own token on named repositories without opening issues and accepts `--store-in FILE` and `--store-out FILE`. `measurement.py report --store FILE --window-days <days>` computes the same report offline over any chosen window up to 90 days from a downloaded store artifact, reporting per-repository coverage and judge versions (including repositories removed from the installation whose records remain in the store).

### Validation

CI is the only gate. While building, run the narrow validation that the ticket or Work Brief needs: the entry files of the changed behaviour, each with its suite's `command` from `tests/suites.json`, as `tests/README.md` says, together with the tools some entry files need. No local copy of the CI's selection runs before a push; the required validation is the one that `docs/agents/delivery.md` states. The pull request's CI then runs repository hygiene and the selected suites under the recording, reported in `ci / verdict`. A recording check that fails reports additions, and they land in the same change: the additions workflow commits them onto the pull request, or the manual route applies them, as [Apply recorded additions](#apply-recorded-additions) says.

The tests fetch no specification and install no package; the updater and move-decision tests run offline against mocked responses and local fixture repositories. They prove policy and update behaviour; cross-repository event delivery, App authentication, repository settings and tag promotion show only in a caller's live runs.

#### Apply recorded additions

A recording check that fails lists the additions in its job summary, and every Linux shard and the Windows job whose check reported read additions uploads its own artifact, `recording-additions-shard-<number>` or `recording-additions-windows`; a job without read additions that recorded entry times or list replacements uploads `recording-entry-times-shard-<number>` or `recording-entry-times-windows` and passes. Each artifact holds one file named `additions.json`.

This repository applies them with the part `additions` of the [automation workflow](#the-automation-workflow), which `automation-caller.yml` calls. GitHub starts the caller on `workflow_run` when `CI` completes, from its file on the default branch. After a failed `pull_request` run from a branch of this repository that uploaded at least one unexpired `recording-additions-*` artifact, while an open pull request still has the run's head commit, the job `additions` downloads every `recording-additions-*` and `recording-entry-times-*` artifact of the run and applies every `additions.json` to `tests/suites.json` with `recording.py apply` of the recording step of the workflow's own revision, before any write token exists (writing raw entry times for exit-0 entry files through the 25% threshold, without speed correction). Runs that uploaded only `recording-entry-times-*` artifacts trigger no commit. It commits the changed suite list as the [GitHub App](#github-app) onto the pull request's branch, with the subject `chore: apply the recorded additions of run <run id>` and `[dependabot skip]` in the body, and pushes it with a lease on the run's head. A Dependabot pull request gets the same commit: the run that `workflow_run` starts after Dependabot's run reaches the App's key, and [Dependabot auto-merge](#dependabot-auto-merge) accepts the commit. The App's push starts `CI` on the new head, where `select` reuses the passed result of every unit that passed in the failed run, with or without additions, unless the new head changes something that the unit read under the applied lists, and runs the units whose stored lists name the suite list, as [Push lookup and reuse](#push-lookup-and-reuse) says.

The bound: a failed run whose head commit carries the subject `chore: apply the recorded additions of run <run id>` applies nothing. Each push by a person, an agent or Dependabot therefore gets at most one automatic round. At the bound, when applying leaves the suite list unchanged, or when applying or a step after it fails, the job `additions-comment` comments once on the pull request with the reason and the manual route below, which names the file of the caller that called the workflow. A head that moved since the run, or a closed pull request, stops both routes without writing, and ends the automatic route without a comment.

Two properties keep the write token away from a pull request's code, and `tests/test_ci_policy_automation.py` fails when either is removed. The job acts only for a branch of the same repository: its job-level condition requires the head repository of the run to be the repository itself, and the step that reads the run refuses any other, also for a start by hand. And it applies the additions before any write token exists: the workflow token of the job only reads, the App's token is minted after the apply step and after the check that only the suite list changed, and no step before it reads the secret.

The shared automation workflow contains no rebuild logic; a change to the job reaches the automatic route only after its merge, since GitHub starts the caller from its file on the default branch.

The manual route, after the job's comment or for a run whose additions it did not commit:

1. Start the caller on the default branch with the run id from the address of the failed run, `.../actions/runs/<run-id>`:

   ```text
   gh workflow run automation-caller.yml -f part=additions -f run-id=<run-id>
   ```

   It opens or updates a pull request onto the reporting branch, from `chore/apply-recorded-additions-<run-id>`, whose only change is the suite list. Merge it once its checks pass.
2. Without the workflow, download every recording artifact (`recording-additions-*` and `recording-entry-times-*`) of the run, each into its own directory under `<dir>`, and apply every `additions.json` in one command, from the repository root:

   ```text
   gh run download <run-id> -p 'recording-additions-*' -p 'recording-entry-times-*' -D <dir>
   python3 actions/recording/recording.py apply --suites tests/suites.json <dir>/*/additions.json
   ```

   With `-p`, `gh` extracts each artifact into a directory named after it, so the files named `additions.json` stay apart. Push the changed suite list in the same change; the next run checks the applied lists.

### The v1 channel

The shared surface is [shared-surface.json](../actions/repository-hygiene/shared-surface.json): the reusable workflows that callers use through `v1`, the part folders they load through `$/`, the pins file and the updater branch. The catalogue is on it, and every consumer kit lies in a part folder, so they are part of it, as is `actions/repository-ruleset`, the ruleset template that the consumer kit of the pipeline leads to. Callers receive a change under a path of the shared surface only when `v1` moves, and creating or moving `v1` changes the later runs of every caller; moving it back does not undo those runs. This repository's own callers run the changed workflows from the pull request on. A consumer reads the catalogue, the consumer kits and the ruleset template at `v1`, so a change to them reaches consumers only when `v1` moves.

`v1` moves only to a commit whose CI on `main` passed, by a manual promotion and by the automatic move alike. Because this repository calls `pipeline.yml`, `automation.yml`, `release.yml` and `suite-jobs.yml` by local path, that CI has run the pipeline workflows the commit carries. The promotion class of the changed paths decides how a commit reaches `v1` ([Promotion classes](#promotion-classes)): a commit whose changes compared with `v1` stay in the read class moves it automatically, after the commit's CI on `main` passed, as [Automatic v1 move](#automatic-v1-move) states; every other move is the question that [Who moves v1](#who-moves-v1) states. A move gets no separate check afterwards: the consumers' ruleset and caller runs after the move are the check ([Find the consumers](#find-the-consumers)). No move starts a run in a consumer: those runs start with each consumer's next pull request, push or Dependabot merge, and a consumer of the suite selection runs every suite until a run on its default branch has passed under the new digest ([A move of v1 in a consumer](#a-move-of-v1-in-a-consumer)). `v1` moves back, as [Roll v1 back](#roll-v1-back) says, when one of them fails on the moved workflows. A `promote-v1.yml` run writes the old and the new commit to its summary, `promote` prints them for a manual move, and a caller's run records the commit that `v1` resolved to; no other record of a move is kept.

The automatic move has one accepted limit. No run in another repository precedes it. A commit in the read class that passes this repository's CI and fails only in a consumer therefore moves `v1` first, and it shows as a red `ci / verdict` in that consumer. A read-class job holds only read permissions, so the effect is a blocked merge in that consumer until the revert of [Roll v1 back](#roll-v1-back) lands. No test covers these four kinds of failure:

- GitHub's resolution of a reference from another repository;
- an expression that GitHub evaluates with a consumer's values;
- a step's shell that the pipeline suite does not run in Bash;
- a pinned action that behaves differently in a repository of another shape.

A move that changes check names ships in one coordinated move. When `v1` moves, no consumer that [Find the consumers](#find-the-consumers) finds has an open pull request. Right after the move, each consumer updates its default-branch ruleset to the new check names and then its callers, before any other work. [Change a check name](#change-a-check-name) gives the procedure.

#### Who moves v1

Creating or moving the shared `v1` tag is a question to the user, including the first creation, a rollback and a move that changes a check name: one promotion changes every caller's subsequent runs, and moving the tag back does not undo those runs' effects. The one exception is automatic: `.github/workflows/promote-v1.yml` moves `v1` to a commit on `main` whose changes compared with the current `v1` stay in the read class, once the commit's CI on `main` passed, as [Automatic v1 move](#automatic-v1-move) states. The merge of such a commit into `main` is the approval for that move. One commit outside the read class moves the same way: the pins update, a pins-only pull request that the updater App opened from `chore/update-ci-pins` and the repository owner merged, whose merge is its review and its approval. Every other creation or move of `v1` still needs the question. A commit that changes the write class or the template class never moves `v1` automatically, whoever made it, so a Dependabot pull request that merged itself moves `v1` only when its changes stay in the read class.

`promote-v1.yml` and `promote_v1.py` reach one other repository and no further one: the management repository. Only the job `move` names the environment `promote-v1` and reads a deploy key. After a move by hand that changes the template class, `promote` reads the management repository's sync caller, replaces it when it differs from the caller at `v1` and starts the sync there, and `caller` repeats that; only the local Git configuration of the checkout names that repository. The name of the management repository reaches no run summary, log or terminal output of the workflow or of these subcommands, which print nothing that `gh` returns, and nothing here asks GitHub about a consumer, sends one an event or holds a token for one. `tests/test_hygiene_v1_promotion_manual.py` and `tests/test_ci_policy_hygiene_promotion.py` fail when a reach to another repository is added.

#### Promotion classes

[shared-surface.json](../actions/repository-hygiene/shared-surface.json) gives every path of the shared surface one promotion class under `classes`. A file takes the class of the longest recorded path that holds it, so a part folder states its class once and a file or folder inside it with another class states its own.

| Class | Paths | Promotion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| read | the shared workflows whose jobs only read and that no workflow of another class calls, `pipeline.yml` among them, the part folders that only they load (`publication-checks`, `python-tests`, `dotnet-test`, `node-test`) and the catalogue | automatic, after the CI on `main` |
| write | the shared workflows that grant a write permission to a job or to an App token (`automation.yml`, `release.yml`, `measurement.yml`), `suite-jobs.yml`, which `automation.yml` calls, and every part folder that one of them loads: `pipeline`, `recording`, `repository-hygiene`, `suite-selection`, `python-language-step`, `dependabot-auto-merge`, `issue-title` and `measurement` | by hand, after the question |
| template | the consumer kits with the fixed callers, `consumer_checks.py`, which holds the generated Dependabot configuration and the caller check, the ruleset template, `sync.yml` and the sync's part folder, which holds the sync caller | by hand, after the question; the management repository's caller is brought in line and the sync starts right after the move |

A part folder or a shared workflow takes the highest class among the shared workflows that load or call it, whatever else does: `actions/pipeline` is in the write class because `automation.yml` and `release.yml` load it, although the pipeline loads it too. A change inside such a folder reaches a workflow that writes, so it stays a question, a Dependabot update included. The folders in the write class hold the configuration schema and `promote_v1.py` with `shared-surface.json`, which decide the promotion, so a change to the classes is a question too. They also hold the pins file, which has the one exception: the reviewed pins update of [Automatic v1 move](#automatic-v1-move). A Dependabot update in the write class has no exception.

`tests/test_ci_policy_shared_surface.py` fails on a path of the shared surface without a class, on a path with two, on a class other than the three, on a shared workflow that grants a write permission in the read class, and on a path in the read class at or below a part folder or a shared workflow that a shared workflow outside the read class loads or calls. A new path of the shared surface gets its class in the change that adds it. A part that needs a new trigger, permission or secret changes a fixed caller, which is a template-class change.

#### The ruleset on v1

Two actors move `v1`, and no other actor can update or delete the tag:

| Move | Actor | On the bypass list as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The automatic move of `promote-v1.yml` | the deploy key of the environment `promote-v1` | `DeployKey` |
| A move by hand with `promote` | the repository owner's own login | `RepositoryRole` 5, the admin role |

The ruleset "v1 tag" of this repository targets `refs/tags/v1`, restricts updating and deleting it, and holds only those two actors on its bypass list:

```json
{
  "name": "v1 tag",
  "target": "tag",
  "enforcement": "active",
  "conditions": { "ref_name": { "include": ["refs/tags/v1"], "exclude": [] } },
  "rules": [{ "type": "update" }, { "type": "deletion" }],
  "bypass_actors": [
    { "actor_id": 5, "actor_type": "RepositoryRole", "bypass_mode": "always" },
    { "actor_id": null, "actor_type": "DeployKey", "bypass_mode": "always" }
  ]
}
```

Read the active ruleset back and compare it with the block above:

```text
gh api repos/CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/rulesets --jq '.[] | select(.target == "tag") | .id'
gh api repos/CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/rulesets/<id> --jq '{target, enforcement, conditions, rules, bypass_actors}'
```

The token of a run cannot be on a bypass list: GitHub refuses the GitHub Actions integration as a bypass actor with HTTP 422, "Actor GitHub Actions integration must be part of the ruleset source or owner organization" (tried on this repository on 2026-11-15). The `move` job of `promote-v1.yml` therefore holds no write token and pushes the tag over SSH with a write deploy key. Its private key is the secret `PROMOTE_V1_DEPLOY_KEY` of the environment `promote-v1`, whose deployment branches are limited to `main`, so only a job that runs from `main` and names that environment reads the key, and only the `move` job names it. The job checks GitHub's host against the Ed25519 host key that `https://api.github.com/meta` lists under `ssh_keys` and offers no other identity. That host key, the key file and the SSH options of every push of the workflow stand once, in `promote_v1.py`: a step names the variable that holds its key with `--deploy-key`, and the program writes the key to a file that only the job's user reads, for the length of the subcommand.

The `DeployKey` actor covers every deploy key of a repository, so that key is the only deploy key of this repository. The updater App is not on the list: neither its token nor a copy of its private key in a consumer moves `v1`. The ruleset "main checks" gives deploy keys no bypass, so the key cannot push to `main` past the required checks.

The user creates the key, the environment and the ruleset; adding a deploy key, replacing this one or changing the environment or the ruleset is a question to the user (`docs/agents/publication.md`). To replace the key, generate an Ed25519 key pair without a passphrase, add its public key as a deploy key with write access, set the environment secret to its private key, and delete the old deploy key.

#### Automatic v1 move

`promote-v1.yml` runs on every push to `main`, one run at a time. [promote_v1.py](../actions/repository-hygiene/promote_v1.py) carries each of its three jobs:

1. `decide` reads the current `v1` and compares the shared surface at `v1` with the pushed commit. The job reports that `v1` follows the commit only when the current `v1` is an ancestor of the commit and every changed file of the shared surface is in the read class; who made such a commit does not count. It also reports it for the pins update, when `pins.json` is the only changed file outside the read class and all of these hold:
   - the commit is the merge commit of a merged pull request that the updater App opened from `chore/update-ci-pins`;
   - the repository owner's account merged it;
   - the commit changes only `pins.json`.

   No other file of the write class and no other pull request takes this exception, so a Dependabot pull request that changes the write class stays a question. For a pins-only commit the job mints a metadata-only App token to learn the App's login.
2. `await` runs only when `decide` reports that `v1` follows the commit. It waits for the completed `ci / verdict` check run of GitHub Actions on the target commit, and exits non-zero when no completed, passed verdict arrives. `await` reads no run of another repository.
3. `move` runs only when `await` reports the move, rechecks the `ci / verdict` check run at the moment it writes the tag, and moves `v1` to the commit.

The `await` job polls the runs every 30 seconds through the GitHub API with the job's token, which holds `checks: read`. Each request times out after 20 seconds, and a poll starts only while it ends within 10 minutes of the start of the wait, even when every request takes its full timeout. A read that fails counts as a run that has not completed, and polling goes on. The job has `timeout-minutes: 14`, under the 15-minute limit of `ubuntu-slim` ([GitHub changelog, 2026-01-22](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-01-22-1-vcpu-linux-runner-now-generally-available-in-github-actions/)), so the wait ends and the summary is written before the runner stops the job. Any conclusion other than `success`, a run that has not completed when the wait ends, or a read that still fails on the last poll fails `await` with a non-zero exit and keeps `v1`.

The run summary states the outcome of `decide`:

- The paths of the shared surface hold identical content at the current `v1` and at the commit: `v1` stays and no promotion is needed.
- A changed file is in the write class or the template class, and the commit is not the pins update: `v1` stays, and the summary states the question for a promotion by hand, with the changed files and their classes, and the `prepare` command that asks it with the facts of the move ([Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand)). A later commit in the read class does not move `v1` either until that promotion, since the comparison is with `v1`.
- No `v1` exists, the current `v1` is no ancestor of the commit, or a changed file has no promotion class: `v1` stays, and the summary gives the reason and says that a manual promotion is needed.
- Otherwise `await` waits for the commit's CI on `main`, and `move` moves `v1` when `await` reports the move. When the CI kept `v1`, the summary of `await` gives the reason and says that a manual promotion is needed.

The move pushes with `--force-with-lease=refs/tags/v1:<previous tag object>`, so a tag that moved after the decision rejects the push and keeps its newer target; the summary records the old and new SHA. No job holds a write token: `move` pushes over SSH with the deploy key of the environment `promote-v1` ([The ruleset on v1](#the-ruleset-on-v1)); `decide` reads the current tag through the GitHub API with the job's token, because its checkout holds no credentials.

#### Promote v1 by hand

Every other creation or move of `v1` is a question to the user (`docs/agents/publication.md`): a promotion that changes the write class or the template class, a rollback, the first creation, a move that changes a check name, and a commit in the read class that the automatic move left behind. Where a procedure of this document says that a change reaches callers through this section, a change that stays in the read class reaches them through [Automatic v1 move](#automatic-v1-move) instead.

A move that changes the template class reaches the management repository right after the push, in two steps through the `gh` CLI. First `promote` brings the management repository's caller in line with `v1`: it reads the blob of `.github/workflows/sync.yml` on that repository's `main`, compares it with the blob of [actions/sync/caller.yml](../actions/sync/caller.yml) in the Git object of the commit that `v1` now names, never with the working tree, and replaces the file with one commit only when the two blobs differ. It replaces a caller and never creates one: a named repository without that file counts as a failed read. The comparison is with the installed file and not with the earlier `v1`, so it also repairs a caller that an earlier update left behind or that was changed there by hand, and a rollback brings back the caller of the earlier commit. Then `promote` starts `sync.yml` in the management repository with `workflow_dispatch` on its `main`, so that the sync opens its pull requests in the consumers at once. The management repository is private and no tracked file names it. The checkout that runs `promote` names it once in its local Git configuration:

```text
git config --local sharedworkflows.managementRepository <owner>/<name>
```

Without that setting `prepare` refuses a move that changes the caller, because the management repository would keep the old one. A move that leaves the caller as it is goes on: `promote` then starts no sync and says so, and the scheduled sync brings the consumers up to date within twelve hours.

`promote` and `caller` make every request about the management repository with the login of the `gh` CLI. The write of a file under `.github/workflows/` needs a token with the `workflow` scope beside `repo`; the start of the sync alone needs only `repo`. `gh` prefers a token in the environment, `GH_TOKEN` and then `GITHUB_TOKEN`, over its stored login, so a session that sets one writes with that token: unset both, or make sure the token is the owner's and holds both scopes. `gh auth status` shows the account and the scopes in use.

Three subcommands of [promote_v1.py](../actions/repository-hygiene/promote_v1.py), run from the repository root, carry the move: `prepare` gathers the facts and prints the question, `promote` moves the tag after the answer, and `caller` repeats the caller update and the start of the sync without moving the tag. They read the CI through the authentication of the `gh` CLI and scan with the locally installed Gitleaks.

1. Run `prepare`. The agent whose delivery merged a change to `main` that does not promote automatically, one in the write class or the template class, runs it once the commit's CI on `main` has completed and before it ends the delivery, as `docs/agents/delivery.md` states, so that no such change stays on `main` unpromoted without the question. The target is the head of `origin/main` after its fetch; `--commit <sha>` names another commit on `origin/main`. When `promote-v1.yml` reported that `v1` stays, its run summary names the reason.

   ```text
   python3 actions/repository-hygiene/promote_v1.py prepare [--commit <sha>]
   ```

   It reports the current `v1` commit and the object that `git ls-remote origin refs/tags/v1` shows, never a local `v1` tag; the target; the kind of move, a promotion, a rollback or the first creation; the changed files under the `paths` of [shared-surface.json](../actions/repository-hygiene/shared-surface.json), each with its promotion class; the shared digests the move changes; the commits between the two; the conclusion of `ci / verdict` for the target, read once; the result of the push gate's Gitleaks scan for the tag push (`docs/agents/delivery.md`); and, for a move that changes the template class, whether the sync caller changes between the two commits and what follows the move in the management repository.
2. `prepare` refuses, names the reason, asks no question and exits with `1` when the target is not on `origin/main`, is neither a descendant nor an ancestor of the current `v1`, has a `ci / verdict` check run that cannot be read, is still running, concluded other than `success` or does not exist, when the Gitleaks scan reports a finding or cannot run because Gitleaks is not installed, when the shared surface is identical at `v1` and the target, so that no move is needed, or when the move changes the sync caller and the checkout names no management repository. CI that is still running is not a failure: run `prepare` again once it has completed.
3. Otherwise `prepare` prints the question, which names the current `v1` commit, the target, the kind of move, the changed shared-surface files with their promotion classes and the shared digests the move changes, says that the move changes the later runs of every caller and, for a move that changes the template class, that right after it the move replaces the management repository's caller when it differs from the caller at the target and the sync starts, and below it the `promote` command. Ask the user the question as printed. Only a yes to that question approves the move.
4. After a yes, run the printed command. `--old` is the object the remote ref held, empty (`--old=`) for the first creation:

   ```text
   python3 actions/repository-hygiene/promote_v1.py promote --old <object> --new <commit>
   ```

   `promote` runs the push gate's Gitleaks scan again, rechecks `ci / verdict` for the target commit, and pushes nothing on a finding or without a completed, passed verdict. It pushes with `--force-with-lease=refs/tags/v1:<object>`; a rejected push means that `v1` moved after `prepare` and keeps its newer target, so start again at step 1. On success it prints the old and the new commit; a later rollback names the old one.

   After a move that changes the template class, `promote` prints whether the management repository's caller was equal or was replaced and that the sync started, without the management repository's name and without anything that `gh` returned. When the read of the caller, its write or the start of the sync fails, `v1` has moved and `promote` exits with `1`. A failed read or write starts no sync, since the sync would run the old caller against the new shared workflow. GitHub can refuse a start right after a write that changes the caller's triggers, before it has registered the file. The message names the command that repeats the caller update and the start against the remote `v1`, as often as needed and without moving the tag:

   ```text
   python3 actions/repository-hygiene/promote_v1.py caller
   ```

   When the commit that `v1` names holds no file at `actions/sync/caller.yml`, as after a rollback to a commit from before the caller lay there, `promote` and `caller` write nothing to the management repository, delete nothing there, start no sync and exit with `1`; the management repository keeps its caller, and whether it fits that commit is checked by hand. Beyond the read, the write and the start `promote` and `caller` ask GitHub about no other repository and send none an event.
5. Watch the consumers' first runs after the move. Later workflow resolutions in all callers use the new version without repository edits; jobs already running keep the commit they resolved. [Find the consumers](#find-the-consumers) lists the consumers. In each, watch the first run of every caller after the move: the runs of the required check `ci / verdict` and the runs of its other callers. The move starts no run: each starts with the consumer's next pull request, push or Dependabot merge, and with the suite selection the pull-request runs run every suite until a run on the default branch has passed under the new digest ([A move of v1 in a consumer](#a-move-of-v1-in-a-consumer)); `gh run list --repo <consumer>` lists them, and a run records the commit that `v1` resolved to. A failure that the consumer can fix in its own repository, as the section "When `v1` moves" of its consumer kit says, is fixed there. When a run fails on the moved workflows, roll `v1` back as [Roll v1 back](#roll-v1-back) says.

A move that changes check names follows [Change a check name](#change-a-check-name).

#### Roll v1 back

A rollback is another one-way move of `v1`, to a previously validated commit. It does not undo the runs that used the newer commit.

1. Select the earlier commit: the old commit that `promote` printed, the old commit in the summary of a `promote-v1.yml` run, or the commit that `v1` resolved to in a caller's run before the move, in one of the consumers ([Find the consumers](#find-the-consumers)).
2. Run `prepare` with the earlier commit, as step 1 of [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand) says; it names the move a rollback:

   ```text
   python3 actions/repository-hygiene/promote_v1.py prepare --commit <earlier-commit>
   ```

3. Ask the user the printed question again. The answer to an earlier promotion does not cover the rollback.
4. After a yes, run the printed `promote` command, as step 4 of [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand) says. A rollback that changes the template class brings the management repository's caller to the caller of the earlier commit. When the rollback changes the shared digest, the consumers of the suite selection run every suite again until a run on their default branch has passed under the digest of the earlier commit, as after a promotion.
5. Undo a faulty change in the read class with a revert on `main`. The revert of a read-class change is itself a read-class change, so `promote-v1.yml` moves `v1` to it once the CI on `main` passed, and no later push moves `v1` back onto the fault. Until the revert lands, a move back by hand keeps `v1` off the fault. Run `prepare --commit <earlier-commit>` and the printed `promote` command, as steps 2 to 4 say. The revert lands before any other read-class change merges, since `promote-v1.yml` would otherwise move `v1` to a commit that still holds the fault. While the shared surface at `v1` differs from `main` in a file of the write class or the template class, `promote-v1.yml` leaves `v1` in place; the next move is a manual promotion.
6. Watch the consumers' first runs after the rollback, as step 5 of [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand) says. When a run fails on the workflows that `v1` names after the rollback, roll `v1` back again, to another validated commit, from step 1 of this procedure.

A rollback across a check-name change brings back the old check names, so it is a coordinated move as [Change a check name](#change-a-check-name) states, in reverse. Before the rollback, no consumer has an open pull request, as [Find the consumers](#find-the-consumers) shows. Right after it, each consumer reverses its ruleset change and then its caller change, each a question to the user. This repository's callers run by local path from `main`, so its ruleset "main checks" goes back together with a pull request on `main` that reverts the check-name change: the ruleset changes right before that pull request merges, in the same window.

### Find the consumers

A consumer calls the shared workflows at `CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/.github/workflows/<workflow>.yml@v1` and the shared steps at `CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/actions/<folder>@v1` from its workflows; both references start with `CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/`. No list of consumers is kept, in this repository or elsewhere: it would go stale, the reason [ADR 0001](adr/0001-consumers-reach-the-shared-parts-through-a-catalogue.md) gives against a copy of the catalogue in every consumer. Find them before and after every move of `v1`, with the authentication of the `gh` CLI; this repository calls its shared workflows and shared steps by local path, so neither route lists it.

1. Search the owner's code. Each line names a consumer and one of its workflow files that holds such a reference:

   ```text
   gh search code "CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/" --owner CrankyAnt --limit 1000 --json repository,path --jq '.[] | select(.path | startswith(".github/workflows/")) | .repository.nameWithOwner + " " + .path'
   ```

   The search also matches documentation, so `--limit` stays above the number of all matches and the filter keeps the workflow files. Code search covers only the code that GitHub has indexed, so it can miss a consumer or a workflow changed since the last indexing.

2. The complete route is the sweep. It lists every repository of the owner that is not archived and reads each file under `.github/workflows/` on its default branch, in a POSIX shell:

   ```bash
   for repo in $(gh repo list CrankyAnt --no-archived --limit 1000 --json nameWithOwner --jq '.[].nameWithOwner'); do
     gh api "repos/$repo/contents/.github/workflows" --jq '.[].path' 2>/dev/null | while read -r path; do
       gh api "repos/$repo/contents/$path" -H 'Accept: application/vnd.github.raw' | grep -F 'CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/' >/dev/null && echo "$repo $path"
     done
   done
   ```

   A repository without `.github/workflows/` prints nothing, and an archived repository runs no workflow, so the sweep leaves it out. A move uses the sweep's result; the search is the quick check in between. Both run by hand in the maintainer's terminal: no workflow and no script of this repository runs them, so no run summary or log of this repository holds their output.

3. List each consumer's open pull requests with `gh pr list --repo <consumer> --state open`.

The part's tests read only its own folder and its own workflows; a check that spans the parts goes in the matching `tests/test_ci_policy_*.py` entry file of `ci-policy`.

9. Callers call the workflow at `v1`, so a caller works only once the workflow exists there. The part reaches callers through [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand) after the merge.

### Add or remove a pinned tool

To add a tool:

1. Add an entry under `tools` in [pins.json](../actions/repository-hygiene/pins.json), keyed by the tool's executable name in lowercase: `install_tools.py` places the executable under that name, and `hygiene.py` looks it up by it. The fields are those of [Pins](#pins). Take each `sha256` from the upstream checksums file and check it against your own download of the asset; with `checksums: null`, compute it from the download. Then let `install_tools.py` download both assets and verify them against the pins:

   ```text
   python actions/repository-hygiene/install_tools.py --pins actions/repository-hygiene/pins.json --platform linux-x64 --tool <name> --destination <temporary directory>
   python actions/repository-hygiene/install_tools.py --pins actions/repository-hygiene/pins.json --platform windows-x64 --tool <name> --destination <temporary directory>
   ```

2. Run the Windows executable from the second destination with `--version` and check that it reports the pinned version.
3. Add the code that runs the tool, and the tool to each step that installs it. The steps of `pipeline.yml`'s `repository-hygiene` job that install the pinned tools name every tool they install, gated on the checks that need them. A tool that a suite needs is a suite setup of `tests/suites.json`, unless it is `node` or `gitleaks`, which are pinned tools of the configuration file as [Pinned tools](#pinned-tools) states; the schema's enum of `pinned-tools` and `PINNED_TOOLS` in `suite_selection.py` name those two. For a suite setup, add a setup under the top-level `setups` whose name is the tool's executable name, with `"script": "tests/setups/pinned-tool.sh"` for a pinned binary, since that script installs the tool of the setup's name, and name it in `setups` or `entry_setups` of the suites that need it. The suite jobs of `suite-jobs.yml` read the list, so no workflow file changes for it. A new hygiene check follows [Change a hygiene check](#change-a-hygiene-check).
4. Add the name to the tool lists of the tests: the expected set in `test_pins_file_validates_and_every_tool_has_a_linux_entry` in `tests/test_hygiene_repository_hygiene.py`.
5. Validate as [Validation](#validation) says. The changed pins file selects the entry files that read it; it is not a repository-wide `always` path.
6. `pins.json` is part of the shared surface, so the tool reaches callers through [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand) after the merge. From the merge on, `update-ci-pins.yml` proposes the tool's newer releases.

To remove a tool:

1. Remove every place that runs or installs the tool by name; `git grep -n <name>` finds them:

4. `pins.json` and the hygiene workflow are part of the shared surface, so callers stop running the tool after [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand).

A tool that pip installs, such as `reuse`, `ruff` or `mypy`, is an entry under `python` in `pins.json`, keyed by its normalized package name, with the fields that [Pins](#pins) describes:

### Act on an updater pull request or a failed updater run

Two updaters open review pull requests with the token of the [GitHub App](#github-app): the [pins updater](#the-pins-updater) from `chore/update-ci-pins`, which changes only `pins.json`, and the [specification updater](#the-specification-updater) from `chore/update-conventional-branch-spec`, with the title `chore: update Conventional Branch specification to <version>`, which changes only `spec.json`, `spec-metadata.json` and `tests/fixtures/publication_checks/specification-cases.json`.

A specification update pull request is reviewed before it merges:

1. Review the specification for semantic changes, not just its version or checksum: compare `spec.json` with the vendored version, and read the versioned document that `spec-metadata.json` names as `source`.
2. Read the diff of `tests/fixtures/publication_checks/specification-cases.json`. Each case records whether the official regex of the new version accepts a fixed branch name, so a changed `matches` value is a rule change upstream. The fixture shows the change; it approves nothing.
3. Compare the changes with the local subset in `check-branch.cjs`. A branch that the subset accepts but the new regex rejects fails every caller's check after the promotion. A change to the subset follows [Change a naming rule](#change-a-naming-rule) in a pull request of its own; a commit added to the review branch is lost when the next updater run pushes that branch again.
4. Merge the pull request when its CI is green and the subset still holds. A closed pull request leaves the vendored version in place, and the next weekly run proposes the version again. `spec.json` is part of the shared surface, so callers check against the new specification after [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand). A merged pins update moves `v1` by itself, as [Automatic v1 move](#automatic-v1-move) says.

A pins update pull request is reviewed before it merges too:

1. Read the column "sha256 source" of its body for each proposed tool. A tool whose pin names a `checksums` asset shows that upstream checksums file, against which the run checked every download; only a tool with `checksums: null`, such as ShellCheck and zizmor, shows a sha256 computed from the download. A tool under `python` takes the sha256 values that PyPI lists, and the Node.js pin is not downloaded by the updater: `actions/setup-node` verifies the download it installs.
2. Find the follow-ups that `pins.json` cannot carry, with the release notes of each proposed version and the pull request's CI: the actionlint ignore patterns `ACTIONLINT_IGNORE`, `ACTIONLINT_WORKFLOW_IGNORE` and `ACTIONLINT_LABEL_IGNORE` in `hygiene.py`, which match the text of actionlint's messages; the output of a tool that `hygiene.py` reads; and new findings of a tool in this repository's workflows. A follow-up goes in a pull request of its own, since a commit added to the review branch is lost when the next updater run pushes that branch again. A follow-up in the write class or the template class reaches callers only through [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand), and once it is on `main`, `promote-v1.yml` keeps `v1` at a pins merge until that promotion.
3. Merge the pull request from the repository owner's account when its CI is green. Only the owner's merge of the App's pull request from `chore/update-ci-pins` lets `promote-v1.yml` move `v1` for the pins file, which is in the write class, as [Automatic v1 move](#automatic-v1-move) says. Its run summary then says whether `v1` moved; after a move, watch the consumers' first runs as step 5 of [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand) says, and for a stay, follow that procedure. A failed run follows [Act on a failed `promote-v1.yml` run](#act-on-a-failed-promote-v1yml-run).

For a failed run, `gh run list --workflow <update-ci-pins.yml or update-publication-spec.yml>` lists the runs and `gh run view <run-id> --log-failed` prints the log of the failed step. The next step depends on the step that failed:

- `actions/create-github-app-token`, the first step of `propose`: check the repository variable `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_CLIENT_ID`, the repository secret `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`, and the App's installation with Contents and Pull requests read and write, as [GitHub App](#github-app) describes. Changing any of them is a question to the user (`docs/agents/publication.md`).
- The plan or apply step of the pins updater, `Check the upstream releases of every pin` or `Apply the verified pins plan`: a checksum mismatch is listed in the run summary under "Checksum failures, nothing proposed", with the tool, the version, the asset and both sha256 values. Nothing from that release is pinned by hand; report the mismatch to the user, and the next weekly run checks again. Exit code 2, with an `::error::` line that names the problem, means that `pins.json` on `main` does not satisfy `pins.schema.json`; correct it in a pull request, which reaches callers through [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand). An unreachable upstream does not fail the run: it is listed under "Unreachable upstreams, pins left unchanged". `Apply the verified pins plan` reads the `pins-plan` artifact written by `plan` without querying upstreams again and validates it against `pins.schema.json`.
- The plan or apply step of the specification updater, `Check the official Conventional Branch specification` or `Refresh the vendored specification`: `update_spec.py` exits with status `1` (and writes `failed=true` to `--output`) on a network outage, a malformed candidate, a regex that does not compile in Node.js, a difference from the versioned upstream document, or same-version content drift (`Specification refresh failed:` or `Specification refresh needs review:`), leaving all files unchanged and proposing nothing. For a site outage or a versioned-document mismatch, the next weekly run checks again; report a regex that does not compile or same-version content drift to the user. An identical or older upstream version exits `0` without writing. `plan` runs with `--no-apply --write-candidate` and uploads the `spec-candidate` artifact when changed; `propose` downloads it and applies it with `--candidate` without querying upstreams again.
- `Check the changed files`: `Unexpected changed file: <path>` means the refresh changed a file outside those it may change, a defect in the updater script or the workflow. Record it as an issue with the run link; the allowed list stays.
- `Install the pinned Gitleaks`: the download failed, or its sha256 differs from the pins file. Nothing was pushed. Run the updater again after a download failure; report a sha256 mismatch to the user.
- The scan before the push, `Scan the commit before pushing` of the pins updater or `Scan unpublished commits before pushing` of the specification updater, each of which scans only the commits that no remote branch holds: Gitleaks reported a finding. Nothing was pushed. The redacted log names the rule, the file and the line. A false positive follows [Change this repository's Gitleaks configuration or baseline](#change-this-repositorys-gitleaks-configuration-or-baseline); a real secret goes to the user.
- `Push the review branch`: the lease rejected the push because the review branch changed between the fetch and the push. Read the branch and its open pull request (`gh pr list --head <branch>`). Run the updater again when that change may be replaced; otherwise merge or close that pull request first. A push refused for permission points to the App's Contents permission.
- `Create or update the review pull request`: the branch was pushed, but the pull request was not created or edited. Check the App's Pull requests permission and run the updater again; it pushes the branch again under a new lease and creates or edits the pull request. A pins pull request opened by hand does not come from the App, so `promote-v1.yml` does not move `v1` after its merge.
- Any other step, such as `Configure the GitHub App identity` or the commit step: record it as an issue with the run link.

To run an updater again, `gh run rerun <run-id> --failed` repeats the failed jobs of that run, and `gh workflow run update-ci-pins.yml -f apply=true -f cooldown_days=7` or `gh workflow run update-publication-spec.yml -f apply=true` starts a new run that proposes; without `apply=true` a run only previews. The scheduled run on the next Monday also tries again.

### Act on a failed `promote-v1.yml` run

A failed run of [Automatic v1 move](#automatic-v1-move) leaves `v1` where it was, unless the tag moved after the decision. `gh run list --workflow promote-v1.yml` lists the runs and `gh run view <run-id> --log-failed` prints the log of the failed step. The next step depends on where the run failed:

- `Identify the updater App`, the App step of `decide`: the job could not mint its metadata-only App token for a pins-only commit. Check the repository variable `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_CLIENT_ID`, the repository secret `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_PRIVATE_KEY` and the App's installation on this repository, as [GitHub App](#github-app) describes; changing any of them is a question to the user (`docs/agents/publication.md`). Once they hold, `gh run rerun <run-id>` decides again for the same commit.
- `await` kept `v1`: the job passed and its summary gives the reason. For a run of CI on `main` that failed, read the run with `gh run view <run-id> --log-failed`; a fault is fixed by a commit on `main`, whose own push decides again. For a run of CI on `main` that had not completed when the wait ended, `gh run rerun <run-id>` decides again once it has completed.
- `await` ended by its timeout: the runner stopped the job at its `timeout-minutes`, before the outcome and its summary. The wait ends before that limit, so the checkout took the job's time. Run `gh run rerun <run-id>`.
- `Move v1 under a lease on its previous commit`, the push of `move`: after a failed push `move` reads `refs/tags/v1` on the remote again, and the summary names one of three outcomes above git's error:
  - "failed; `v1` still names `<old>`", or "failed; no `v1` exists" for a first creation: the push failed for a reason other than the lease and `v1` did not change. After a network or server error, `gh run rerun <run-id> --failed` repeats the move under the same lease. A refusal for permission, such as `Permission denied (publickey)`, a key without write access or a rule on tags, points to the deploy key, the environment `promote-v1` or the ruleset on the tag, as [The ruleset on v1](#the-ruleset-on-v1) describes; `Host key verification failed` means that GitHub's host key no longer equals the one pinned as `GITHUB_HOST_KEY` in `promote_v1.py`, which a change to that one line and to its copy in `tests/test_hygiene_v1_promotion.py` replaces. Changing a key, an environment, a ruleset or a setting is a question to the user, and the move runs again once it is resolved.
  - "was rejected; the tag no longer names `<old>` and keeps its newer target": the lease rejected the push because `v1` moved after the decision, and git's error says `stale info`. Repeat nothing; the newer target stays. When the commit still has to reach `v1`, follow [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand).
  - "failed and `refs/tags/v1` could not be read again, so the state of `v1` is unknown": run `git ls-remote origin refs/tags/v1`. When it shows `<old>`, or no tag for an empty `<old>`, act as for "failed; `v1` still names `<old>`"; when it shows another object, act as for "was rejected".
- Any other step: record it as an issue with the run link.

### Rotate the App's private key

A GitHub App holds up to 25 private keys at once, and its last key can be deleted only after a new one exists ([Managing private keys for GitHub Apps](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/creating-github-apps/authenticating-with-a-github-app/managing-private-keys-for-github-apps)), so the new key works everywhere before the old one goes. The key belongs to the App, not to a repository: deleting the old key stops every repository that still holds it. Each change to the App's settings and each secret change below is a question to the user (`docs/agents/publication.md`).

1. In the App's settings, under **Private keys**, generate a private key. GitHub downloads it as a PEM file; keep that file outside every repository and delete it after step 5.
2. Replace the repository secret `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_PRIVATE_KEY` of this repository with the new key:

   ```text
   gh secret set PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_PRIVATE_KEY --repo CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows < <key file>
   ```

3. Find the consumers that hold a copy: run the sweep of [Find the consumers](#find-the-consumers) and keep the lines whose path is `.github/workflows/automation.yml`, the fixed caller, or `.github/workflows/full-recording.yml` or `.github/workflows/apply-additions.yml`, the install targets of the earlier suite-selection consumer kit. `gh secret list --repo <consumer>` shows that each of them holds `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`. Replace the secret in each of them with the command of step 2 and `--repo <consumer>`.
4. Verify the new key with the token verification of the full recording, started by hand on the default branch as [Full recording](#full-recording) says, in this repository and in each consumer from step 3: `gh workflow run automation-caller.yml --ref <default branch> -f part=full-recording -f verify-token=true` here and `gh workflow run automation.yml --repo <consumer> --ref <default branch> -f part=full-recording -f verify-token=true` in a consumer. `full-recording-close` then mints the App's token whether or not the suite list changed, and its run summary reports the outcome under `Report the token verification`. A pull request that the run proposes is handled as [Act on the full recording's pull request or a failed `close`](#act-on-the-full-recordings-pull-request-or-a-failed-close) says. Only a run that reports a minted token verifies the key; a run whose token step did not run verifies nothing, so start another. A failed token step means that the repository's secret does not hold the new key: set it again and start a new run. A consumer that still runs a full recording of its own, installed from the earlier kit, starts that workflow with `-f verify_token=true` instead. A consumer with the additions workflow but without the full recording verifies on its next additions run that commits or opens a pull request.
5. Once every repository from steps 2 and 3 has a run whose token step passed with the new key, delete the old key in the App's settings, under **Private keys**. A compromised key is deleted right after step 3 instead; a repository whose secret is wrong then shows it on its next run that mints, as [Act on an updater pull request or a failed updater run](#act-on-an-updater-pull-request-or-a-failed-updater-run) and [Act on the full recording's pull request or a failed `close`](#act-on-the-full-recordings-pull-request-or-a-failed-close) describe.

## Reference

### Pins

[pins.json](../actions/repository-hygiene/pins.json) holds every exact pin the shared workflows use: per tool its upstream repository, version, the name of the upstream checksums asset (or `null`), and one entry per platform with the asset name, the executable's path inside the archive and the asset's sha256; plus the Node.js version. [pins.schema.json](../actions/repository-hygiene/pins.schema.json) documents the format and [pins.py](../actions/repository-hygiene/pins.py) enforces it. The fields of a tool entry:

- `repository`: the GitHub repository, `owner/name`. Its release tags must be `v{version}`; `pins.py` builds every download URL from that tag.
- `version`: the exact `major.minor.patch` release.
- `checksums`: the name of the upstream checksums asset with `{version}` in place of the version, such as `actionlint_{version}_checksums.txt`, or `null` when the release publishes none.
- `platforms`: a `linux-x64` entry for CI and a `windows-x64` entry for installs on Windows, each with `asset` (the release asset name with `{version}`), `binary` (the executable's path inside the archive) and `sha256` (the asset's sha256). The `windows-x64` asset is a `.zip` and its `binary` is `<name>.exe`.

`python` holds the tools that pip installs, keyed by their normalized package name: `reuse`, `ruff` and `mypy`. An entry holds `requirement` (the tool with its extras, such as `reuse[charset-normalizer]`), `version`, `build` (the packages that build the tool from its sdist) and `packages`. `packages` locks every package of the closure, the tool and its build packages included, with its version and the sha256 of each of its distributions that installs on `linux-x64` or `windows-x64` from CPython 3.12 on.

[install_tools.py](../actions/repository-hygiene/install_tools.py) downloads the assets of one platform for the tools named with `--tool`, verifies every sha256 before extracting anything and places the executables in one directory. Its `--platform` is optional: without it the platform is that of the machine, such as `linux-x64` or `windows-x64`. The pipeline's `repository-hygiene` job, the tool setups of `tests/suites.json` through `tests/setups/pinned-tool.sh`, the pinned Gitleaks of the suite jobs, the Python language step and the pre-push scan of the two updaters use it. `pipeline.yml` and `update-publication-spec.yml` take their Node.js version from the composite action's output; the `node` setup of `tests/suites.json` takes its version from the pins file as its `version-file`, outside the recording.

For a tool under `python`, `install_tools.py` creates a virtual environment of the interpreter that runs it in `<destination>/<name>-venv`. pip installs the build packages there first and then the other locked packages with `--require-hashes`, `--no-deps` and `--no-build-isolation`, and the installer copies the tool's console script into the destination. A file whose sha256 the lock lacks stops pip, and the installer removes the environment.

### The pins updater

`update-ci-pins.yml` runs weekly on Monday at 17:36 UTC and by hand through `workflow_dispatch`; manual runs preview unless `apply: true`, and take `cooldown_days` (default 7, with an explicit `0` preserved via `format('{0}', inputs.cooldown_days)`) as the minimum release age. The `plan` job runs all upstream queries once with `--write-plan` and uploads the verified `pins-plan` artifact when any pin changes; the `propose` job downloads that artifact and applies it with `--apply-plan` without querying upstreams again. [update_pins.py](../actions/repository-hygiene/update_pins.py) proposes, per tool, the newest stable GitHub release that is at least `cooldown_days` old and newer than the pin, and for Node.js the newest release of the pinned major line that is at least `cooldown_days` old; a younger release waits, also on manual runs, and a new Node.js major line is never proposed. A proposal refreshes every platform entry of that tool: each asset is downloaded, its sha256 is taken from the upstream checksums file and checked against the download, or computed from the download when the upstream publishes none (ShellCheck and zizmor), which the pull request body states per tool. A checksum mismatch fails the run, so that run proposes nothing, not even for the other tools; an unreachable upstream leaves that pin unchanged and is listed in the run summary.

For a tool under `python`, the updater proposes the newest stable PyPI release under the same rule. pip, in a temporary virtual environment of the updater's interpreter, resolves the tool's requirement at that version and the build requirements from the `pyproject.toml` of its sdist, with `--uploaded-prior-to` where that pip has it, and also evaluates PEP 508 environment markers across both `linux-x64` and `windows-x64` (CPython 3.12 to 3.14) so platform-specific dependencies such as Windows-only packages are resolved and locked alongside host dependencies. The updater locks each resolved package with the sha256 values that PyPI lists. A resolved package with a distribution younger than the cooldown leaves the pin unchanged and is listed in the summary.

One run proposes all its changes in one pull request from `chore/update-ci-pins`, updated in place with `--force-with-lease` while it is open, with the token of the [GitHub App](#github-app). The pull request changes only `pins.json`; any other changed file stops the run. The pinned Gitleaks scans the commit before the push.

### The specification updater

`update-publication-spec.yml` checks the official latest endpoint of the Conventional Branch specification weekly on Monday at 17:06 UTC. Manual dispatch previews by default; `apply: true` proposes a pull request. The `plan` job queries upstream once with `--no-apply --write-candidate` and uploads the verified `spec-candidate` artifact when a newer version is found; the `propose` job downloads it and applies it with `--candidate` without querying upstreams again. A site outage, a malformed or invalid candidate, or same-version content drift exits with status `1`, leaves all files unchanged and creates no pull request that week; an identical or older upstream version exits `0` without writing. Before the updater force-pushes an existing review branch, it reads that branch's exact remote tip and uses it in `--force-with-lease`, so a later scheduled run cannot replace a newer review branch without first reading it.

A newer version must match its versioned upstream document and compile in Node.js. The fixture of observed cases records observed official-regex outcomes for a fixed corpus, so the pull request exposes rule changes; it does not approve them or change the local subset. Same-version content drift is reported for review without replacement.

This repository's `.github/dependabot.yml` checks weekly for changes to pinned GitHub Actions, the npm lockfile of the publication checks, and the NuGet packages and the .NET SDK (`nuget` and `dotnet-sdk`) of `actions/recording/etw-consumer`. Commitlint dependency updates are reviewed in this repository; no consumer lockfile needs refreshing. Dependabot pull requests follow the rules under [Dependabot auto-merge](#dependabot-auto-merge); scheduled update pull requests never auto-merge, and no scheduled pull request moves `v1` except a merged pins-only update.

### GitHub App

The user creates one GitHub App and installs it on this repository. A consumer that switches the full recording or the additions workflow on gets an installation as the [repository settings](../actions/suite-selection/consumer-kit/README.md#repository-settings) of the suite-selection consumer kit state. Each installation is a question to the user. The App has repository **Contents: read and write** and **Pull requests: read and write**, and no other requested permissions. GitHub's mandatory metadata read access is implicit. No account permission or webhook subscription is needed.

The repository variable `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_CLIENT_ID` holds the App Client ID and the repository secret `PUBLICATION_CHECKS_APP_PRIVATE_KEY` its private key. The user supplies these credentials; the workflows create neither the App nor its repository credentials or settings, and changing any of them is a question to the user. Five workflows of this repository use the key. Three propose changes as pull requests: the [pins updater](#the-pins-updater) (`update-ci-pins.yml`), the [specification updater](#the-specification-updater) (`update-publication-spec.yml`) and the [full recording](#full-recording) (the job `full-recording-close` of `automation.yml`, which `automation-caller.yml` passes the key). Two commit to a pull request's branch: the [additions workflow](#apply-recorded-additions) (the job `additions` of `automation.yml`) commits the applied suite list, or proposes it as a pull request onto that branch when started by hand, and the [rebuild workflow](#the-rebuild-workflow) (`rebuild-etw-consumer.yml`) commits the rebuilt ETW consumer to a Dependabot pull request's branch. Each mints a token through SHA-pinned `actions/create-github-app-token`, scoped to the current repository and those two permissions, the rebuild workflow to `contents: write` alone, only when it has a change to propose, and the full recording also when a run by hand asks for its token verification; the installation token lets the pull request or the push trigger CI, and the normal workflow token stays read-only. The fifth, `promote-v1.yml`, mints only a metadata-only token, to learn the App's login, as [Automatic v1 move](#automatic-v1-move) says. Every consumer that switches the full recording or the additions workflow on, as the [suite-selection consumer kit](../actions/suite-selection/consumer-kit/README.md) says, or that still runs the workflows it installed from the earlier kit, holds its own copy of the Client ID and the private key, under the same variable and secret names, and commits or proposes its changed suite list with the App's token. The fixed caller passes the key to the automation workflow by name, and the workflow reads the Client ID from the caller's repository variables. [Rotate the App's private key](#rotate-the-apps-private-key) and [Replace the App](#replace-the-app) reach this repository and those consumers. See [GitHub App tokens in Actions](https://docs.github.com/en/apps/creating-github-apps/authenticating-with-a-github-app/making-authenticated-api-requests-with-a-github-app-in-a-github-actions-workflow).

### Sync App

The sync App is a GitHub App of its own, used for nothing but the [sync](#sync). The user creates it; its creation, each installation and each key are questions to the user.

| Permission | Level | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contents | write | push the sync's branch |
| Pull requests | write | open the sync pull request and request auto-merge |
| Workflows | write | change the fixed callers under `.github/workflows/` |
| Issues | read | find open issues with the label `shared-ci` |
| Metadata | read | read a repository, its merge settings and its rulesets |

The App holds no Administration permission, so its key cannot change a setting or a ruleset. It subscribes to no event and needs no webhook.

The App is installed on selected repositories only, the consumers that the sync keeps equal, chosen one by one; only the owner adds a repository to the installation. A consumer outside the installation receives no sync updates. Never install it on `CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows`: a key that reaches that repository could change what every consumer runs, and the sync fails before any write when it finds that repository in the installation. The App is not installed on the management repository either: the sync opens its issues there with the token of the run, and the App's token is never sent to that repository.

The management repository holds the App's Client ID as the variable `SYNC_APP_CLIENT_ID` and its private key as the secret `SYNC_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`, both in the environment `sync`, which allows `main` as its only branch; [Set up the sync environment](#set-up-the-sync-environment) creates it and [Rotate the sync App's private key](#rotate-the-sync-apps-private-key) replaces the key. The shared workflow reads both names, and `tests/test_ci_policy_sync.py` holds this section and that procedure against it.

The App has no part in keeping the management repository's own caller equal. That write uses the owner's login, as [Promote v1 by hand](#promote-v1-by-hand) says, so no App installation and no key reaches the management repository for it.

### Measurement App

The Measurement App is a GitHub App of its own, used for nothing but the [measurement](#measurement). The user creates it; its creation, each installation and each key are questions to the user.

| Permission | Level | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actions | read | read workflow runs, jobs and `ci-plan` artifacts |
| Metadata | read | list installed repositories |

The App holds no write permission and no other read permission. It subscribes to no event and needs no webhook.

The App is installed on selected repositories only, the consumers, chosen one by one; only the owner adds a repository to the installation. A consumer outside the installation is not measured, and an installed repository without `ci.yml` opens a finding. The App is not installed on the management repository: the measurement reads its own `measurement-records` artifact and opens its issues there with the run's `GITHUB_TOKEN` (`actions: read` and `issues: write`), and skips `--own-repository`.

The management repository holds the App's Client ID as the variable `MEASUREMENT_APP_CLIENT_ID` and its private key as the secret `MEASUREMENT_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`, both in the environment `measurement`, which allows `main` as its only branch; [Set up the measurement environment](#set-up-the-measurement-environment) creates it and [Rotate the Measurement App's private key](#rotate-the-measurement-apps-private-key) replaces the key. The shared workflow reads both names, and `tests/test_ci_policy_measurement.py` holds this section and those procedures against it.

### Runner choice

A job of this repository's CI that is off the critical path and needs only the system `python3`, `git`, `gh` and pinned binaries runs on `ubuntu-slim`, with the system `python3` and no `setup-python`: `dependabot-auto-merge` in `automation.yml`, `promote-v1.yml`, `update-ci-pins.yml` and `update-publication-spec.yml`, the jobs `plan` and `push` of `rebuild-etw-consumer.yml`, the verdict job `verdict` of `pipeline.yml`, and the closing job `full-recording-close` of `automation.yml`. Those jobs cost less there. The hygiene job runs on `ubuntu-slim` only when a measurement shows it finishing within one minute there, so that it never bills more minutes on a smaller runner; the measurement so far keeps it on `ubuntu-26.04`.

### zizmor policy

The shared policy, [zizmor-policy.yml](../actions/repository-hygiene/zizmor-policy.yml), states the pinning rule: the shared workflow references listed by `shared-surface.json` and the shared actions that the consumer kits call, such as `CrankyAnt/SharedWorkflows/actions/recording`, may use a moving ref; every other external reference, `actions/*` included, needs a commit pin, a full 40-character commit SHA. The version comment after a commit pin is a convention of the consumer kits, which the policy does not check. The `ci-policy` check rejects missing or extra exceptions for shared workflows and shared actions. It applies to a caller without a zizmor configuration of its own. This repository's own `.github/zizmor.yml` requires a commit pin for every external action reference; a `$/` reference resolves to the running commit, as [Same-repository references](#same-repository-references) says, and needs no pin.
